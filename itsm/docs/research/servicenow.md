# ServiceNow — complete feature catalog (ITSM, ITOM, ITAM, SPM, ESM, platform, AI)

> Research date: 2026-11-07. Scope: the ServiceNow AI Platform (formerly "Now Platform") and every product that matters for service management. Facts were checked against official ServiceNow documentation, store and family release notes, and ServiceNow Community articles where possible. Items marked "(unverified)" come from prior knowledge or secondary sources and could not be confirmed against a primary source. The report was written in two passes. The first pass covered platform through workforce. The completion pass (same date) added portal, esm, csm, analytics, ai, access, integration, admin, devops and field_service, the release-family notes and the closing sections. Some access-control and AI details in the completion pass were cross-checked against the sibling cross-vendor studies in this research set (access.md, ai.md), which cite official ServiceNow documentation. SPM, IRM and some HRSD/FSM details still rely partly on prior knowledge and are marked accordingly.

## Overview

**Positioning.** ServiceNow is the reference enterprise ITSM/ESM platform: one multi-instance SaaS platform, one data model (a "task" supertable plus the CMDB), and one codebase for every workflow product (IT, employee, customer, security, risk, finance, creator). Its core pitch for 2025-2026 is "the AI platform for business transformation": generative AI (Now Assist, renamed **ServiceNow Otto** in 2026), agentic AI (AI Agents, AI Agent Studio/Orchestrator, Action Fabric with MCP/A2A), AI governance (**AI Control Tower**), and data unification (**Workflow Data Fabric**, RaptorDB, Knowledge Graph, Context Engine preview) on top of the long-standing ITIL process suite.

**Product families relevant to service management.**
- IT: ITSM, ITOM (Discovery, Service Mapping, Event Management/AIOps, Health Log Analytics, Metric Intelligence, Cloud, Certificate Management), ITAM (HAM, SAM, SaaS License Management, Cloud Cost Management, EAM), SPM (demand, project, agile, APM, resource, financial planning, EA), DevOps Change Velocity, Digital Product Release.
- Employee: HR Service Delivery (HRSD), Workplace Service Delivery (WSD), Legal Service Delivery (LSD), Source-to-Pay / Procurement, Employee Center (Pro), EmployeeWorks (Moveworks-based, 2026).
- Customer: Service CRM (renamed from Customer Service Management, CSM, at Knowledge 2026), Sales CRM (renamed from Sales and Order Management), Field Service Management (FSM).
- Security and risk: Security Operations (SIR, Vulnerability Response, Configuration Compliance, Threat Intelligence), Integrated Risk Management / GRC (policy & compliance, risk, audit, BCM, operational resilience, third-party risk, privacy, ESG), AI Control Tower.
- Platform: App Engine, Workflow Studio (Flow Designer, playbooks, decision tables, IntegrationHub), UI Builder/Next Experience, ServiceNow Studio and IDE, SDK/Fluent, Build Agent, ATF, Platform Analytics, Process Mining, Workflow Data Fabric.

**Editions and tiers (ITSM).**
- *Legacy model (until 9 April 2026):* ITSM **Standard** (incident, problem, change, request/catalog, knowledge, CMDB, SLA, basic reporting), **Professional/Pro** (adds Performance Analytics, Predictive Intelligence, Virtual Agent, Agent Workspace features, Continual Improvement Management, Walk-up, Workforce Optimization, DevOps Change Velocity, Process Optimization), **Enterprise** (adds Process Mining, advanced automation/orchestration). Generative AI was sold as **Pro Plus** / **Enterprise Plus** (Now Assist for ITSM requires Pro Plus or Enterprise Plus). Sources disagree on exactly where some apps sat; treat the Standard/Pro/Enterprise split as approximate.
- *New model (since 9 April 2026; legacy SKUs end of sale 1 July 2026, converted at renewal):* **Foundation / Advanced / Prime**, with AI bundled in every tier and usage metered in "assists".
  - **ITSM Foundation:** incident (basic), service catalog & request, asset/cost, CMDB, Virtual Agent, Predictive Intelligence, knowledge, out-of-the-box Now Assist/Otto, Moveworks, Workflow Data Fabric, AI Control Tower, plus an assist allocation.
  - **ITSM Advanced:** Foundation plus problem, change, major incident, on-call, walk-up, Platform Analytics Advanced, Process Mining, AI Voice Agents, agentic workflows, higher assist allocation.
  - **ITSM Prime:** Advanced plus DevOps Change Velocity, Digital Product Release, AI Agents for ITSM, the **L1 Service Desk AI Specialist**, Moveworks for ITSM Prime, a digital end-user experience (DEX) agent, and the only tier where customers can **build net-new custom AI skills and agents** (NASK / AI Agent Studio). Highest assist allocation.
  - Combined SKUs: ESM Foundation (ITSM Foundation + a business suite), ServiceOps Advanced (ITSM Advanced + ITOM Advanced on a shared credit model), and an ITSM Prime add-on for CSM/Industry Prime customers.
  - ITOM has only **Advanced** (Discovery, ACC for visibility, Service Mapping, CMDB 360, Tag Governance, Cloud Account Management, API Insights) and **Prime** (adds Event Management, Metric Intelligence, Health Log Analytics, ACC monitoring, Service Observability, Synthetic Monitoring). It is licensed in subscription units (credit meter: 1 credit = 1 SU; 10 credits = 1 ITSM fulfiller).
  - CSM/Service CRM and SecOps also moved to Foundation/Advanced/Prime in April 2026.
- *Licensing personas:* fulfillers (licensed roles such as itil), approvers (approver_user), requesters (no role, free), plus AI usage in assists (for example, the 2026 "Incident Analyzer" costs 4 assists per run).

Store applications (Now Assist/Otto for ITSM, ITSM Advanced, SOW, Event Management, etc.) ship monthly, independently of families. Example: Now Assist for ITSM 18.1.0 (Nov 2026) moved default models to Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Sonnet 4.6.

**Release-family notes (completion pass).**
- *Xanadu (2024):* Platform Analytics experience introduced as the Next Experience analytics successor to Core UI reports and PA dashboards. Now Assist Skill Kit and Now Assist for CMDB (Patch 3) arrived, along with "Triage cases" AI agents for CSM (Patch 7), the Microsoft Copilot integration, AI Search in the knowledge portal, and preparation to deprecate the ITSM Predictive Intelligence Workbench. Automated Skip Resolution for upgrades was expanded.
- *Yokohama (2025):* AI Agents, AI Agent Studio and AI Agent Orchestrator became GA. Voice input and Conversational Q&A came to the Now Assist panel (Patch 3), and Zero Copy Connectors for Databricks, BigQuery, Snowflake and Redshift arrived (Patch 5). Also new: CSDM 5, Deny-Unless ACLs for ITSM tables, change flows replacing workflows for new customers, and no further support for the ITSM PI Workbench.
- *Zurich (2025):* AI Agent Fabric added MCP server/client and A2A. The MCP Server Console with AI Control Tower needs Zurich Patch 9 or Australia Patch 2. Other additions: Build Agent, developer sandboxes, Vault Console, Machine Identity Console, the context-menu rewrite with a chosen LLM, granular SOW admin roles and the Coral default theme.
- *Australia (2026, GA 5 May):* Employee Center gained Simplified Navigation, the Enhanced Requests Experience, Otto chat integration through the Moveworks embedded assistant, the "Approve your requests" AI agent and quick start tests. Virtual Agent patches added a prompt library (P4), Otto branding (P5), the enhanced chat widget on third-party sites (P6) and automated evaluations (P7). Support for legacy Performance Analytics content ended: existing content keeps working, and new Core UI content creation is allowed through the end of 2027. The Workflow Data Fabric Hub was renamed Zero Copy Connector Hub. FSM added Manager Mobile, lead-time management, territory availability APIs and optimization run summaries. Process Mining added idle-time analysis. Fresh instances deny agentic resources that have no ACL, and query ACLs are auto-loaded by plugins. Now Assist for Employee Center Pro is a new app.
- *Brazil (2026, GA 5 Nov):* No new Employee Center or Virtual Agent features. FSM gained Dispatcher Workspace quick filters, breaks on the calendar and avoid-ferry routing, operational notes, Proxy Actions, skill levels with expiry, Schedule Optimization v2.0 (queue registries replace custom queue properties), Shift Scheduling v8.0, arrive-by appointments and scheduling AI agents. Platform changes (listed in the table) include Studio + IDE merged, Build Agent trial by default, parameterized ATF and the enhanced security plugin auto-activated.
- *Store cadence:* Many products ship as Store apps on parallel version tracks per family (for example Employee Center Pro 42.x for Australia and 38.x for Zurich in 2026). Feature availability therefore depends on both the family and the app version.

**2026 branding and corporate moves.** Now Assist, Moveworks and "AI Experience" were merged and renamed **ServiceNow Otto** (announced at Knowledge 2026 on 5 May 2026; in-product relabeling around Aug 2026; entitlements unchanged). Moveworks acquisition closed 15 Dec 2025 (AI front door, enterprise search, reasoning engine). Veza (identity security graph) and Traceloop (AI observability) underpin the 2026 AI Control Tower; Armis (exposure management) was announced in Dec 2025 (deal status unverified). Knowledge 2026 also introduced **Action Fabric** (headless platform for external agents via an MCP server; Anthropic is the first design partner), **EmployeeWorks**, an "Autonomous Workforce" of AI specialists, and "Autonomous CRM".

**UI generations.** Core UI (UI16, legacy forms/lists), Next Experience (Polaris, Unified Navigation, workspaces built with UI Builder; Coral default theme since Zurich), Service Portal (AngularJS widgets), Employee Center, Now Mobile and the ServiceNow Agent mobile app, plus an "AI-native fulfiller experience" (2026, details unverified).

### Implementation notes
- Model a polymorphic **task supertable** (shared columns plus a type discriminator) with module tables extending it. SLAs, approvals, assignment, journals, attachments, watchers and audit then work once for every record type. Use JSONB for dynamic attributes; Brazil's "compliance dynamic schema" (name-value pairs without new columns) confirms the pattern.
- Store journals (work notes vs customer-visible comments) in a separate append-only table keyed by record and element. Build the activity stream as a merged, time-ordered feed of journal entries, field audits, emails and system events.
- Use one **condition language** (a JSON AST equivalent to encoded queries) everywhere: list filters, UI policies, ACL conditions, SLA start/pause/stop, assignment rules, notification conditions, user criteria and trigger rules. Compile it both to SQL (server) and to a JS predicate (client).
- Flow engine: persist the execution context so waits, approvals and timers can resume. Version flows (draft vs published snapshot). Make actions idempotent. Add error branches (try/catch) and execution logs per step.
- Approval engine: evaluate rule types (any/all/percent/first), resolve delegates when approvals are created, send reminders/escalations, and mark siblings "no longer required" when a rule is satisfied.
- Business-time math: expand schedule spans (RRULE-like) into intervals and subtract holidays per time zone. This one service is reused by SLA, on-call, auto-close and change windows.
- i18n: keep translation tables for UI strings, choice labels and translated fields, and an RTL-aware layout (logical CSS properties).

Resolved incidents auto-close after N days (glide.ui.autoclose.time; **Brazil counts business days**, 0 disables). [ITSM]
- **Priority lookup (impact × urgency)** — Data lookup rules (Priority Lookup Rules) map Impact and Urgency to Priority. Default 3×3: 1/1→1 Critical; 1/2 and 2/1→2 High; 1/3, 2/2, 3/1→3 Moderate; 2/3, 3/2→4 Low; 3/3→5 Planning. A UI policy makes Priority read-only. Only admins and data-lookup admins edit the matrix. Priority drives SLAs. Problem has its own lookup. [ITIL]
- **Assignment** — Assignment rules (ordered conditions → group/user), assignment data lookups (category/subcategory/CI → group), CI support group auto-population, **Predictive Intelligence** group/category prediction, and **Advanced Work Assignment (AWA)**, which pushes work to agents through queues with capacity, skills and presence (inbox in workspace; chat and records). Assign-to-me and reassignment from SOW lists (Yokohama). AI "routing mode selector" for reassign tasks (July 2026). [ITSM]
- **Incident tasks** — incident_task child work items for other groups, with their own state and SLAs. Closed tasks are locked from editing since Zurich. [ITSM]
- **Parent/child incidents** — Children link to a parent; resolving the parent cascades resolution code and notes to children, and comments/work notes can propagate. "Copy incident" and "create child incident" behavior is configurable in Admin Center (Yokohama). [ITSM]
- **Major Incident Management (MIM)** — Agents propose a candidate with "Propose Major Incident", or **trigger rules** auto-flag candidates (for example P1 on a critical business service). A major_incident_manager promotes, rejects, demotes or closes. Promoting a candidate makes the major incident its parent. Major incident states: proposed, accepted, rejected, canceled. The **Major Incident Workbench** is a single pane for MIM managers, communication managers and resolvers (overview, timeline, communications, tasks, child incidents, affected CIs/services, conference calls). Also included: a major incident playbook, **Post-Incident Report (PIR)** with flagged activity events forming the PIR timeline, PIR co-contributors (Australia), announcements as banner or portal widget (Xanadu), GenAI email templates for MI communications (Yokohama), "add similar incidents as children" using similarity solution definitions (Zurich), and quick start tests. [ITSM Advanced / Major Incident Management]
- **Incident communication plans (Task Communications Management)** — Communication plans attach automatically when conditions match (incident type, priority, audience). For example, a P1 plan sends updates more often than P2. Plans create communication tasks that run in sequence. ### Implementation notes

### Implementation notes
- Make change models first-class: `change_model` (type, active, presets) → `model_state` (initial flag, implementation flag) → `model_transition` (from, to, condition AST or script, required fields, auto/manual) → `state_field_policy` (state, field, mandatory/read-only). The form engine and API both enforce policies.
- Approval policy = ordered decision table over policy inputs → approval definitions (approver source: a group manager field, a CAB group, a named group, or a script). Support "first match" and "all matching" modes, and gate later decisions on earlier outcomes (the community reports parallel firing as a pitfall).
- Risk = max(engine results). Each engine writes {risk, impact, explanation, inputs}. Calculated risk = lookup(probability band, impact), where the band comes from ordered success-probability definitions (for example team success score ≥ X, or model success ≥ Y). Keep manual overrides audited.
- Conflict detection is an async worker per change: (1) overlapping planned windows on the same CI and its one-hop parent/child CIs, (2) window ⊄ CI maintenance schedule (business-time intersection), (3) window ∩ blackout schedules ≠ ∅, (4) assignee calendar overlap, (5) active lockdown match. Cap conflicts per type, store typed conflict rows, and rerun when CI, dates or state change.
- Success score: a daily aggregate per group over a rolling window. Inputs: share of successful closures, unsuccessful/backed-out changes, changes linked to incidents (caused-by), emergency and unauthorized share. Normalize to a band with 4 ratings and keep history for trend charts.
- Lockdown: a record with a window, scope condition and reason. On activation, set matching in-flight changes on hold with reason "Lockdown"; on release, restore the previous state.
- Scaled change: parent change plus a generator that creates child changes per CI batch, inherits plans, and staggers the schedule within maintenance windows.

### Implementation notes
- Store the class hierarchy as table inheritance (or a single CI table with a class discriminator plus per-class JSONB attributes and a class-attribute registry). Record source provenance per attribute (multisource) so "who set this value" is answerable.

- Impact analysis is graph traversal from CI to the services that depend on it (BFS over "Depends on/Used by" and "Runs on" edges, with a depth cap and cycle detection). Cache materialized service → CI membership for alerts and change risk.
- Discovery needs an on-prem collector (MID-like agent) that pulls jobs from the cloud instance (outbound-only HTTPS), runs scans and patterns, and posts results through the IRE API. Store credentials encrypted, or fetch them from external vaults at run time.

[SLM]
- **Breach visibility in workspaces** — SLA timers on lists and record pages (time left, % elapsed, color states for on-track, at-risk and breached), "about to breach" filters in SOW and agent landing pages, and the AI **Escalation agentic workflow** (2026), which weighs remaining SLA time, urgency, priority and impact and assigns an escalation label (Critical/High/Medium) with a reason. [SOW] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Run an SLA engine on every task write: evaluate the start/pause/resume/stop/reset/cancel conditions of all applicable definitions (filtered by table) in a deterministic order, and write task_sla transitions with timestamps. Store transitions as events so the timeline and repair can replay from audit history.
- Breach time = start + duration in business time (schedule-aware addition skipping non-working spans and holidays in the chosen time zone) + accumulated business pause. Recompute when paused or resumed. Maintain both wall-clock and business elapsed values.
- Retroactive start: set start to a task timestamp and optionally replay pauses from history. Relative durations call a pluggable function (for example end of next business day).
- Milestone notifications: schedule timer jobs at 50/75/100% of business duration (rescheduled on pause/resume) instead of polling all task SLAs.
- Multiple SLAs per task are normal. Always show the nearest-breaching active SLA in lists.

### Implementation notes
- Pipeline: ingest (connectors/webhooks) → normalize (field mapping) → rules (filter/transform/threshold) → CI binding (lookup by host/IP/FQDN/CI id) → de-dup by message key into alerts (count, first/last event, severity = latest) → correlation/grouping → impact propagation → actions (incident creation, remediation, on-call). Keep raw events short-lived (partition by day), and keep alerts longer.
- Grouping algorithms: (1) rule-based primary/secondary; (2) topology-based: alerts on CIs connected within k hops in the CMDB within a time window; (3) temporal co-occurrence learned from history (alerts that historically co-occur within Δt); (4) text similarity of descriptions (embeddings). Merge groups with explainable reasons.
- Maintenance suppression: if a CI is covered by an implementing change or maintenance window, mark alerts as maintenance and do not create incidents.
- Dynamic thresholds: per-metric seasonal baselines (for example hour-of-week medians and MAD, or Holt-Winters). Score anomalies by deviation, and only alert after sustained anomalies.
- On-call: resolve the responder at time t from rota layers (base rotation → overrides/coverage → time off). Escalation is a state machine per notification (sent → delivered → acknowledged | timeout → next step) with a full delivery log.

- Virtual Agent = topic graph runtime (nodes, typed variables, branches, sub-topic calls) + a discovery layer (keyword index → NLU intent classifier → LLM router reading topic descriptions) + fallback to search/RAG + live-agent handoff that carries the transcript. Keep a channel capability matrix (cards, carousels, file upload, rich text, synthesized answers) and degrade controls per channel.

### Implementation notes
- Build a generic "department service" layer: department → category → subcategory → service, where the service holds the intake form, fulfillment template, audience rule, routing and SLA. HR (COE → topic category → topic detail → HR service) is one configuration of it. Give each department its own record type (table) for security, but one shared task core.
- Separate *opened for* from *subject person* in the data model. Visibility, notifications and to-dos must handle a case about someone who did not raise it (and may not be allowed to see it, as in employee relations).
- Lifecycle event engine = a template of activity sets with triggers (immediate, relative date offset, after set X completes, condition) → activities (task, approval, catalog order to another department, document to sign, survey, link) with an audience and criteria. Instantiate per employee, evaluate triggers on a schedule, and roll child completion up to the parent event. Date offsets must use the employee's location calendar.
- Sensitive departments (HR, ER, legal) need stricter defaults: deny for admins unless granted a department role, impersonation blocking, field-level protection, and separate search/AI indexes so restricted text never feeds shared retrieval.
- Document retention: per document type, a retention clock (from termination or creation date) → purge job, skipped while a legal hold exists. Keep an immutable access and audit log.
- Reservations: model a reservable space with capacity, attributes, map coordinates and a booking policy (max duration, advance window, check-in window, auto-release on no-show). Prevent double booking with an exclusion constraint on (space, time range). Waitlists promote the next requester when a booking is released.

### Implementation notes
- Customer model: `organization` (type: customer/partner/vendor, parent_id) + `person` (contact/consumer) + `household`, plus relationship tables (account↔account partner relationships, contact↔account extra access) that feed the visibility predicate. Customer admins see their account subtree; partners see accounts linked by an active relationship, from the relationship start date only.
- Entitlement resolution at case creation: candidates = active entitlements matching (account | contact | install base item | product) and channel and date, ordered by specificity. - Install base ↔ CI links let alerts become proactive cases: impacted CIs → install base items → accounts → contacts to notify. Bulk communications must respect each contact's channel preference.

### Implementation notes
- Separate ad-hoc reporting (live queries with the viewer's permissions compiled into SQL) from KPI analytics (scheduled snapshots). PA-style indicators need a snapshot fact table `(indicator, date, breakdown, element, value)` filled by collection jobs, so trend lines survive record changes and deletions.
- Breakdown = dimension definition (source query for elements + mapping from the record field to an element). Store the top N elements per breakdown and an "other" bucket to bound storage.
- Formula indicators are evaluated from stored scores (not raw rows), so they work for history. Targets/thresholds are rules over the series that create notifications.
- Process mining: build the event log `(case_id, activity, timestamp, resource, attributes)` from audit/history, then directly-follows graph + variant hashing (ordered activity sequence). Conformance = alignment of each trace against a reference model (Petri net or BPMN), or simpler rule checks (required order, forbidden loops). Root-cause analysis = feature importance (decision tree or logistic regression) of record attributes vs "slow/deviant" labels.
- Respect permissions in every aggregate. Counts on dashboards must equal what the viewer could list, so compile row-level filters into the aggregate SQL, never post-filter.

### Implementation notes
- Keep one **AI gateway** service for every model call: provider adapters, per-skill model routing (small vs large tier), PII masking with reversible placeholders, prompt-injection classification, rate limits, cost metering per tenant/skill/user, and a request log (inputs hashed or redacted, outputs, latency, tokens, verdicts).
- Define a **skill** as data: `{inputs (field bindings, retrievers, scripts), prompt template with versions, model tier, output schema, target (field/note/panel), trigger, roles}`. Out-of-box skills are read-only templates that admins "duplicate to customize". Evaluate skills offline with datasets plus an LLM judge before activation.
- Define an **agent** as data: `{role, instructions, tools[], triggers[], run_as (caller|ai_user), allowed_roles mask, autonomy (supervised|autonomous)}`. Before execution, check invoke permission, compute effective permissions (caller ∩ mask, or the AI user's roles), and verify the identity passes every tool's ACL (fail closed). Every tool call re-checks record-level permissions at execution time.
- The orchestrator is a planner loop (plan → pick agent/tool → execute → observe → re-plan) with a persisted plan and step log, human-approval checkpoints for writes in supervised mode, idempotent tool calls, and a hard budget (steps, tokens, wall time).
- MCP server: expose curated tools (skills, subflows, search, knowledge graph queries) with JSON schemas. Authenticate with OAuth 2.1 per client, map each client to a service identity or the end user (token exchange), apply the same permission layer, and give per-tool audit and kill switches. MCP client: register external servers through an approval workflow (in review → approved → active), and allow only approved tools per agent.
- Classic ML still matters: field classification and similarity are cheap and explainable. Report precision and coverage per class, set per-class confidence thresholds, auto-retrain on a schedule, and promote a new model only if it beats the deployed one on a holdout set.
- Track AI value as first-class data: link each AI action to the record, the identity (AI user) and the outcome (accepted/edited/rejected, "closed by AI"), and roll it up into adoption, deflection and time-saved dashboards.

## Module: access

### Features
- **Identities** — `sys_user` holds company, department, location, cost center, manager, domain, time zone, language, active/locked-out, and the flags "web service access only" and "internal integration user". The identity type **AI user** (Australia/Brazil) is a fixed-role identity that AI agents can run as. Users are provisioned from LDAP/AD, SCIM 2.0 or identity-provider spokes (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID) and HR integrations. Licensing personas: fulfillers (roles such as `itil`), approvers, and requesters (no role). [Platform]
- **Groups** — `sys_user_group` with manager, parent group, email, cost center and one or more **group types** (for example itil, approval, catalog) that filter assignment-group pickers. Membership lives in `sys_user_grmember`. Roles granted to a group flow to every member. [Platform]
- **Roles with containment** — `sys_user_role`. Roles **contain** other roles (a role DAG, `sys_user_role_contains`). Attributes: *Elevated privilege*, *Assignable by* (the role needed to grant it), *Requires subscription* (licensing) and *Grantable*. Scoped roles are prefixed with the app scope (for example `x_app.manager`) and cannot be renamed after saving. Code-first apps define roles with the Fluent `Role()` API (`containsRoles`) and reference platform roles by name, not sys_id. 2024-2026 releases split monolithic roles into **granular roles** so admins configure without full `admin`: for example `sn_incident_admin`, `sn_change_admin`, `sn_mim_admin`, `sn_tcm_admin`, `sn_cmdb_admin`, `sn_on_call_admin`, the HAM/SAM admin roles, granular SOW admin roles, `sn_service_desk_agent` (a tier-1 bundle), and the read/write pairs `sn_incident_read/write`, `sn_problem_read/write`, `sn_change_read/write`, `sn_request_write`. [Platform]
- **Elevated roles and High Security** — The High Security plugin creates `security_admin` (an elevated role). Even an admin must **elevate** in-session to create or change ACLs and high-security settings. Other elevated-privilege roles follow the same pattern. [Platform security]
- **Explicit Roles** — Every user is stamped `snc_internal` or `snc_external`. The two are mutually exclusive, cannot contain other roles, and `snc_internal` must never be elevated. This closes the gap where external (portal, customer, partner) users could read tables that have no role requirement. Knowledge bases get default criteria (Can Read for internal users). `snc_read_only` makes a user read-only across the instance (details unverified). [Platform security]
- **ACL rules (Access Control Lists)** — `sys_security_acl`. **Types**: record (table and field), client-callable script include, processor, UI page, REST endpoint, Next Experience routes/data brokers (unverified names), plus agentic types added with AI Agent Studio (generative AI skill, flow, flow action, AI agent, agentic workflow). **Operations**: create, read, write, delete, execute, list_edit, report_on, report_view, personalize_choices, save_as_template, add_to_list, edit_task_relations, edit_ci_relations, query_match, query_range, conditional_table_query_range and data_fabric. **Requirements** (all must pass inside one ACL): required roles (any one of the list), security attribute (compound type only), data condition and script (`answer = true/false`). An ACL needs at least one requirement, and empty or invalid ACLs deny. **Applies-to** decides whether an ACL applies to a record at all, and the data condition is evaluated after that. An **admin overrides** flag lets the admin role bypass an ACL. The object type cannot change after creation. Code-first ACLs use the Fluent ACL API (`type`, `table`, `field`, `operation`, `decisionType: allow|deny`, `roles`, `script`, `adminOverrides`). [Platform security]
- **Allow-If and Deny-Unless decisions** — **Deny-Unless** ACLs are evaluated first and every applicable one must pass (they define who will *not* be denied). Passing a Deny-Unless never grants access by itself. **Allow-If** ACLs come next and at least one must pass. Yokohama added Deny-Unless ACLs for ITSM tables (the `com.snc.itsm.enhanced_security` plugin, auto-activated in Australia/Brazil) to block unauthenticated access to incident, change and related tables. [Platform security]
- **Evaluation order** — A user must pass a **table** ACL (table → parent table → `*`) and then a **field** ACL in the order table.field → parent.field → `*`.field → table.`*` → parent.`*` → `*`.`*`. The first matching field ACL that succeeds stops processing. Failing every table ACL hides all fields. Parent-table ACLs apply to extending tables (task ACLs protect incident unless overridden). [Platform security]
- **Pre-query vs post-query checks and query ACLs** — Pre-query evaluation checks roles and field permissions. Post-query evaluation checks conditions and scripts per returned row, which is why lists show "Number of rows removed from this list by Security constraints". **Query ACLs** stop blind-query inference: `query_match` governs safe operators (=, !=, IN, NOT IN, SAMEAS, empty checks) and `query_range` governs STARTS_WITH, CONTAINS, >=, <=, BETWEEN and **sorting**. Failing them blocks those operators on that column, and the defaults delegate to read. Australia auto-loads query ACLs during plugin installation for most platform plugins. [Platform security]
- **Security Attributes** — Reusable named predicates (`sys_security_attribute`). The **compound** type (an encoded query over roles/groups, such as `Role=manager^ORRole=admin`) is the only type usable in ACLs and data filters. Scripted true/false, string, integer and list attributes serve other uses. `is_dynamic=false` caches the result per session. Never use `current`, because there is no record context. [Platform security]
- **Security Data Filters (data filtration)** — `sys_security_data_filter` (table, mode *if*/*unless*, a compound security attribute, an encoded-query filter, active flag). They are AND-ed into the SQL **before** the query runs (after table-level ACLs, before row ACLs), so there is no "rows removed" message and no leak through reports, counts or aggregates. They apply by default to `GlideRecordSecure`/sandboxed queries and are opt-in elsewhere. ServiceNow says to pair them with Deny ACLs and to use indexed columns. Filters on a child table do not apply when the parent table is queried. [Platform security]
- **Before-query business rules** — Server scripts that add conditions to every query on a table. Out of box, the "incident query" rule limits users without a fulfiller role to incidents where they are caller, opener or on the watch list. It is preferred over row ACLs for list performance and UX. [Platform]
- **Default-deny property** — `glide.sm.default_mode` decides what happens when only wildcard table ACLs match. *Deny Access* blocks CRUD on all tables unless the user is admin or another ACL grants it. It is a one-way switch. On fresh Australia/Brazil instances, agentic resources (AI agents, agentic workflows, gen-AI skills, flows, flow actions) without explicit ACLs are denied, while upgraded instances must opt in. [Platform security]
- **Secure vs unsecured server APIs** — `GlideRecord` in server scripts bypasses ACLs, while `GlideRecordSecure` and sandboxed queries enforce them (data filters need `enableSecurityFeature` elsewhere). Scoped apps add **application access settings** per table (read/create/update/delete from other scopes, web-service access, "accessible from this scope only"), **cross-scope privileges** and **Restricted Caller Access** (for example HR tables read from a global-scope workspace need an RCA record). [Platform]
- **Field-level protection** — Field ACLs (hide or make fields read-only), read-only option levels (`read_only_option`: strict_read_only vs client_script_modifiable; Australia), masked/encrypted fields (**Column Level Encryption Enterprise**, customer-managed keys through the Key Management Framework), Data Privacy masking in clones and AI prompts, HR field security, and data classification of sensitive fields. [Platform / Vault]
- **Domain separation (multi-tenancy)** — For MSPs and multi-company setups. **Data separation**: domain-separated tables get a `sys_domain` field, users see their domain and its descendants, global is the default, data at a higher level is hidden below, and tenants see each other's data only when granted. **Process separation**: business rules, client scripts, UI policies, UI actions, notifications, SLAs, assignment rules, workflows, views, choice lists and other configuration can be domain-specific; parent logic runs for child domains and can be overridden at any level (`sys_overrides`). **UI separation**: forms and lists per domain. Domain types: global, TOP, customer domains, process domains (shared processes, no core data), data domains (shared data) and a default domain. **Contains** grants a whole domain another domain's data, and **visibility domains** grant extra domains to a user or group. Users, groups, departments, locations and CIs inherit their domain from the company unless a **managed domain** is set. Admins switch the working domain with the domain picker. Service providers can give customers **delegated administration**. Domain separation is activated with the Domain Support – Domain Extensions Installer plugin, can be disabled but never removed, and ServiceNow recommends separate instances when complete isolation is required. Coverage grows by release (more CMDB tables in Australia; SDK `sys_domain`/`sys_overrides` support in Brazil). [Domain separation]
- **Organization structure** — Company (`core_company`, parent company, customer/vendor/manufacturer flags), department (`cmn_department`, parent, head, cost center), location (`cmn_location`, parent, type), cost center (`cmn_cost_center`, parent), business unit and the manager chain (`sys_user.manager`). These drive conditions, user criteria, approvals (manager/department-head approvals), assignment, reporting and domain inheritance. [Platform]
- **User criteria (audience rules)** — Reusable records that combine users, groups, roles, companies, departments, locations, a "match all" switch and an optional script. They are bound as Can Read / Cannot Read / Can Contribute / Cannot Contribute on knowledge bases (article level: read only), as Available for / Not available for on catalogs, categories, items and Employee Center content, and as **HR criteria** in HRSD. Deny wins. **User Criteria Diagnostics** explains effective access. [Platform]
- **Delegation** — **Standard delegation** (`sys_user_delegate`: delegate, start/end, approvals, assignments, CC notifications, meeting invitations), honored by flows and workflows. **Granular delegation** (`sys_granular_delegate` + `sys_delegation_rule`) delegates approvals and assignments separately, with record conditions, notification options and "Exclude delegates" for confidential notifications. Property `glide.approval.delegation.version` picks v1 or v2. Legal Service Delivery lets lawyers limit delegation options. [Platform]
- **Impersonation** — Admins and holders of the `impersonator` role act as another user without a password. Actions are attributed to the impersonated user and the impersonation is logged. HRSD has a property that blocks HR data while impersonating. [Platform]
- **Authentication and session security** — Multi-Provider SSO (SAML 2.0, OIDC), local passwords with policies, **multi-factor authentication**, **adaptive authentication** policies (IP ranges, roles, groups, context), IP address access control, session timeouts, OAuth 2.0 for APIs (also mutual TLS and API keys for integrations), **e-signature** re-authentication on approvals, and self-service password reset/account unlock. [Platform security]
- **Access Analyzer and debugging** — **Evaluate Access**, **Compare Access** (two users' table-level access with role, security attribute, condition and script status) and **Access Simulator**. **Debug Security Rules** shows ACL evaluation per field. User Criteria Diagnostics covers knowledge and catalog. [Platform security]
- **Security Center** — Hardening score, version-based hardening checks and risk acceptance, a security scanner, metrics, an access-control console and identity/access section, and granular security admin roles. Brazil auto-activates the enhanced security plugin for ITSM tables on upgrade. [Security Center]
- **Delegated development** — Non-admin developers get per-application permissions to build and deploy specific scoped apps (exact permission list unverified). [App Engine]
- **AI permissions** [AI] — AI invocation ACLs, run-as identity (dynamic user vs AI user), role masking, downstream ACL conjunction across all tools, deny-by-default for agentic resources, AI Search early binding of security filters, Genius Results only from readable content, and Moveworks mirrored permissions (see ai). [AI Agent Studio]

### Implementation notes
- Compute effective access as **(any Allow grant) AND (every applicable Deny-Unless restriction)**, evaluated table → field with inheritance and wildcards. Cache per (user session, table, operation). Keep the rule store declarative (roles + compound attribute + condition AST), with scripts as a rare escape hatch.
- Compile row-level rules (data filters, before-query conditions, domain predicate, user-criteria predicates) into SQL before execution. Use post-query checks only for rules that cannot be compiled, and tell the user rows were hidden. Lists, counts, reports, search, exports, notifications and AI retrieval must all go through the same secure query layer.
- Protect against inference: deny filtering and sorting on columns a user may not range-query (query_match vs query_range semantics).
- Domain separation = a `domain_id` on records plus a domain tree. Visibility = own domain and descendants, plus explicit visibility grants. Configuration lookup walks from the record's domain up to global (nearest override wins). Index `(domain_id, …)` on hot tables.
- Roles are a DAG: precompute the transitive closure per user at login (refresh on change). Mark elevated roles that need step-up re-authentication and expire after a time window. Log every elevation.
- Provide `explain(user, record, operation)` and "compare two users" tools from day one. ServiceNow's Access Analyzer exists because rule interaction is hard to reason about.

### Implementation notes
- Build integrations as **actions with typed inputs/outputs** (a spoke = versioned bundle of actions plus a connection alias). Flows call actions and never hold secrets. The credential store resolves an alias to a connection at run time (per environment/tenant), with secrets encrypted or fetched from an external vault.
- Offer a generic REST/OpenAPI importer that generates actions from a spec, plus REST/SOAP/SSH/JDBC/file protocol steps. Add pagination ("data stream") primitives that stream pages into a loop without loading everything into memory.
- An on-prem relay (MID-style) should connect outbound only, long-poll a job queue, run jobs with local credentials and post results back. Support clusters (leases with heartbeat, re-queue on timeout) and signed self-upgrades.
- Expose a uniform record API (CRUD + query language + field selection + display values + batch) that always runs under the caller's permissions, and a custom-endpoint framework (versioned routes, auth, rate limits, OpenAPI docs). Meter calls per tenant.
- Zero-copy access = virtual tables backed by remote SQL (pushdown of filters, projections, limits). Cache with a TTL, map types, apply the platform's row/field permissions on top, and never write remote data into the local audit trail unless asked.
- Inbound events and webhooks need idempotency keys, signature validation and a dead-letter queue. Outbound calls need retries with exponential backoff, circuit breakers and per-connection rate limits.

### Implementation notes
- Treat configuration as versioned metadata separate from data. Every metadata write records (object id, payload, author, change set). A change set exports as a signed package. - Upgrades must never overwrite customer-modified metadata: compute a three-way diff (old base, new base, customer version) per object, auto-apply when the customer did not touch it, otherwise create a "skipped" review item with merge tooling. Auto-resolve rules must not silently hide new base-system fields.
- A static analysis framework (rule = target selector + predicate/AST lint + severity + category) runs on demand, per change set and in CI. Findings link to objects and track resolution.
- Ship a test framework that admins can use without code (UI and API steps) plus per-module smoke-test packs that run after every upgrade.
- Admin delegation: granular admin roles per module (incident admin, change admin, SLA admin, catalog admin) with an elevated-role pattern for security settings, and an audit trail of every configuration change.

### Implementation notes
- Separate the customer-facing **work order** from dispatchable **tasks**. A task carries hard constraints (skills with minimum level, parts availability, time window, territory, certifications not expired) and soft objectives (travel time, overtime, SLA priority, technician preference).
- Two scheduling modes: (1) **immediate** greedy assignment of a new urgent task (score candidates on constraint feasibility, travel ETA and remaining SLA time), and (2) **batch optimization** over a horizon as a vehicle-routing problem with time windows (VRPTW). Use a solver such as OR-Tools with breaks, lunches and shift bounds, and store a run summary (objectives, dropped tasks and technicians with reason codes) for explainability.
- Breaks are schedule items that pause SLA/work timers on the active task. Offline mobile needs a sync protocol (local queue of state transitions with timestamps, conflict rules where the server state wins for assignment and the device wins for work notes and time entries).
- Appointment availability = (technician shifts in the territory − booked tasks − travel buffers) projected into customer-visible slots. Never expose technician identity or location through the booking API.

## Access control & org structure

- **Principals.** Users (`sys_user`, flagged internal or external through Explicit Roles, plus "web service access only", integration and **AI user** identity types), groups (`sys_user_group`, with types and parent groups) and roles (`sys_user_role`) that contain other roles. Roles reach users directly or through groups. Licensing follows roles: fulfiller roles (for example `itil`) cost money, approver and requester access is cheap or free. Since 2024 every major product has **granular roles** (`sn_<product>_read/write/admin`, `sn_service_desk_agent`, CMDB editor/admin, HAM/SAM admin roles, on-call admin and so on), so configuration and day-to-day work no longer need `admin` or broad `itil`.
- **Permission model.** A rule engine of **ACLs** per table, field and operation (create, read, write, delete, execute, list_edit, report_on/report_view, query_match/query_range, edit_ci_relations, data_fabric and others), each combining roles, a compound **security attribute**, a data condition and a script, all of which must pass. **Deny-Unless** rules are evaluated first and all must pass. **Allow-If** rules then need at least one pass. Table rules come before field rules, with parent-table and wildcard fallbacks. Row scoping comes from pre-query **Security Data Filters** and before-query business rules, which compile into SQL so lists, counts and reports are correct, plus post-query row ACLs that remove rows after the fact. Query ACLs block inference through range filters and sorting. Admin-level changes to security need elevation to `security_admin` (High Security). `glide.sm.default_mode` can make the whole instance deny by default.
- **Org hierarchy.** Company (parent/child, customer/vendor/manufacturer flags) → business unit → department (with head and cost center) → users with a manager chain. Locations form their own hierarchy (region → site → building → floor), and cost centers are hierarchical too. These objects drive user criteria, approvals (manager, department head), assignment, reporting, CSM account visibility and domain inheritance.
- **Data-visibility scoping.** Default ITSM visibility gives fulfillers all incidents, while requesters see only records where they are caller, opener or watcher (the "incident query" before-query rule). **User criteria** (users, groups, roles, companies, departments, locations, script; deny wins) scope knowledge bases, articles and knowledge blocks, catalogs, categories, items and Employee Center content. **HR criteria** and COE security policies scope HR services and cases. **CSM** scopes by account hierarchy, partner relationships and contact relationships for customer, customer admin, partner and partner admin roles. Sensitive apps (HR, security incidents, legal) live in their own scopes and tables with stricter ACLs, field-level security, impersonation blocking and Restricted Caller Access.
- **Multi-company and domain separation.** **Domain separation** adds a `sys_domain` stamp to records and a domain tree (global → TOP → customer/process/data domains). Users see their domain and descendants, plus extra domains granted through visibility domains or "contains" relationships. Configuration (business rules, SLAs, notifications, assignment, workflows, forms, choices) can be overridden per domain and inherits downward. Users, groups, locations and CIs inherit their domain from the company unless a managed domain is set. Service providers can delegate limited administration to customers. Once on, it cannot be removed. ServiceNow recommends separate instances when total isolation is required. Lighter multi-company setups use the company field, before-query rules and user criteria without domains.
- **Delegation and acting for others.** Standard delegation (`sys_user_delegate`: approvals, assignments, notifications, with start/end dates) and **granular delegation** (separate rules for approvals vs assignments, with record conditions and exclusion of confidential notifications). Requesters can order catalog items for someone else (Requested For), and HR distinguishes *opened for* from *subject person*. Appointment delegation groups let assistants book for executives. FSM proxy actions let managers act on a technician's tasks. Impersonation is logged and attributed to the impersonated user, and HR data can be blocked while impersonating.
- **Authentication.** Multi-Provider SSO (SAML 2.0/OIDC), MFA, adaptive authentication (IP, role, group, context), IP access control, session policies, OAuth 2.0/2.1 for APIs and MCP, mutual TLS, API keys, e-signature on approvals, LDAP/SCIM provisioning, and the Machine Identity Console for integration accounts.
- **Explainability and assurance.** Access Analyzer (evaluate, compare two users, simulate), Debug Security Rules, User Criteria Diagnostics, Security Center hardening score and scanner, and audit of ACL, role and membership changes.
- **How AI respects permissions.** (1) **Invocation ACLs** on skills, AI agents, agentic workflows, flows and flow actions decide who may run them (any authenticated user, specific roles, or public). Fresh Australia/Brazil instances deny agentic resources that have no explicit ACL. (2) A **run-as identity** decides whose permissions the AI uses: the *dynamic user* (the invoking person, or the triggering record's context) or a fixed *AI user* with its own minimal roles. (3) **Role masking** narrows a dynamic user's roles to an approved subset (effective = caller ∩ allowed). The run-as identity must satisfy the ACLs of **every tool** on an agent, so the check fails closed. All record access during the run goes through normal ACLs and data filters. **AI Search/Genius Results** compile the user's security filters into retrieval (early binding) and fall back to per-hit `canRead` checks (late binding). External content keeps its source permissions through identity aliasing, and Moveworks mirrors ServiceNow user criteria and source permissions into relationship tuples. Data Privacy masking and Now Assist Guardian (prompt-injection and offensiveness detection, sensitive-topic filters) protect prompts and outputs. **AI Control Tower/AI Gateway** governs which MCP servers and clients are approved (AI Steward), enforces OAuth 2.1, blocks sensitive data and can pause any agent or connection instantly. A2A traffic is outside AI Gateway.

## Differentiators

- **One platform, one data model, one security model.** Every workflow product (IT, HR, customer, security, risk, finance, field, workplace, legal) shares the task supertable, CMDB, SLA engine, approvals, knowledge, portal and AI layer. Cross-department processes (onboarding touching HR, IT, facilities and security) and cross-domain AI context work without integration.
- **CMDB depth and data governance.** The IRE (identification, reconciliation and refresh rules with multisource provenance, Dynamic IRE), CSDM 5 as a prescriptive service model, CMDB Health KPIs, Data Manager lifecycle policies, CMDB 360 and certified Service Graph Connectors make it the reference CMDB.
- **Native discovery and service mapping.** MID Server-based agentless discovery, cloud/Kubernetes discovery, ACC agents and top-down, tag-based and traffic-based service mapping feed ITSM impact, change risk and AIOps from the same graph.
- **Change management maturity.** Change models with state field policies, multi-engine risk (conditions, assessments, calculated risk, ML), team Change Success Scores, policy-driven approvals, conflict detection with maintenance/blackout windows, change lockdown, scaled change, CAB Workbench and DevOps change automation with pipeline gating.
- **Rule-based access control with domain separation.** Allow-If/Deny-Unless ACLs per table, field and operation, security attributes, pre-query data filters, query ACLs against inference, user criteria and true multi-tenant domain separation with process and UI overrides. It is among the most granular permission models in the market, and it has diagnostics (Access Analyzer).
- **Agentic AI at platform level with governance.** AI Agents, Orchestrator, Studio, evaluations, AI Specialists, and a governance stack (AI Control Tower, AI Gateway, Guardian, Data Privacy masking, invocation ACLs, AI-user identities, role masking, deny-by-default for agents).
- **Open agent interoperability.** The MCP Server Console exposes governed ServiceNow skills, flows and knowledge to any MCP client (Claude, Copilot Studio, Bedrock). MCP clients and A2A let ServiceNow agents use external tools and agents ("headless" ITSM through Action Fabric).
- **Model choice.** Now LLM, certified Azure OpenAI/Gemini/Claude, BYOK and BYOLLM, with per-skill model selection and data masking, instead of a single locked model.
- **Breadth of prebuilt AI.** Hundreds of out-of-box skills and agents across ITSM, ITOM, CMDB, ITAM, SecOps, IRM, CSM, HR, FSM, SPM, knowledge and developer tooling, plus Moveworks enterprise search and assistant.
