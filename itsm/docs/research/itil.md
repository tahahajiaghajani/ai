# ITIL 4 (and ITIL Version 5 status/changes) — the complete practice model as tool requirements

## Overview

**What ITIL is, for a tool builder.** ITIL is a vendor-neutral best-practice framework. It is not software and not a certifiable standard: individuals get certified, organizations do not (ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 is the certifiable service-management standard that ITIL maps to; tools are assessed by third parties such as Pink Elephant's PinkVERIFY). Every major ITSM product (ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Freshservice, BMC Helix, Ivanti Neurons, ManageEngine ServiceDesk Plus, TOPdesk, SysAid, Xurrent, SolarWinds, Halo) uses ITIL's vocabulary, record types, practices, roles, value-flow model and metrics as its product spine. This report converts the full ITIL model into concrete tool requirements: records and fields, lifecycle states, rules, roles, KPIs (with formulas), UI, automation and AI.

**Ownership and release history.** UK CCTA (1989, ~30 books) → OGC (v2 2001; v3 "2007 edition" with the 5-stage service lifecycle; ITIL 2011 edition) → AXELOS (Capita + UK Cabinet Office joint venture, 2013) → PeopleCert (acquired AXELOS, June 2021; owns the ITIL trademark). ITIL 4 Foundation: February 2019; Managing Professional / Strategic Leader books: 2020; 34 ITIL 4 practice guides: January 2020 (later re-issued with "capability assessment and development" sections and "tips for practice success"); ITIL Maturity Model: 2021; ITIL 4 Practice Manager scheme (single-practice "Practitioner" certificates and three 5-practice bundles MSF/PIC/CAI): 2023; ITIL 4 extension modules: Sustainability in Digital & IT, Acquiring & Managing Cloud Services, Business Relationship Management, Digital & IT Strategy (leader).

**ITIL (Version 5).** Yes — published. PeopleCert launched "ITIL (Version 5)" on ~29–30 January 2026 (sources differ by one day). Official name is "ITIL"; "(Version 5)" only distinguishes it; the name no longer expands to "IT Infrastructure Library". Rollout (per training-provider timelines): ITIL Foundation (Version 5) exam 12 Feb 2026; Foundation Bridge for ITIL 4 holders 26 Feb 2026; Product, Service and Experience modules 12 Mar 2026; Strategy and Transformation 9 Apr 2026; Managing Professional transition module 14 May 2026 (reported). PeopleCert reportedly describes the content as ~40% retained, ~36% new, ~24% changed (unverified against PeopleCert's own pages). ITIL 4 remains on sale and its certificates remain valid. Official Practice Guides updated for v5 (terminology, AI integration for selected practices, added product focus) were announced for H2 2026 via PeopleCert Plus; whether all 34 were released by 2026-11-07 could not be verified (unverified) — the 2020–2023 ITIL 4 practice guides remain the most detailed practice-level reference and are what this report uses for practice internals.

**Version 5 status as of 2026-11-07 (checked against Global Knowledge's ITIL Version 5 FAQ, itil.org.uk, Purple Griffon and Dion Training pages):**
- Published and examinable: Foundation (Feb 2026), Foundation Bridge, Product, Service, Experience (Mar 2026), Strategy and Transformation (Apr 2026), MP transition (May 2026). The three Practice Manager modules (Monitor, Support & Fulfil; Plan, Implement & Control; Collaborate, Assure & Improve) exist as v5 courses; MSF (v5) covers incident, service desk, service request, monitoring & event, and problem management. Training providers list v5 MSF classes through 2027.
- The scheme has Foundation, **eight advanced modules** (MSF, PIC, CAI, Product, Service, Experience, Strategy, Transformation) and **one extension module, AI Governance**. ITIL 4 extension modules (Sustainability, Cloud, BRM, etc.) are no longer separate; their content was absorbed into the core v5 guidance. Single-practice courses are not part of the v5 scheme; only the three bundled practice modules are.
- **ITIL AI Governance (Version 5):** announced (Global Knowledge article, 20 Aug 2026); accredited training starts around **16 Nov 2026** (Purple Griffon). Exam per that provider: 40 questions, 90 minutes, pass mark 70%, closed book (another provider says open book — conflicting). Prerequisites are also reported inconsistently (ITIL 4/v5 Foundation vs none). Stated learning outcomes: govern AI adoption across digital products and services; identify and mitigate regulatory, ethical, security and reputational AI risks; apply decision-authority frameworks, data-governance principles and fairness criteria; establish AI transparency, explainability and accountability mechanisms; assess AI readiness and the current governance landscape; design AI governance structures; sustain continuous AI governance.
- **Practice content access:** "All 34 ITIL (Version 5) practices are available exclusively via PeopleCert Plus membership"; the 15 practices in the Practice Manager modules come with the training. The v5 Foundation exam gives **0%** weight to detailed practice workflows (ITIL 4 Foundation gave 42.5% to seven practices); value streams are 5% and AI 2.5% of the Foundation exam (Dion Training, secondary). Foundation is closed-book; Product, Service, Experience, Strategy and Transformation exams are (planned) open-book.
- **Coexistence:** no ITIL 4 retirement date announced; ITIL 4 and v5 run in parallel for at least 12 months; ITIL 4 Foundation remains a valid prerequisite for v5 advanced modules. v5 certificates carry a 3-year renew-by date (CPD 20 points/year via PeopleCert Plus, another ITIL certificate, or re-exam).
- New v5 Foundation concepts per training summaries: generative AI, agentic AI ("operate with a degree of autonomy", taking actions toward objectives rather than fixed instructions), AI maturity models, AI governance, the ITIL AI Capability Model (6C), experience level agreements (XLAs, described as "a new concept"), observability (term attached to monitoring & event and to infrastructure & platform management).

**"Editions / tiers" (the qualification scheme is the closest analogue to editions).**
- ITIL 4: Foundation → Managing Professional (Create, Deliver & Support [CDS]; Drive Stakeholder Value [DSV]; High-velocity IT [HVIT]; Direct, Plan & Improve [DPI]) | Strategic Leader (DPI + Digital & IT Strategy [DITS]) | Practice Manager (Monitor, Support & Fulfil [MSF: service desk, incident, problem, service request, monitoring & event]; Plan, Implement & Control [PIC: change enablement, deployment, release, service configuration, IT asset]; Collaborate, Assure & Improve [CAI]; or single-practice Practitioner certificates — 15 offered) | ITIL Master; plus extension modules.
- ITIL (Version 5): Foundation (+ Bridge) → ITIL Practice Manager (one of MSF / PIC / CAI + Transformation) | ITIL Managing Professional (Product + Service + Experience + Transformation) | ITIL Strategic Leader (Strategy + Transformation) | ITIL Master (all three designations); one extension: ITIL AI Governance. Seven publications: ITIL Foundation, ITIL Product, ITIL Service, ITIL Experience, ITIL Strategy, ITIL Transformation, ITIL AI Governance (all "Version 5").

### ITIL 4 core model (what the tool must embody)
- **Key concepts.** Value = "the perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something". Service = "a means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks". Product = "a configuration of an organization's resources designed to offer value for a consumer". Service offering = one or more services for a target consumer group, made of goods (transferred), access to resources (granted) and service actions (performed). Output (deliverable) vs outcome (result for a stakeholder). Costs and risks are removed from or imposed on the consumer. Utility = functionality to meet a need (fit for purpose); warranty = assurance the product/service will meet agreed requirements (fit for use: availability, capacity, security, continuity). Organization roles: service provider and service consumer; consumer roles customer (defines requirements, responsible for outcomes), user (uses services) and sponsor (authorizes budget). Service relationship = service provision + service consumption + service relationship management.
- **Four dimensions of service management:** (1) organizations and people, (2) information and technology, (3) partners and suppliers, (4) value streams and processes; constrained by PESTLE external factors (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental). Every practice and practice success factor is described across all four dimensions.
- **Service Value System (SVS):** opportunity/demand → [guiding principles, governance, service value chain, practices, continual improvement] → value.
- **Seven guiding principles:** focus on value; start where you are; progress iteratively with feedback; collaborate and promote visibility; think and work holistically; keep it simple and practical; optimize and automate. (Unchanged in Version 5.)
- **Governance:** a governing body evaluates, directs and monitors (ISO/IEC 38500 pattern; v5 summaries add "engage stakeholders" first, and describe four governance patterns — directive, guided, federated, autonomous; secondary source).
- **Service value chain (ITIL 4):** six activities — plan, improve, engage, design & transition, obtain/build, deliver & support. Value streams are scenario-specific combinations of value-chain activities and practices.
- **Continual improvement model (ITIL 4):** What is the vision? → Where are we now? → Where do we want to be? → How do we get there? → Take action → Did we get there? → How do we keep the momentum going? Improvements are tracked in a Continual Improvement Register (CIR).
- **34 management practices** in three groups: 14 general management, 17 service management, 3 technical management (table below).
- **Practice-guide anatomy (important for tool design):** purpose; terms; scope (and what belongs to other practices); practice success factors (PSF = "a complex functional component of a practice that is required for the practice to fulfil its purpose"), each with key metrics; value-stream contribution heat-map over the six value-chain activities; processes (inputs → activities → outputs, workflow diagrams); roles with competency profiles coded L (leader), A (administrator), C (coordinator/communicator), M (methods & techniques expert), T (technical expert); information & technology (record data, automation table rating each activity's automation impact); partners & suppliers; capability criteria (up to ~30 per practice, each mapped to a PSF, a dimension and a capability level 1–5); tips per guiding principle.
- **ITIL Maturity Model:** practice capability levels 1 (informal/intuitive) to 5 (continually improving/optimized); SVS (organizational) maturity levels 1–5; assessment types: comprehensive (practices + SVS), high-level (SVS only), selected practices.

### ITIL 4 publications beyond Foundation and their tool implications
- **CDS (Create, Deliver & Support):** value streams and value-stream mapping; team culture, service empathy, positive communication, competency profiles, employee satisfaction; shift-left (move knowledge closer to the user via self-service, better-trained front line, swarming); swarming; queues/backlogs and prioritization (triage, Kanban, WIP limits); sourcing, buy-vs-build, SIAM; "integrated service management toolset", information models, integration & data sharing, workflow & collaboration tools, reporting & advanced analytics, automation (RPA, AI/ML, CI/CD). Example value streams: "development of a new service" and "restoration of a live service" (7 steps: acknowledge and record the user contact [engage] → investigate, classify as incident, attempt fix [deliver & support] → get a fix from the specialist team [obtain/build] → apply the fix [design & transition] → verify resolution [deliver & support] → ask for user feedback [engage] → identify improvement opportunities [improve]).
- **DSV (Drive Stakeholder Value):** customer journey Explore → Engage → Offer → Agree → Onboard → Co-create → Realize; touchpoints and service interactions; service relationship types basic / cooperative / partnership; CX/UX and journey mapping; demand shaping and offers; agreements (SLAs and value-focused agreements); onboarding and offboarding; user engagement, service empathy, omnichannel; value realization tracking and reporting.
- **HVIT (High-velocity IT):** five desired outcomes — valuable investments, fast development, resilient operations, co-created value, assured conformance; Lean/Agile/DevOps/SRE techniques (CI/CD, IaC, chaos engineering, error budgets, blameless reviews, Toyota Kata, Kanban, design thinking, hypothesis-driven development, policy-as-code); safety culture, ethics, complex adaptive systems (behaviour-pattern names unverified).
- **DPI (Direct, Plan & Improve):** direction & scope of control; governance, risk & compliance; policies, controls and guidelines; strategy cascade; assessments (SWOT, balanced scorecard, gap analysis, maturity, benchmarking); measurement & reporting (cascaded goals → CSFs → KPIs → metrics, dashboards); continual improvement; organizational change management & communication; value-stream mapping.
- **DITS (Digital & IT Strategy):** digital organization and digital transformation, vision/mission/objectives, business models, digital positioning, innovation and disruption, investment and funding, digital readiness assessment, strategic risk (details summarized; individual model names unverified).
- **Extensions:** Sustainability in Digital & IT (ESG targets, carbon/energy/e-waste data, sustainable design); Acquiring & Managing Cloud Services (cloud sourcing lifecycle, supplier/contract/financial controls); Business Relationship Management (relationship maturity, value management).

### Value streams (examples a tool should ship as templates)
- ITIL 4 CDS: restoration of a live service (above); development of a new service; user support (service desk + incident + problem + knowledge + SLM + monitoring & event).
- Common ITIL-aligned value streams (names used across ITIL literature, IT4IT and vendors): incident-to-resolution (detect → register → classify → diagnose → resolve → close → review); request-to-fulfilment (request → approve → fulfil → deliver → confirm → feedback); problem-to-fix (identify → analyse → workaround/known error → change → verify → close); change-to-deploy / requirement-to-deploy (RFC → assess → authorize → schedule → build/test → deploy → review); demand/idea-to-value (idea → evaluate → fund → design → build → transition → realize); event-to-remediation (event → correlate → alert → automate/incident → resolve); onboard-to-offboard and hire-to-retire (enterprise); procure-to-retire for assets (request → procure → receive → deploy → maintain → retire → dispose); strategy-to-portfolio (IT4IT S2P), requirement-to-deploy (R2D), request-to-fulfil (R2F), detect-to-correct (D2C).
- Version 5 adds core vs enabling value streams (enabling examples: procurement, supplier onboarding) and value-stream metrics: cycle time, wait time, lead time, flow efficiency (value-adding time ÷ lead time), and activity classification (value-adding, supporting, coordinating, non-value-adding) — secondary source.

### ITIL (Version 5) — what changed and what it adds to tool requirements
**Changed / new (per itsm.tools analysis of the Foundation text, itil.com articles, PeopleCert community and training-provider summaries):**
- Scope reframed from IT service management to "digital product and service management"; reference point Industry 5.0; described as "AI-native" and "complexity-native".
- Service Value System renamed **ITIL Value System** (same components: guiding principles, governance, value chain, practices, continual improvement; new visual).
- **ITIL Product and Service Lifecycle Model** replaces the six-activity service value chain with eight activities used as the value chain: Discover, Design, Acquire, Build, Transition, Operate, Deliver, Support. Non-linear "ecosystem" model — any activity can lead to any other; every activity can lead back to Discover; versions of the same product/service can be in different stages simultaneously; products and services are "two sides of the same, digitally-enabled technology solution" managed in one shared lifecycle. ITIL 4's combined "design & transition" is split; operate / deliver / support are distinguished. Each stage is documented with why, who, when, key outputs and metrics (Support stage, per a secondary summary: purpose "identify and resolve incidents, fulfil disaster recovery when needed" and capture consumer feedback; metrics: user/customer satisfaction, speed of restoration, negative impact of incidents and disasters, SLA performance).
- New **ITIL Service Relationship Model** (provision, consumption, relationship management; relationship types basic, cooperative, collaborative/partnership). The **service journey model** (from ITIL 4 DSV) moves into Foundation.
- **Four Dimensions of Product and Service Management** (renamed). "Organizations and people" gains "Organizations, people and AI" (human–AI collaboration). "Information and technology" gains the **ITIL AI Capability Model (6C)**: creation, curation, clarification, cognition, communication, coordination.
- Practices: 34 names unchanged; regrouped into 2 groups (22 + 12); Foundation Appendix A adds one-page practice "cards"; official Practice Guides to change significantly for some practices (v5 updates announced for H2 2026).
- Continual Improvement Model refined (one secondary site renders steps 6–7 as "Are we getting there?" and "How do we keep the improvements relevant?" — unverified).
- New appendices: Appendix C roles (generic roles such as practice owner/manager, product/service owner, head of product & service management, sustainability officer, risk & compliance manager), Appendix F Maturity Model, Appendix G Transformation Model.
- New books: ITIL Strategy (VUCA-oriented strategy), ITIL Product, ITIL Service, ITIL Experience (service journey mapping, customer + user + employee experience; "employee experience predicts customer experience"), ITIL Transformation (complexity-driven transformation model; secondary source describes four layers — governance, initiation, execution, embedding — and evolutionary/revolutionary/adaptive change patterns), ITIL AI Governance (extension).
- Key terms gain **experience** and **sustainability** next to utility and warranty; secondary summaries group service-level metrics into utility, warranty, sustainability and experience. Digital spectrum: purely analogue, digitally supported, digitally enabled, purely digital. Sustainability is embedded throughout instead of being an extension.

**Extra tool requirements implied by Version 5:**
1. A unified **product + service** record model: digital products and the services built on them share one lifecycle; track lifecycle stage per version (Discover…Support) and allow several versions in different stages at once.
2. **Lifecycle-stage tagging** of every work item and workflow step (eight v5 stages, and optionally the six ITIL 4 activities) so value-stream analytics can be produced in either vocabulary.
3. **Experience management:** XLA definitions, experience indicators (sentiment, time lost, digital-experience telemetry), journey maps with touchpoints, moments of truth and emotions, surveys per touchpoint, employee-experience metrics next to customer experience.
4. **Sustainability data:** energy/carbon attributes on CIs, assets, services and suppliers; e-waste disposal tracking; ESG KPIs in service reviews.
5. **AI governance as a product feature:** AI use-case inventory (including "shadow AI"), 6C classification per AI feature, risk profile (impact, reversibility, transparency, data sensitivity, regulatory, ethical), human–AI collaboration mode per feature, confidence thresholds, approval gates, audit logging, explainability, continuous monitoring and kill switches; mapping to ISO/IEC 42001 controls and EU AI Act obligations.
6. **Complexity-aware work handling:** choose model/automation vs specialist routing vs swarming vs act-first by complexity domain; record the chosen approach.
7. **Transformation and maturity tooling:** maturity assessments (practice capability + value-system maturity), OKRs, transformation roadmaps and stakeholder maps, ADKAR/Kotter tracking.
8. **Service relationship model data:** relationship type per consumer/provider pair and per supplier; consumer-side roles (customer, user, sponsor) captured on accounts and agreements.

## Module: platform

### Features
- **Common work-record base** — every ITIL record (incident, problem, known error, change, release, deployment, request/request item/task, event/alert, knowledge article, CI, asset, contract, risk, CIR item, project, review) shares: number, title/short description, description, state + substate, priority, owner (person) and assignment group, requested by / requested for, service and service offering, CI, opened/updated/closed timestamps, public comments vs internal work notes, attachments, watch list, related records, SLA instances and a full audit history. ITIL's ownership rule: "each incident should have a person responsible for it at any time" — generalize to all work records (owner never null after triage; ownership transfer is logged). [ITIL 4 Incident PG; industry]
- **Data-quality rules: concurrent, complete, comprehensive** — the practice guide requires records to be updated during (not after) the work, to describe what was done in detail and why. Tool: activity timeline with actor/time/action/rationale, structured "action started / action completed" times when entries are written late ("to avoid the creation of a false history log"), automatic logging of every automation and integration action, real-time customer-visible updates when consumers can see the record through a portal. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §2.4.3, §5.1]
- **Single backlog and team-level prioritization** — "Incidents should await processing in a single backlog, together with other tasks (planned and unplanned)"; prioritization is needed only when resources conflict; each team prioritizes by resource availability, target resolution time and estimated processing time; Kanban and WIP limits are recommended. Tool: per-team cross-type backlog (incidents, requests, changes, problems, project tasks) with drag-rank, WIP limits per column, estimated effort, SLA-risk indicator and aging. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §2.4.2.1; CDS]
- **Models as first-class configuration** — incident models, major-incident model, service request models, change models (standard/normal/emergency and sub-models), release and deployment models, problem/RCA templates; a model bundles triggers/classification, workflow, roles, approvals/authority, task templates, SLAs, automation scripts and communication templates; versioned and periodically reviewed ("periodic incident review" updates models). [ITIL 4]
- **Workflow / flow designer** — low-code state machines and flows (inputs → activities → outputs, matching the ITIL process definition: "a set of interrelated or interacting activities that transform inputs into outputs"), parallel branches, subflows, timers, approvals, conditions, integration actions, human tasks, error handling; flows attachable to models. [ITIL 4 PG; industry]
- **Business rules and data policies** — on-create/on-update rules, mandatory fields per state transition (e.g., resolution code and notes to resolve; backout plan to authorize a change), defaulting, auto-assignment, field visibility, client- and server-side validation. [industry]
- **Approval engine** — single/multi-level, sequential or parallel (all, any, quorum, percentage), approvers resolved by rule (manager of requested-for, CI/service owner, change authority, CAB, budget holder/sponsor, data/resource owner), delegation, approve from email/mobile/chat, auto-approval policies (pre-authorized standard changes, low-cost items), timeouts and escalation, mandatory rationale on reject, full audit (non-repudiation). [ITIL 4 change authority, SRM approvals, infosec non-repudiation]
- **Notification and communication engine** — event-driven (state change, assignment, comment, SLA warning/breach, approval, major-incident update), templates with variables, channels (email, SMS, push, Teams/Slack, voice), subscriptions and stakeholder lists, digest, quiet hours; "stakeholder communications should be updated whenever there are changes in the status of the incident". [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Calendars and schedules** — business-hours/holiday calendars per service, region and SLA; change schedule; maintenance and blackout windows; on-call rotas; shift schedules; all time-zone aware. [ITIL 4]
- **Complexity-aware routing** — the incident PG distinguishes simple (pre-defined/automated model), complex (specialist group) and very complex (swarming, safe-to-fail experiments, Cynefin-style decision framework) situations; v5 is "complexity-native". Tool: complexity field on work items, rules that pick the handling pattern, a "start swarm" action and an experiment log. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; ITIL v5]
- **Value-stream modeler** — define value streams as ordered steps, each tagged with value-chain activity (ITIL 4 six activities and/or v5 eight lifecycle activities) and contributing practices; bind workflow states to steps so each record emits step entry/exit events for lead/cycle/wait time and flow-efficiency analytics. [ITIL 4 CDS/DPI; ITIL v5]
- **Integrated toolset / unified data model** — CDS stresses an integrated service management toolset that shares information across practices; cross-linking of all records, a single CMDB and a single user/org model are prerequisites. [ITIL 4 CDS]
- **Global search** — across records, knowledge, catalog, CIs and people with permission filtering; typo tolerance, synonyms; search analytics. [ITIL 4 knowledge pitfalls "difficult to use interfaces"]
- **Import/export and bulk operations** — CSV/Excel import with transform maps and identification rules, scheduled imports, export to CSV/PDF/XLSX, bulk update with audit. [industry]
- **Audit and history** — field-level history (old/new/actor/time), immutable, exportable; e-signature on approvals; retention policies. [ISO/IEC 20000; ITIL infosec non-repudiation]
- **Localization and accessibility** — multi-language UI and content (catalog items, knowledge, notifications), RTL scripts, locale formats, WCAG accessibility. [industry; PESTLE social]
- **Mobile** — agent app (queues, update, approve, scan barcodes, on-call acknowledge), requester app (raise, track, approve), offline mode for field work, push notifications. [industry; DSV omnichannel]
- **Guiding principles as product behaviours** — focus on value (value/outcome fields and benefit tracking), start where you are (import + assessment), progress iteratively with feedback (feedback loops, surveys), collaborate and promote visibility (shared timelines, public status), think and work holistically (cross-practice links), keep it simple and practical (minimal mandatory fields, configurable forms), optimize and automate (automation after optimization; automation metrics). [ITIL 4]

### Implementation notes
- Base table `work_item` (id, type, number, state, priority, owner_id, group_id, service_id, ci_id, requested_for, timestamps, sla_summary) plus per-type extension tables or a JSONB attributes column; per-type state machine definitions stored as data (states, transitions, guards, required fields, actions).
- Activity stream as an append-only event log (event sourcing); metrics such as reassignment count, time in state, % waiting time and first-time resolution are derived from it, never from mutable counters alone.
- Model versioning: an in-flight record keeps a reference to the model version it started with; model edits create a new version.
- Backlog rank: lexicographic/fractional rank per team; "priority" remains an attribute, "rank" is the team's decision (ITIL distinguishes evaluating impact/urgency from prioritization).
- Calendar service computes business durations (with pause intervals) for SLAs and metrics; cache per calendar/day.
- Every automated action writes to the timeline with actor = automation identity, rule id and model version, giving explainability and AI auditability.

## Module: incident

### Features
- **Practice card: Incident management** — Purpose: "to minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible." Normal operation is defined by SLAs or internal service specifications, and for invisible failures by CI/technical specifications or expert opinion. Scope: detecting and registering incidents; diagnosing and investigating; restoring affected services and CIs to agreed quality; managing incident records; communicating with stakeholders throughout the lifecycle; reviewing incidents and initiating improvements. Out of scope (handled by other practices): investigating causes (problem), communicating with users (service desk), implementing changes (change enablement, deployment, release, project, software development, infrastructure & platform), monitoring (monitoring & event), improvement initiatives (continual improvement), service requests (service request management), disasters (service continuity). Value-chain contribution: engage, deliver & support, design & transition, improve, obtain/build. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Definitions to embed in UI/help** — Incident: "An unplanned interruption to a service or reduction in the quality of a service." Major incident: "An incident with significant business impact, requiring an immediate coordinated resolution." Incident model: "A repeatable approach to the management of a particular type of incident." Workaround: "A solution that reduces or eliminates the impact of an incident or problem for which a full resolution is not yet available. Some workarounds reduce the likelihood of incidents." Technical debt: "The total rework backlog accumulated by choosing workarounds instead of system solutions that would take longer." Swarming: multiple people with different expertise work together "until it becomes clear which competencies are the most relevant and needed." Escalation: an activity that obtains additional resources to meet service targets or customer expectations. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; CDS]
- **PSFs and processes** — PSFs: (1) detecting incidents early; (2) resolving incidents quickly and efficiently; (3) continually improving the incident management approaches. Processes: (a) Incident handling and resolution — detection → registration → classification → diagnosis → resolution → closure; inputs: monitoring & event data, user queries, configuration and asset information, service catalogue, SLAs with consumers and suppliers, capacity/performance information, continuity and security policies/plans, problem records, knowledge base; outputs: incident records, status communications, problem investigation requests, change requests, incident reports, knowledge-base updates, restored CIs and services. (b) Periodic incident review — review & records analysis → incident model improvement initiation → model update communication; run every 2–3 months or when triggered; outputs: updated models and procedures, change requests, improvement initiatives, review reports. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Roles** — incident manager (competency profile LCTA: coordinates handling, multi-team work, monitors teams, ensures awareness, runs reviews, develops expertise; may be the resource/service/product owner); major incident manager (MIM: wider authority, dedicated resources, main point of contact during major incidents); service desk agent (registration, classification, closure); technical specialist (detection, diagnosis, resolution); supplier (diagnosis, resolution, review); user (detection, resolution confirmation); product owner/service owner (closure in some models, reviews, model improvement). [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Incident record — ITIL-recommended content (PG Table 5.1)** — incident title (function/process + observed fault or degradation); user(s) affected / reporting user; current impact (text: actual effect on user/customer workflow); future impact (potential impact while the incident continues); time of first symptom; time of last good function; detail of affected item (asset IDs, application and process names, CI reference); detail of comparable item not affected; investigation detail (steps and results); assigned to (person or group). Also exchanged: information source; failing product/service/CI; impacted users/services; symptoms; when observed; whether an automatic fix was applied (and if not, why); geographic and virtual location; nature and extent of impact; similar systems that might be affected; sequence of events; every action taken with results. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §5.1]
- **Incident record — tool fields beyond ITIL** — number; channel (phone, email, chat, portal, walk-in, virtual agent, monitoring/event, API); caller, affected user, VIP flag; company, department, location; service, service offering, CI; category/subcategory; impact, urgency, computed priority; state/substate and on-hold reason (awaiting user, supplier, change, problem); parent/child incidents; related problem/known error; caused-by change; major incident status; resolution code and notes; close code; knowledge used/created; SLA timers (response, resolution, OLA, supplier UC); reassignment and reopen counters; timestamps (occurred, detected, registered, accepted for diagnosis, diagnosis start/end, resolved, closed); effort and cost; resolution price (for chargeback); supplier ticket reference; automatic-resolution flag; detected-before-user-report flag; satisfaction score. [industry]
- **Detection and registration** — automatic registration from monitoring (event identified as incident by predefined classification; record associated with the CI where the event was detected; predefined technical data registered; specialists notified); user-detected via service desk channels (agent triages the query, confirms it is an incident, registers data). Promote responsible service consumption: encourage reporting of suspicious behaviour and tolerate false reports. [ITIL 4 Incident PG Table 3.2]
- **Classification (manual, part-automatic, automatic)** — classification qualifies impact, identifies the responsible team for failed CIs/services, links the incident to past/ongoing events, incidents and problems, and may reveal a predefined solution; "part-automatic categorization is made manually but is based on suggestions made by the system"; automated detection and categorization "may benefit from machine learning solutions, using the data available from past incidents, events, known errors, and other sources"; automatic classification discovers impact on services/users, available solutions and responsible team. Wrong CI association must be reported to configuration control. [ITIL 4 Incident PG] [AI]
- **Impact × urgency priority matrix with backlog prioritization** — impact = effect on the business (who, where, when); urgency = time available before the business is negatively affected; a configurable matrix maps to priority and target times; ITIL insists the evaluation is input to prioritization, not prioritization itself, and teams may override SLA-driven order with justification. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; CDS]
- **Incident models and automated resolution** — predefined procedures for typical/recurring incidents (often tied to known errors): standardized routing and handling, automated resolution ("self-healing") with runbooks, model-specific SLAs, tasks and communications; models also define third-party involvement. Automated incidents are applied, tested, confirmed, auto-updated and auto-closed with a report to the responsible team. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Diagnosis, escalation and team structures** — functional escalation between teams; tiered (L1/L2/L3) or flat/self-organizing teams; ITIL suggests replacing L1→L2 escalation with pairing and L3 inter-team collaboration to reduce reassignments; grouping by technical domain, product/service, territory or consumer type. Tool: reassignment with mandatory reason, assist/pair requests, expert finder, reassignment counters and ping-pong alerts. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §4.2]
- **Swarming workspace** — start a swarm on a complex or major incident: auto-create a chat/bridge, invite skill-matched experts (from workforce skills), shared hypothesis and experiment log (safe-to-fail experiments), decision log, task split, return to single ownership when competencies are clear. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; CDS]
- **Major incident management** — model content per ITIL: criteria distinguishing major incidents from disasters and other incidents; accountable coordinator (MIM); dedicated temporary team; dedicated resources including budget (urgent third-party consultation, procurement); special investigation methods (swarming); agreed communication model for users, customers, regulators, media and other stakeholders; agreed review and follow-up procedure. Tool: MI candidate → proposed → accepted flow, MI dashboard/war room, conference bridge, communication plan with cadence and templates, status page and stakeholder subscriptions, executive summary, timeline, post-incident review record with actions, automatic problem creation. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §2.2]
- **Resolution and closure** — specialists apply solutions sequentially or in parallel; a solution may require a change (create change from incident, pre-filled); closure procedures may include user confirmation of restoration, resolution cost calculation and reporting, resolution price calculation and invoicing, problem investigation initiation and incident review; who formally closes (product owner, service owner, incident manager or service desk agent) depends on the incident model. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Workaround and technical-debt tracking** — record the workaround used, link to the known error, count incidents resolved by workaround per known error → feeds problem prioritization and the technical-debt register. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Periodic incident review** — scheduled review sessions sampling major incidents, incidents not resolved in time, new incident types or all incidents of a period; outputs: model updates (through change enablement when models are under change control), improvement initiatives in the CIR, communications to affected teams, review reports. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §3.2.2]
- **Supplier collaboration** — incident models define third-party involvement; standard interfaces (data-exchange rules, tools, processes) with suppliers in multi-vendor environments; supplier ticket references, supplier-pending SLA pause, supplier UC timers. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §6]
- **Satisfaction capture** — survey on resolution/closure; user satisfaction with incident handling is an official key metric. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Automation map (PG Table 5.2)** — detection: monitoring tools and event-correlation engines (impact high); registration: user-query management, workflow and collaboration tools (high); classification: workflow, collaboration, knowledge, configuration tools and ML-based classification engines (very high when volumes are high); diagnosis: analysis/investigation, knowledge, configuration and collaboration tools (high); resolution: remote administration, automated deployment, collaboration (high, especially for remote locations); closure: workflow and collaboration (medium); review: collaboration, analytics/reporting and survey tools (medium–high); model improvement initiation: workflow and backlog tools (low–medium); model update communication: communication and collaboration systems (medium–high). [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **KPIs (official key metrics, with formulas)** — PSF "detect early": time between occurrence and detection = avg(detected_at − first_symptom_at); % detected via monitoring = count(source=monitoring)/count(all). PSF "resolve quickly and efficiently": time to acceptance for diagnosis = avg(accepted_at − detected_at); diagnosis time = avg(diagnosis_end − diagnosis_start); reassignments per incident = Σreassignments/count; % waiting time = Σtime in waiting states / Σtotal handling time; first-time resolution rate = resolved without reassignment (or at first contact)/resolved; meeting agreed resolution time = resolved within target/resolved; user satisfaction = avg CSAT or % satisfied; % resolved automatically = auto_resolved/resolved; % resolved before reported by users = resolved_before_user_report/resolved. PSF "continually improve": % resolutions using previously recorded solutions = resolved with KB/KE link/resolved; % resolved using incident models; improvement of key indicators over time (trend slope); balance between speed and effectiveness (e.g., MTTR vs reopen rate). Industry additions: MTTA, MTTR/MTRS, backlog size and age, reopen rate = reopened/resolved, incidents per service/CI/user, major-incident count and duration, cost per incident = Σ(effort × rate)/count. Metrics must be sliced by incident type, service, group and period. [ITIL 4 Incident PG Table 2.2; industry]
- **AI uses named by ITIL v5 material** — AI detection, auto-classification, suggested resolution; incident summaries and stakeholder updates (creation/communication), correlation and noise reduction (curation/cognition), severity/root-cause prediction (cognition), assignment and runbook triggering (coordination) — each with governance questions: confidence thresholds, audit trails, explainability in post-incident reviews, review before publishing AI-written updates, autonomous vs approved actions and rollback. [ITIL v5 via incident.io/PeopleCert community] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Recommended states (ITIL does not mandate states): New → Assigned → In Progress ⇄ On Hold (substates: awaiting user, supplier, change, problem, evidence) → Resolved → Closed; Cancelled from New/Assigned; Resolved → In Progress on user rejection or reopen within N days; Closed is terminal (a recurrence opens a new linked incident). Major-incident overlay: none → candidate → proposed → accepted → resolved → PIR complete.
- Store all timestamps needed for the official metrics (first_symptom_at, last_good_at, detected_at, registered_at, accepted_at, diagnosis_start/end, resolved_at, closed_at); derive waiting time from state history.
- Priority matrix table keyed by (impact, urgency, optional service tier) → priority + targets; overrides require reason and are audited.
- ML classification: text + metadata classifier trained on resolved incidents (category, service, CI, group); show top-3 suggestions with confidence (part-automatic); auto-apply only above a governed threshold; log accept/override as training feedback; retrain on drift.
- Correlation: cluster open incidents by CI/service/time window/text similarity → propose parent/child and major-incident candidates; link to recent changes on the same CIs ("caused by change" suggestions).
- Auto-closure: Resolved → Closed after N business days if no rejection; satisfaction survey sent at Resolved.

## Module: problem

### Features
- **Practice card: Problem management** — Purpose: "to reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes of incidents, and managing workarounds and known errors." Three phases: problem identification (reactive and proactive), problem control (analysis, documenting workarounds and known errors, prioritization by risk), error control (managing known errors, identifying potential permanent solutions, raising change requests, periodically re-assessing known errors and workaround effectiveness). PSFs: "Identify and understand the problems and their impact on services"; "Optimize problem resolution and mitigation" (not every problem needs removal — balance cost, risk and impact on service quality). [ITIL 4 Foundation; Problem PG via CDS study guide]
- **Definitions** — Problem: "A cause, or potential cause, of one or more incidents." Known error: "A problem that has been analysed but has not been resolved." Workaround: as in incident. [ITIL 4]
- **Practice metrics, roles and models (practice guide, via secondary)** — problem model = a repeatable approach for a particular type of problem; roles: problem manager (coordinates activities, prioritizes the problem backlog), problem coordinator (supports investigation and communication); processes: proactive identification, reactive identification, problem control (investigate root cause, document and communicate the known error and workaround), error control (design a permanent fix, raise a change request, monitor known errors and update workarounds, close after verified resolution). Metrics: incidents without a known error; problems identified; incidents requiring urgent problem investigation; incidents prevented by problem resolution (proactive value); incidents resolved by problem investigation (reactive value); open known errors (backlog). Recommendations: publish a "top business problems" list prioritized by business value; use swarming; SLAs do not apply directly to problems; gather input from users and customers; use AI/automation for pattern detection, automated RCA, trend prediction and knowledge linking. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)] [AI]
- **Proactive problem identification process** — inputs: error information from vendors and suppliers; potential errors submitted by specialist teams, by external user and professional communities, and by users; monitoring data; service configuration data. Activities: review of submitted information (not duplicating an existing problem, applicable to the organization), problem registration (or rejection with notification to the submitter), initial categorization and assignment to the specialist group responsible for the presumed CI/service/product. Outputs: problem records, feedback to the problem initiator. Treated as risk management: focus on key systems and components with the highest impact; assess probability and impact of identified vulnerabilities. [ITIL 4 Problem PG Table 3.1 via CDS]
- **Reactive problem identification process** — triggered by an ongoing incident with significant impact, by multiple incidents, after incident resolution, or by incident-record analysis (many similar incidents, major incidents, poor availability). Inputs: information about ongoing incidents, incident records and reports, monitoring data, configuration data, SLAs. Problem registration captures description, associated CIs, estimated impact and probability of incidents, associated and potentially affected services, impact on the organization and customers. Techniques: statistical, impact and trend analysis to identify common causes. [ITIL 4 Problem PG Table 3.2 via CDS]
- **Problem record fields** — number, title, description, source (reactive incident, incident trend, major incident, proactive: vendor advisory, monitoring, audit, community, user), category, services, CIs, impact, probability/likelihood, risk score and priority, owner and assignment group, state, root cause (narrative + cause code + contributing factors: people/process/technology/supplier/change), RCA method, workaround (text + effectiveness rating), known-error flag and date, permanent-fix proposal, related incidents (count rolled up), related changes (fix change, caused-by change), cost of related incidents (technical-debt indicator), target dates, review date, closure code (fixed, workaround accepted, risk accepted, duplicate, cannot reproduce, no further action). [industry]
- **Known error records / KEDB** — symptoms, root cause (if known), workaround, affected CIs/services, status (active, fix in progress, fixed, retired), linked knowledge article for agents and self-service, linked change, incident count since publication; incidents can be matched and linked to known errors during classification. [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Problem control and prioritization** — risk-based ranking (likelihood × impact, frequency of related incidents, workaround cost, business criticality); decision to fix, mitigate or accept risk, with risk acceptance approved by the service/risk owner and logged in the risk register. [ITIL 4]
- **Error control** — evaluate permanent-solution options, raise change requests (pre-filled from the problem), track fix delivery through release/deployment, verify recurrence has stopped, re-assess workarounds periodically, retire known errors. [ITIL 4]
- **Root-cause analysis workspace** — templates for 5 Whys, Ishikawa/fishbone, Kepner-Tregoe, fault-tree, timeline analysis (imported from incident timeline), change-correlation, Pareto; hypothesis/evidence list; action items with owners and due dates; blameless review culture (no-blame, collective responsibility). [industry; ITIL 4 Incident PG culture guidance; HVIT]
- **Trend and pattern analysis** — recurring incidents by CI, service, category, location, time; Pareto charts; "problem candidate" queue fed by rules and ML clustering. [ITIL 4 PG] [AI when clustering is ML-based]
- **Integration points** — incident ↔ problem (M:N), problem ↔ known error ↔ knowledge, problem → change (fix), problem ↔ risk (accepted risks), vendor advisories → proactive problems, vulnerability findings → proactive problems, problem actions → CIR. [ITIL 4]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — % problems identified proactively = proactive/total; mean time to identify root cause = avg(rca_completed_at − registered_at); mean time to known error = avg(ke_published_at − registered_at); % problems with documented workaround; % problems resolved within target; recurring-incident rate = incidents linked to known errors/total incidents; incident reduction after fix = (incidents in N days before fix − after)/before; open problem backlog and age; % known errors published to the knowledge base; technical debt index = Σ(active known errors × related incident volume or cost). [industry; derived from PSFs]
- **AI** — pattern detection and problem-candidate suggestion; automated root-cause hypotheses from incident clusters, change history and topology; draft known-error articles; similar-problem detection (v5 examples: pattern detection, automated root cause analysis). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Recommended states: New → Assessed (accepted or rejected with feedback to initiator) → Root Cause Analysis → Known Error (workaround documented; may precede full RCA) → Fix in Progress (change linked) → Resolved (fix deployed) → Closed (verified, risk accepted, duplicate, cancelled). Known-error status kept as its own flag/record because ITIL defines a known error as "analysed but not resolved".
- Problem-candidate rule: ≥ N incidents with the same CI + category within X days, or a major incident, or ML cluster above a size/density threshold; deduplicate against open problems.
- Vendor/vulnerability advisory ingestion keyed by advisory/CVE id + affected product; match to CMDB software CIs to decide applicability before registration.
- Roll-up counters via triggers on the incident–problem link table; recompute technical-debt score nightly.

## Module: change

### Features
- **Practice card: Change enablement** — Purpose: "to maximize the number of successful service and product changes by ensuring that risks have been properly assessed, authorizing changes to proceed, and managing the change schedule." Change: "The addition, modification, or removal of anything that could have a direct or indirect effect on services." Scope is decided per organization (typically IT infrastructure, applications, documentation, processes, supplier relationships). Processes: change enablement practice planning and optimization; change lifecycle management. PSFs (per secondary sources, wording unverified): ensuring changes are realized in a timely and effective manner; minimizing the negative impact of changes; ensuring stakeholder satisfaction with changes; meeting change-related governance and compliance requirements. ITIL 4 separates technical change enablement from organizational change management (people side). [ITIL 4 Foundation; PIC course; Change PG via secondary]
- **Change enablement processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — the four PSFs above are confirmed by a second secondary source (ITIL v5 Compass). (a) *Change enablement practice planning and optimization*: initiation (establish/review scope, policies and procedures) → review and planning (review planned changes, assess dependencies, coordinate scheduling) → change model and procedure improvement → communication of procedural changes. (b) *Change lifecycle management*: registration → assessment (risk, impact, resources, dependencies) → authorization (by the change authority defined for the model) → planning (steps, timing, rollback) → realization control (monitor implementation against the plan) → review and closure (outcome, lessons learned). Change model = "a repeatable approach for a change type, defining steps, roles, timescales and escalation". Normal changes may be of unknown, medium or high risk; emergency changes require post-implementation review. Roles: change manager (coordinates the process and the schedule), change coordinator (supports assessment and planning), change authority. Practice metrics: stakeholder satisfaction with change outcomes and timeliness; change success rate; time-performance index of change (time/cost/quality); realization time per change model; change-related incidents; changes as error sources; satisfaction with procedures; compliance ratio (compliant vs non-compliant changes); compliance incidents caused by changes. Recommendations: delegate authority (not every change needs executive approval); automate extensively (orchestration for standard changes); remove irrelevant bureaucracy; assess thoroughly before authorization. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Change types** — Standard: low-risk, pre-authorized, well understood, fully documented, implemented without further authorization; often initiated as service requests; risk assessed when the standard change procedure is created, not per execution (re-assessed if anything changes). Normal: scheduled, assessed and authorized following a process; change models based on the type determine roles for assessment and authorization; low-risk normal changes can be authorized by someone empowered to decide quickly, often via automation; initiation may be automated (e.g., by a CI/CD pipeline). Emergency: implemented as soon as possible (resolve an incident, apply a security patch); not typically in the change schedule; expedited assessment and authorization; may use a separate change authority (small group of senior managers); documentation may be deferred; reduced testing accepted in some cases. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Change authority** — "a person or group responsible for authorizing a change"; ITIL 4 stresses assigning the right authority per change type/model and decentralizing authority (peer review, team leads, product owners) in Agile/DevOps contexts; CAB as an advisory forum, emergency CAB (ECAB) for emergencies. Tool: authority rules table (change model × risk × service criticality → authority: automation, peer reviewer, team lead, service owner, CAB, ECAB, business owner). [ITIL 4]
- **Change schedule** — "used to help plan changes, assist in communication, avoid conflicts, and assign resources"; also used after deployment to provide information for incident management, problem management and improvement planning. Tool: calendar/Gantt by service, CI, location and team; maintenance windows; blackout/freeze periods with exception approval; conflict detection (same CI, dependent CIs, overlapping windows, blackout overlap, resource clashes); published forward schedule of change for service desk and stakeholders; overlay of releases and deployments. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Change request (RFC) record** — number; type and change model; category; requester; change owner/coordinator; assignment group; short description; justification/business reason and expected outcome; affected services and CIs (from CMDB) with impact summary; risk (calculated + override with reason); impact; priority; planned start/end; actual start/end; maintenance window; implementation plan; test plan and evidence; backout/remediation plan; communication plan; expected outage/downtime; conflict status; approvals and authority; CAB meeting/agenda item; related incidents, problems, known errors, releases, deployments, pipeline runs/commits; post-implementation review (outcome: successful, successful with issues, unsuccessful, backed out; lessons learned); close code. [industry]
- **Risk and impact assessment** — risk questionnaire with weighted answers; automatic factors (CI criticality, number of dependent services from CMDB impact analysis, change window, team's historical success rate, test coverage, previous failures of similar changes); business-impact preview listing affected services, customers and SLAs; collision detection. [ITIL 4; industry] (risk prediction from history: [AI])
- **CAB management** — auto-built agenda from changes awaiting CAB, attendees and quorum, decision capture (approve, reject, defer, request more info), minutes, virtual CAB with asynchronous voting, CAB workbench calendar view. [industry]
- **Standard change catalogue** — library of pre-authorized change templates with owner, versioning, approval of the template itself, success-rate monitoring and automatic demotion to normal on repeated failure; orderable from the portal/request catalogue. [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Change tasks** — implementation, test, validation and communication tasks across groups with sequencing and dependencies; task completion gates state transitions. [industry]
- **DevOps change automation** — create and progress changes via API from CI/CD; policy-as-code gates (tests passed, peer review in VCS, security scan, error budget available) auto-approve low-risk changes; link commits, pull requests, builds and artifacts; record deployment results back on the change. [ITIL 4 HVIT; industry]
- **Post-implementation review** — mandatory for failed, emergency and high-risk changes (configurable); outcome codes; link change-induced incidents; lessons learned → CIR; feeds standard-change candidacy (repeatable successful normal changes). [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Communication of planned changes** — notifications to affected users/customers through service desk channels; ITIL notes the content, format and timing come from change enablement and release management while the service desk owns the channel. [ITIL 4 Service Desk PG via CDS]
- **Unauthorized change detection** — compare discovered CI changes (CMDB/discovery drift) with authorized changes in the window; raise audit findings or incidents. [industry; configuration control]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — change success rate = successful/completed; failed change rate = (failed + backed out)/completed; change-induced incident rate = changes linked as "caused by" to incidents/implemented changes; emergency change ratio = emergency/total; unauthorized changes detected (count); change lead time = implemented_at − created_at (by type); approval cycle time = authorized_at − submitted_at; % standard (pre-authorized) changes; schedule adherence = implemented within planned window/implemented; % PIRs completed; backlog of changes awaiting authorization; DORA: deployment frequency, lead time for changes, change failure rate, time to restore service. [PIC course (change success rate, failed change rate, lead time); DORA/HVIT]
- **AI** — risk scoring and success prediction with explanation, conflict detection, implementation/backout plan drafting, CAB briefing summaries, auto-approval recommendation for low-risk changes under confidence thresholds (v5 example: AI risk assessment in change enablement). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- State machines by type: Normal: New → Assess → Authorize → Scheduled → Implement → Review → Closed (Cancelled before Implement). Standard: New → Scheduled → Implement → Review → Closed (no authorization step). Emergency: New → Authorize (ECAB/emergency authority) → Implement → Review → Closed (documentation completion enforced in Review). Changing scope, CIs or planned dates after authorization sends the change back to Assess/Authorize.
- Risk = f(impact, likelihood) mapped to Low/Moderate/High/Very High; impact from CMDB blast radius (recursive traversal of "depends on/used by" with criticality weights); likelihood from questionnaire + historical failure rate of the same model/CI class/team.
- Conflict engine: interval-overlap index per CI and per dependent CI (depth-limited graph traversal), blackout windows and shared resources; re-run on any schedule change.
- Authority routing: rules evaluated top-down; output an approval plan (sequential/parallel groups) attached to the change.

## Module: release

### Features
- **Practice card: Release management** — Purpose: "to make new and changed services and features available for use." Release: "A version of a service or other configuration item, or a collection of configuration items, that is made available for use." A release may contain infrastructure and application components, documentation, training, updated processes or tools, and knowledge articles. In waterfall contexts releases are large and planned with deployment; in Agile/DevOps contexts releases are small and frequent and may be decoupled from deployment (components deployed but released later, e.g., via feature flags). Includes release planning, release scheduling/coordination and release review. Roles: release manager (v5 role list), release coordinator, product owner. [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5 roles]
- **Practice card: Deployment management** — Purpose: "to move new or changed hardware, software, documentation, processes, or any other component to live environments. It may also be involved in deploying components to other environments for testing or staging." Approaches: phased deployment (portions of the production environment at a time), continuous delivery (components integrated, tested and deployed when needed, frequent feedback loops), big bang (all targets at the same time), pull deployment (software placed in a controlled repository and users download when they choose). Works closely with release management and change enablement; in ITIL 4 a technical practice, in v5 a product & service practice. [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5]
- **Practice card: Service validation and testing** — Purpose: "to ensure that new or changed products and services meet defined requirements." The definition of service quality comes from customer needs, business objectives and risk; test strategy defines the overall approach; test plans per release; acceptance criteria; test types include functional, performance, security, usability, regression, user acceptance (UAT) and operational acceptance. Contributes to Build (v5) / obtain-build and design & transition (ITIL 4). [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5 lifecycle mapping]
- **Release, deployment and SVT processes (practice guides, via secondary)** — *Release management* requirements: organization-wide release approaches; releases that work within value streams and across service relationships. Processes: (a) release model development and improvement (analyse product architecture and service relationships → review/develop the approach → review/develop release models → communicate models); (b) release planning and coordination (identify the applicable model → plan the release instance → verify service components → verify release procedures → execute → verify → review). Release model = a repeatable method for a type of release; release = business decision to make functionality available, deployment = technical movement. Metrics: satisfaction with service introduction and with releases; adoption of the practice; partner/consumer alignment with release models; audit/compliance issues; successful release rate and errors; % release-related incidents; schedule adherence; release backlog throughput. *Deployment management* requirements: effective deployment approaches; effective deployment within value streams. Processes: (a) deployment model development and improvement (plan → implement → test the model → review deployments and records → initiate improvements → update and communicate); (b) deployment lifecycle management (plan → verify service components → verify target environments → execute → verify the deployment). Terms: environment ("a subset of IT infrastructure dedicated to a particular purpose"), continuous integration, continuous delivery (releasable at any time), continuous deployment (automatic to production). Roles: deployment manager, deployment practitioner. Metrics: satisfaction with change rate and lead time; adoption; partner alignment; audit issues; successful deployment rate and errors; deployment-related incidents; schedule adherence; backlog throughput. *SVT* functions: define and agree validation and testing approaches matching the organization's need for speed and quality; verify components, products and services meet agreed criteria. Processes: (a) management of the organizational approach (analyse strategy and resources → develop validation approaches → create testing models per product type → communicate guidelines → review); (b) service validation (agree acceptance criteria → verify they are met); (c) test planning and execution (analyse requests → identify testing models → plan → execute → manage exceptions → review results). Test types: functional, non-functional (performance, security, usability, accessibility, scalability), UAT, regression, integration, smoke. Roles: tester, test manager, quality specialist. Metrics: approach adherence; stakeholder satisfaction; product quality compliance; utility and warranty requirements fulfilment; design validation; satisfaction with testing capability; financial losses from missed issues. Recommendations: risk-based and shift-left testing; testable design and automation. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Release record** — name/version, type (major, minor, patch, emergency), release model, product/service, scope (features, user stories, fixes including problem/known-error fixes), release window, planned/actual dates, linked changes, deployment records per environment, build artifacts (from the artifact repository/definitive media library), release notes, training and communication items, readiness checklist, go/no-go decision and approvers, rollback plan, known errors accepted into production, status. [industry; ITIL]
- **Release lifecycle** — Planning → Build → Test (validation) → Readiness review (go/no-go) → Deploy → Early life support (hypercare) → Review → Closed; Cancelled. [industry; ITIL v3 heritage]
- **Release calendar and release trains** — releases per product on a shared calendar overlaid with the change schedule; dependency mapping between releases; capacity of release slots. [industry]
- **Deployment record** — target environment (dev/test/staging/prod), approach (phased/canary/blue-green/big-bang/pull/continuous), targets (CIs, user segments, % rollout), start/end, per-target result, pipeline run id, automated test results, rollback flag; production deployments linked to an authorized change or covered by a standard-change policy. [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Artifact repository / definitive media library** — controlled store of authorized versions and their hashes; software CIs reference approved versions; source for pull deployments and license compliance. [ITIL heritage; ITIL 4 pull deployment]
- **Feature flags and progressive delivery** — record which features are enabled for which segments, separate "deployed" from "released"; kill-switch tracking. [ITIL 4 release/deployment distinction; HVIT]
- **Test management (SVT)** — test strategy per product, test plans per release, test cases, runs and results, defects (with severity, link to known errors when released with defects), requirement ↔ test traceability, acceptance criteria evaluation, UAT sign-off approvals, test-environment booking, test data management. [ITIL 4 SVT]
- **Early life support** — elevated monitoring and priority routing for incidents on newly released components, defined exit criteria (incident rate, SLA attainment), handover to operations. [industry; ITIL heritage]
- **Known errors on release** — defects accepted at go-live are registered as known errors with workarounds so the service desk can handle related incidents. [ITIL heritage]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — release success rate = releases without rollback or P1/P2 incident within N days/total; on-schedule release rate; escaped defect rate = defects found in production/total defects; rollback rate = deployments rolled back/deployments; deployment frequency; lead time from code commit to production; mean deployment duration; test coverage % (requirements with passing tests/requirements); UAT first-pass rate; incidents during early life per release. [industry; DORA]
- **AI** — release-note generation from linked stories/changes, test-case generation, deployment-risk prediction, test-failure triage (v5 examples: AI-assisted coding and testing in Build; risk assessment and deployment optimization in Transition). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Data model: Release 1–n Change; Release 1–n Deployment (per environment) 1–n Deployment target (CI); release state derived from children plus go/no-go gate.
- CI/CD webhooks create deployment records automatically; production deployment without authorized change triggers a policy violation (block or flag).
- Traceability matrix (requirement × test × defect × release) supports go/no-go automation (all must-have requirements have passing tests; no open critical defects).

## Module: request

### Features
- **Practice card: Service request management** — Purpose: "to support the agreed quality of a service by handling all pre-defined, user-initiated service requests in an effective and user-friendly manner." Service request: "a request from a user or a user's authorized representative that initiates a service action which has been agreed as a normal part of service delivery." Types: request for a service delivery action (provide a report, replace a toner cartridge); request for information (how to create a document, office hours); request for provision of a resource or service (phone or laptop for a user, virtual server for a team); request for access to a resource or service (access to a file or folder); feedback, compliments and complaints. Guidance: service requests are a normal part of service delivery, not failures; requests and fulfilment should be standardized and automated to the greatest degree possible; policies define which requests need limited or no further approval; users' expectations of fulfilment times must be clearly set based on what the organization can realistically deliver; opportunities for improvement must be identified and implemented to produce faster fulfilment and more automation; some requests need approvals (financial, information security), some none; fulfilment can include standard changes. PSFs (paraphrased by a secondary v5 study site): (1) ensuring service request fulfilment procedures are optimized for all services; (2) ensuring all requests are fulfilled according to agreed procedures and to users' satisfaction. Roles: request initiator, fulfiller groups, service desk agent, approvers, catalogue/request-model owner (no dedicated "request manager" is required because fulfilment is distributed). [ITIL 4 Foundation; SRM PG via ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **SRM processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Service request fulfilment control*: categorize the request and identify the matching request model → initiate the model and control it through its predefined steps → requests with no model go to *ad hoc fulfilment control* → review fulfilment (done correctly, user satisfied). (b) *Service request review and optimization*: analyse request records and reports for trends and improvement opportunities → propose request-model improvements → communicate model updates to stakeholders. Common request types: access, information, standard changes, equipment, service subscriptions, move/add/change (MAC). Recommendations: start with the most popular, high-volume, well-understood requests; include feedback capture in every request model; use monitoring & event data to trigger maintenance-type requests automatically. Tool categories named: workflow management and collaboration, monitoring and event management, publishing, social media, analysis and reporting, work planning and prioritization. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Request catalogue (request models)** — each catalogue item is a request model: user-facing name and description, category, audience/entitlement (who may see/order), dynamic form (variables, validation, conditional logic, reference lookups), quantity, price/cost, expected delivery time, approval policy, fulfilment workflow and task templates, fulfilment group, SLA/OLA, linked service offering, linked standard-change template, related knowledge, bundles/order guides (e.g., "new starter" bundle), on-behalf-of ordering, cart/checkout. ITIL notes a request catalogue is often a view of the service catalogue. [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Request structure** — request (order) → requested items (one per catalogue item) → fulfilment tasks (per group/step); fields: requested for/by, delivery location, due date, approval state, stage (approval → fulfilment → delivery → completed), captured variables (snapshot), cost, SLA, closure code (fulfilled, partially fulfilled, rejected, cancelled). [industry]
- **Approval policies** — none/automatic, line manager, cost-center/budget owner above thresholds, resource/data owner (access requests), information security, multi-step and parallel; delegation and out-of-office; rejection reasons visible to requester. [ITIL 4]
- **Access requests** — entitlement catalogue (roles, groups, applications, data sets), owner approval, provisioning (automated via identity system or manual task), time-bound access with auto-expiry, revocation on offboarding, periodic recertification (link to security/access). [ITIL 4 SRM + information security]
- **Fulfilment automation** — integrations with identity (AD/Entra/LDAP), HR, procurement, cloud provisioning, endpoint management, RPA; zero-touch fulfilment for standard items; automated status updates; fall-back manual tasks. [ITIL 4 "standardized and automated"]
- **Feedback, compliments and complaints** — dedicated request types routed to relationship managers or service owners, with trend reporting and links to improvement actions. [ITIL 4]
- **Expectation management** — expected delivery time shown at order, proactive status notifications, "my requests" tracking with stage visualization, due-date breach warnings. [ITIL 4]
- **Request model review** — periodic analysis of item usage, fulfilment time, rework, automation potential, retirement of unused items. [ITIL 4 improvement guidance]
- **Practice-guide key metrics (via secondary)** — catalogue completeness (share of common request types covered by a model); unsupported requests (requests with no predefined model, i.e., handled ad hoc); fulfilment-team satisfaction with model instructions; average fulfilment time and cost by type; % automated fulfilment; % fulfilled within SLA; incidents caused by incorrect fulfilment; user satisfaction with fulfilment; requests fulfilled with procedural deviations. Tool: an "ad hoc / no-model" flag on requests and a deviation flag on tasks so these metrics can be computed. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — fulfilment within expectation = fulfilled ≤ committed time/fulfilled; average fulfilment time per item; zero-touch rate = fully automated fulfilments/total; self-service ordering rate = portal or virtual-agent requests/total; approval cycle time = avg(approved_at − submitted_at); rejection rate; rework/reopen rate; request backlog and age; cost per request; requester satisfaction; % catalogue items with automated fulfilment. [industry]
- **AI** — conversational ordering via virtual agent (slot filling of item variables), intent detection (request vs incident vs question), item recommendation ("people like you ordered"), approval recommendation, catalogue-item drafting from a description, fulfilment-time prediction. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Catalogue item = versioned definition (JSON Schema for variables + UI layout + workflow reference + approval policy + SLA + entitlement rules); ordered items store a snapshot of definition version and answers.
- Entitlement evaluated server-side on render and on submit (user attributes: company, department, location, role, group, cost center).
- Approval engine resolves approvers dynamically (manager_of(requested_for), owner_of(resource), group, role) and supports re-evaluation when answers change before submission.
- Keep "stage" (user-friendly progress) separate from "state" (workflow control).

## Module: knowledge

### Features
- **Practice card: Knowledge management** — Purpose: "to maintain and improve the effective, efficient, and convenient use of information and knowledge across the organization." Premises: knowledge is processed and used in every value stream; focus on discovering and providing high-quality information — available, accurate, reliable, relevant, complete, timely and compliant. PSFs: creation and maintenance of knowledge and its transfer and use across the organization; effective use of information for decision-making across the organization. Concepts: SECI model (socialization tacit→tacit, externalization tacit→explicit, combination explicit→explicit, internalization explicit→tacit), explicit vs tacit knowledge, absorptive capacity ("the organization's ability to recognize the value of new information, to embed it into an existing knowledge system, and to apply it to the achievement of business outcomes"), data–information–knowledge–wisdom. Pitfalls to design against: errors in information collection (poor data entry), no alignment/integration between internal and external sources, information lost through unstructured storage, data lost in migrations, difficult interfaces (poor search). Roles: knowledge manager (v5 role list), knowledge owner/author, reviewer/SME, KCS coach, consumers. [ITIL 4 Knowledge PG via CDS; v5 roles]
- **Knowledge processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Establishing and maintaining the KM environment*: identify KM domains and key stakeholders → analyse KM requirements and capabilities → develop the KM approach and guidelines → communicate and embed → review how KM is applied. (b) *Knowledge routines management*: identify and analyse knowledge demand → identify and review knowledge routines and assets → develop and implement routines → carry out routines → review routines and assets. Terms: knowledge asset ("an information resource that is important for the organization's operations and value co-creation"), DIKW, absorptive capacity (including discarding obsolete knowledge). Practice metrics: knowledge-culture compliance; satisfaction with KM culture; organization-wide adoption; satisfaction with informational support; information compliance (audit results); information quality (accuracy, completeness, timeliness); tool adoption and effectiveness; user satisfaction with KM tools. Tool: "knowledge domain" and "knowledge routine" objects (e.g., "every resolved P1 produces a reviewed article within 5 days", "quarterly review of known-error articles") with owners, schedules and compliance tracking. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Knowledge article record** — number, title, body (rich text, code blocks, images, video), type (how-to, FAQ, troubleshooting, known error/workaround, policy, reference, release note, decision record), knowledge base (per service, department or audience), category and tags, linked services/CIs/catalogue items, language and translations, author, owner, reviewers, version, state, valid-from/valid-to and review date, visibility (public, all employees, agents only, specific groups/companies), rating, helpful votes, views, attach-to-resolution count, flags/feedback comments, attachments. [industry; KCS]
- **Lifecycle and governance** — Draft → Review (SME/technical/compliance) → Approved → Published → Scheduled review → Retired/Archived; minor edit vs new version; owner reminders before expiry; feedback-driven update tasks; KCS v6 alternative states (Work in Progress, Not Validated, Validated, Archived) and competency licensing (Candidate, Contributor, Publisher, Coach) as an option. [industry; KCS v6 (Consortium for Service Innovation)]
- **Knowledge in the flow of work** — contextual suggestions on incident/request/problem forms from title and CI; "attach article to resolve" (counts reuse); "create article from resolution" (capture during work, KCS "solve loop"); flag article as inaccurate; known errors published as articles for agents and self-service. ITIL's incident automation table names knowledge tools in classification and diagnosis. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; KCS]
- **Search experience** — full-text and semantic search, synonyms, typo tolerance, facets, multilingual search, highlighted snippets; search analytics (top queries, zero-result queries, click-through) to find gaps. [ITIL 4 knowledge pitfalls; industry]
- **Self-service and deflection** — articles in portal and virtual agent with "did this help?" capture; deflection measurement; shift-left. [ITIL 4 CDS shift-left]
- **Tacit-knowledge capture (socialization/externalization)** — Q&A communities, expert directory, mentoring/pairing logs, recorded walkthroughs, swarm transcripts summarized into articles. [ITIL 4 SECI; industry]
- **Knowledge for decisions** — dashboards and insight notes linking data to decisions ("I know" vs "I think"); decision records. [ITIL 4 Knowledge PG]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — knowledge reuse rate = resolved records with linked article/resolved; self-service deflection = self-service sessions not followed by a ticket within N hours/sessions; search success rate = searches with a click (or answer accepted)/searches; zero-result rate; article helpfulness = helpful votes/total votes; % articles reviewed before expiry; time to publish = avg(published_at − created_at); article quality index (KCS AQI sampling); contribution rate per agent; content-gap count. [industry; KCS]
- **AI** — article generation from resolved incidents/problems/swarm chats (creation), stale/duplicate/conflicting article detection (curation), answer generation with citations over permitted content (RAG; clarification + communication), translation, semantic search, auto-tagging (v5 examples: AI-powered knowledge creation and search; flagging obsolete articles). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Versioned article table with published-version pointer; translations as child rows bound to a master version; diff view.
- Search index stores per-document ACL attributes (visibility groups, companies); filter before ranking; the same filter applies to RAG retrieval so generated answers never cite inaccessible content.
- Content-gap detection: cluster zero-result queries and resolved incidents without linked articles; create "article needed" tasks for knowledge owners.

## Module: cmdb

### Features
- **Practice card: Service configuration management** — Purpose: "to ensure that accurate and reliable information about the configuration of services, and the CIs that support them, is available when and where it is needed. This includes information on how CIs are configured and the relationships between them." Configuration item: "Any component that needs to be managed in order to deliver an IT service." Configuration management system (CMS): "A set of tools, data, and information that is used to support service configuration management." Key concepts (practitioner syllabus): configuration item, service configuration model, CI lifecycle model, CMS. Activities: collect and process configuration information (manual, discovery, other practices' records); verify and audit; provide information to other practices; configuration control (CI changes authorized through change enablement); maintain service models (how services are composed of CIs). Information may be held in one or several CMDBs and federated; the cost and effort of collecting and maintaining CI data must be balanced against the value it creates. Roles: configuration manager (v5 list), CI owners/data stewards, configuration analysts. [ITIL 4 Foundation; SCM practitioner syllabus]
- **SCM processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Managing a common approach*: analyse stakeholder requirements → define and agree scope, policies and level of detail → communicate and embed into value streams → review and adjust. (b) *Capturing, managing and providing configuration information*: analyse resources and identify CIs → confirm a CI lifecycle model → follow it from creation to retirement → handle exceptions → review the model. (c) *Verifying configuration data*: plan verification → select the lifecycle model to verify against → compare CMDB data with reality (audit) → analyse discrepancies → corrective actions → document and communicate a verification report. Terms: baseline configuration (formally reviewed and agreed), verification, inventory (manual collection) vs discovery (automated collection); CI vs IT asset (CI managed for service delivery and dependencies; asset for financial and lifecycle purposes; many items are both with different data). Roles: configuration manager/coordinator, configuration librarian (maintains the CMDB and records), resource owner (accountable for CI data accuracy in their domain). Metrics: satisfaction with configuration information and with SCM interfaces/processes; decisions affected by poor configuration information; % incorrect CMDB data; % verified CMDB data; cost of SCM; CMDB data usage rate. Recommendation: review CMDB scope and detail regularly to avoid over-engineering; involve suppliers and partners in providing data. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Practice card: Infrastructure and platform management** — Purpose: "to oversee the infrastructure and platforms used by an organization. When carried out properly, this practice enables the monitoring of the technology solutions available to the organization, including solutions from third parties." Covers physical, virtual and cloud infrastructure, networks, data centres, platforms (IaaS/PaaS/SaaS), infrastructure-as-code and platform lifecycle (end-of-life/support). Tool needs: infrastructure inventory and topology in the CMDB, cloud discovery, platform lifecycle and EOL tracking, capacity and cost views, IaC-driven change records, monitoring integration (see event). [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5 moved to product & service practices]
- **CI class model and attributes** — class hierarchy: hardware (server, storage, network device, end-user device, peripheral), software (application, software instance, database, middleware, license-relevant installation), cloud (account, region, VM, container, Kubernetes cluster/namespace, serverless function, managed service), services (business service, technical/application service, service offering), logical (environment, cluster, IP network, load balancer pool), documents, facilities; references to people, groups, locations, suppliers, contracts. Common attributes: name, class, serial, asset tag, model, manufacturer, owner, support group, managed-by group, environment, location, operational status, install status, lifecycle stage, criticality/tier, business service, cost center, discovery source, first/last discovered, sustainability attributes (power draw, carbon factor). [industry; v5 sustainability]
- **Relationships and service models** — typed, directional relationships (depends on/used by, runs on/hosts, contains/contained by, connects to, member of, provides/consumes, backs up/backed up by); service maps from business service → application service → infrastructure; dependency and impact views; dynamic service membership by tags/queries. [ITIL 4 service model; industry]
- **Discovery and data ingestion** — agent/agentless discovery (credentials vault), cloud API discovery, network discovery, service mapping (top-down, traffic/log-based), imports from monitoring, ITAM, endpoint management, virtualization and HR/facilities sources. [industry]
- **Identification and reconciliation** — per-class identification rules (serial + model, hostname + domain, cloud resource id), reconciliation rules (source precedence per attribute), staging/preview, deduplication tasks, staleness aging, health dashboard (completeness, correctness, compliance, duplicates, orphans, staleness). [industry]
- **Configuration control and baselines** — controlled CIs/attributes may change only through an authorized change; drift detection vs approved baseline; baseline snapshots for audits and PIRs; unauthorized-change alerts. [ITIL 4 configuration control; ITIL heritage baselines]
- **Verification and audit** — scheduled audits comparing CMDB vs discovery vs physical reality; attestation campaigns where CI owners certify data; audit findings as tasks. [ITIL 4]
- **CI lifecycle model** — per class: Planned → Ordered/In development → Received/Build → In test/Staging → Operational/Live → (Maintenance/Degraded) → Retired → Disposed; aligned with the asset lifecycle but tracked separately (operational vs financial view). [SCM syllabus concept; industry]
- **Consumers of CMDB data** — incident (affected CI, impact, responsible team; wrong CI associations reported back to configuration control), problem (associated CIs), change (impact analysis, conflicts, risk), event (topology correlation), SLM (service-to-CI mapping), knowledge (articles by CI), continuity (dependencies for BIA), financial (cost allocation), security (vulnerability exposure). [ITIL 4 PGs]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — completeness = CIs with all mandatory attributes/CIs; correctness = CIs passing audit/CIs audited; compliance = CIs matching approved baseline/controlled CIs; staleness = CIs not seen by discovery in N days/discoverable CIs; duplicate rate; orphan rate = CIs without relationships/CIs (for classes that require them); % incidents and changes with a valid CI; % critical services fully mapped; unauthorized changes detected per month; % wrong-CI reassignments. [industry]
- **AI** — duplicate CI detection and merge suggestions, relationship inference from traffic/logs, attribute anomaly detection, natural-language CMDB queries ("what depends on X?"), impact prediction (v5 curation example: detecting duplicate CMDB records). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Graph-in-relational model: `ci` (id, class, core columns, attrs JSONB) + `ci_rel` (parent_id, child_id, type, source); recursive CTE traversals with depth limits and cycle detection; materialized "service closure" tables refreshed on relationship change for fast impact analysis.
- Reconciliation pipeline: import → transform → identify (class rules) → reconcile (attribute precedence, timestamps) → commit with audit; never overwrite a higher-priority source with a lower one.
- Keep operational status, install status and lifecycle stage as separate fields; CI classes defined as metadata (attributes, identification rules, relationship rules, icons).

## Module: itam

### Features
- **Practice card: IT asset management** — Purpose: "to plan and manage the full lifecycle of all IT assets, to help the organization: maximize value; control costs; manage risks; support decision-making about purchase, re-use, retirement, and disposal of assets; meet regulatory and contractual requirements." IT asset: "Any financially valuable component that can contribute to the delivery of an IT product or service." Scope: hardware, software (licenses and subscriptions), cloud and SaaS, mobile, IoT; activities: define and maintain the asset register, inventory, audits, license compliance, lifecycle management from request/procurement to disposal; processes (practitioner course): managing a common approach to ITAM; managing the IT asset lifecycle and records. Related standard: ISO/IEC 19770 (SAM). Roles: IT asset manager, software asset manager, asset owner/custodian, procurement. [ITIL 4 Foundation; PIC course]
- **ITAM processes and lifecycle (practice guide, via secondary)** — processes: (a) *Managing a common approach to ITAM*: analyse stakeholder needs and asset risks → define and agree scope, policies and governance → communicate and embed in value streams → review and adjust; (b) *Managing the IT asset lifecycle and records*: identify which resources qualify as assets → verify assets and the applicable lifecycle model → track assets through each stage → handle assets outside standard models → review lifecycle effectiveness; (c) *Verifying, auditing and analysing IT assets*: plan audit scope and schedule → collect data (inventory or discovery) → check against the register → analyse discrepancies and trends → document and communicate results. Roles: IT asset manager, IT asset custodian (physically cares for assets), IT asset analyst, licence manager, IT asset owner. Metrics: satisfaction with asset information; audit findings and non-compliances; unmet asset-information requirements; ITAM coverage vs plan; ROI, VOI, TCO; effect of utilization improvements; % assets with monitored utilization. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Asset record** — asset tag, serial, model, manufacturer, class (hardware, software license, subscription, cloud, mobile, peripheral, consumable), linked CI, status/substatus, assigned user, department, cost center, location, stockroom, purchase data (PO, invoice, vendor, cost, date), warranty and support contract (expiry), lease terms, depreciation (method, useful life, salvage, current book value), lifecycle dates (received, deployed, retired, disposed), disposal method and certificate, data-sanitization evidence, compliance flags (encryption, MDM enrolled), sustainability attributes (energy rating, embodied carbon, recycled/refurbished). [industry; ITIL 4 Sustainability extension]
- **Hardware lifecycle** — On order → Received → In stock → Reserved → Deployed/In use → In repair/maintenance → In transit → Retired → Disposed/Donated/Sold/Lost/Stolen; stockrooms, transfers, reservations, loaner pools, refresh planning by age and warranty. [industry]
- **Software asset management** — software models and normalization (publisher, product, version, edition), entitlements by license metric (per device, per user, per core/processor, PVU, concurrent, site, subscription/SaaS seat), installation and usage evidence from discovery and SaaS APIs, reconciliation into a compliance position (over/under-licensed), true-up forecasts, license harvesting/reclamation of unused installs, renewal management, vendor-audit defence pack. [ITIL 4 ITAM; ISO/IEC 19770; industry]
- **Procurement and receiving** — request item → approval → purchase order → receipt → asset creation → deployment task; vendor catalogues and pricing. [industry]
- **Contracts, warranties, leases** — linked to supplier contracts; renewal alerts; coverage checks during incidents (is the device under warranty?). [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Audits and counts** — barcode/QR/RFID scanning with mobile app, physical count reconciliation, discrepancy workflow (missing, found, wrong location). [ITIL 4 audits]
- **Financial linkage** — cost allocation to cost centers/services, depreciation schedules, TCO per device/user/service, budget forecasts for refresh. [ITIL 4 "control costs"; financial]
- **Secure disposal** — wipe certificate, e-waste compliant recycler, chain of custody, environmental reporting. [ITIL 4 regulatory; Sustainability]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — register accuracy = assets verified accurate/assets audited; % assets with owner and location; license compliance gap = Σ(required − entitled) where positive, valued in currency; license utilization = used entitlements/purchased; harvest savings; % production assets out of warranty; audit discrepancy rate; average provisioning time; TCO per user; % assets disposed with certificate; e-waste recycled %. [industry]
- **AI** — software title normalization, license optimization recommendations, anomaly detection (unexpected installs, shadow SaaS), contract/entitlement extraction from PDFs, refresh forecasting from failure data. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Asset ↔ CI one-to-one link for managed classes; asset lifecycle (financial) and CI lifecycle (operational) kept separate but synchronized by rules (e.g., asset Retired → CI Retired).
- License engine: one calculator per license metric; compliance position recomputed nightly and on entitlement/installation change; snapshot history for audits.
- Depreciation job writes monthly ledger rows; book value is derived, never edited.

### Features
- **Practice card: Service level management** — Purpose: "to set clear business-based targets for service levels, and to ensure that delivery of services is properly assessed, monitored, and managed against these targets." Service level: "One or more metrics that define expected or achieved service quality." SLA: "A documented agreement between a service provider and a customer that identifies both services required and the expected level of service." Scope: tactical and operational communication with customers about expected, agreed and actual quality; negotiating, agreeing and maintaining SLAs; understanding service design, architecture and dependencies; continual review of achieved vs agreed and expected levels; initiating improvements to agreements, monitoring, reporting and performance. PSFs (verbatim): "Establishing a shared view of target service levels with customers"; "Overseeing how the organization meets the defined service levels through the collection, analysis, storage, and reporting of the relevant metrics for the identified services"; "Performing service reviews to ensure that the current set of services continues to meet the needs of the organization and its customers"; "Capturing and reporting on improvement opportunities, including performance against defined service levels and stakeholder satisfaction." Roles: service level manager, business relationship manager (v5 list), service owner, customer, sponsor. [ITIL 4 Foundation; SLM PG via CDS]
- **v5 SLM scope and processes (via secondary)** — v5 restates the purpose as setting business-based targets for **utility, warranty and experience**; XLAs are introduced alongside SLAs (SLAs alone are described as insufficient for user-facing services). Processes: (a) *SLA management lifecycle*: definition of customer requirements → viability analysis (technical and financial capability) → drafting the SLA → negotiation → communication and enablement of involved teams → review → prolongation (renewal) → withdrawal (service decommissioned or relationship ended). (b) *Oversight of service levels and quality*: customer and user satisfaction surveys; ongoing monitoring of technical and experience metrics; regular service reviews; service quality reporting. Typical SLA components: service description, service hours, measurable targets, measurement and reporting, responsibilities, escalation procedures, review schedule. Metrics: customer satisfaction with SLA content; SLAs overdue for review; customer services without an SLA; % SLAs with measurable targets; % services with regular SLA reports; % services with a monitoring dashboard; % services with regularly collected satisfaction data; satisfaction with reporting; consistency of scheduled reviews; average service quality score; improvement delivery rate. Tool: agreement records carry a lifecycle state matching these eight stages, with renewal and withdrawal reminders. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **SLA quality rules (Foundation)** — SLAs must relate to a defined service in the service catalogue; relate to defined outcomes, not only operational metrics (avoid the "watermelon SLA effect": green outside, red inside); reflect a genuine agreement between provider and consumer; be simple and easy to understand. Tool: SLA must reference a catalogue service/offering; outcome/experience measures stored alongside operational targets. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Agreement types and structures** — SLA (customer), OLA (internal teams), underpinning contract (supplier); service-based, customer-based and multi-level structures; "out-of-the-box" pre-defined service levels (gold/silver/bronze tiers) vs tailored negotiated levels. [ITIL; SLM PG via CDS]
- **Metric categories** — ITIL 4 lists functionality, availability, performance, timeliness, user support, accuracy and UX measures; v5 summaries group service-level metrics as utility, warranty, sustainability and experience. [CDS; ITIL v5 (secondary)]
- **SLA definition engine** — target type (response, resolution, fulfilment, availability %, custom metric), applicability conditions (service, priority, company, category), start/pause/stop/cancel/reset conditions, schedule (business calendar, time zone, holidays), retroactive start, warning thresholds (e.g., 50/75/90%), breach actions (notify, escalate, reassign, raise priority), multiple concurrent SLAs/OLAs/UCs per record, supplier-pending pause. [industry]
- **SLA instance tracking** — per record × definition: state (in progress, paused, achieved, breached, cancelled), elapsed and remaining business time, planned breach time, breach reason code. [industry]
- **Service reviews** — event-based (major incidents, significant change requests, changed business needs) and interval-based (typically monthly, no longer than quarterly); auto-generated review pack (achieved levels, user satisfaction, customer satisfaction, incidents/problems/changes, improvement actions, risks); minutes, decisions and actions to the CIR; review cadence tracking. [SLM PG via CDS]
- **Three views of quality** — achieved service level vs agreed, user satisfaction (service desk feedback, surveys), customer satisfaction (reviews, surveys, social media); SLM interprets data collected by monitoring, measurement and service design practices. Tool: per-service quality panel combining SLA attainment, CSAT and sentiment. [SLM PG via CDS]
- **Experience-level agreements (XLA)** — experience indicators (sentiment, time lost to IT issues, digital-experience scores, effort), experience targets and touchpoint surveys; supports v5 Experience. [ITIL 4 DSV concepts; ITIL v5 Experience; industry]
- **Reports** — attainment by service/customer/period, breach analysis with cause, trend, OLA/UC contribution to SLA breaches, service-credit/penalty calculation, scheduled distribution to customers. [industry]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — SLA attainment = targets met/targets measured; breach count and trend; % services with agreed SLAs and defined outcomes; % service reviews held on schedule; % improvement actions closed by due date; OLA and UC attainment; customer satisfaction per service; watermelon gap = SLA attainment % − experience score % (heuristic for misaligned SLAs). [industry; PSF-derived]
- **AI** — breach prediction (time-to-breach) with proactive escalation, sentiment analysis of feedback, auto-drafted service review narratives, SLA design suggestions from historical performance. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Event-driven SLA evaluation on record change + scheduler for warnings; conditions stored as rule expressions; business durations computed by the calendar service; keep definition version on each instance.
- Availability SLAs computed from outage records per service (planned downtime excluded only when the SLA says so).
- Enforce catalogue linkage (SLA → service/offering) to satisfy the ITIL rule that SLAs relate to defined services.

## Module: event

### Features
- **Practice card: Monitoring and event management** — Purpose: "to systematically observe services and service components, and record and report selected changes of state identified as events. This practice identifies and prioritizes infrastructure, services, business processes, and information security events, and establishes the appropriate response to those events, including responding to conditions that could lead to potential faults or incidents." Two parts: monitoring (detect significant changes of state and communicate them) and event management (manage identified events and initiate the right response). Definitions: Event — "Any change of state that has significance for the management of a service or other configuration item (CI)"; Monitoring — "Repeated observation of a system, practice, process, service, or other entity to detect events and to ensure that the current status is known"; Alert — "A notification that a threshold has been reached, something has changed, or a failure has occurred"; Threshold — "The value of a metric that triggers a pre-defined response". Event types by significance: informational (no action needed), warning (action before negative impact), exception (critical threshold reached: failure, significant degradation, loss of functionality). PSFs (verbatim): "Establish and maintain approaches/models that describe the various types of events and monitoring capabilities needed to detect them"; "Ensure that timely, relevant, and sufficient monitoring data is available to relevant stakeholders"; "Ensure that events are detected, interpreted, and if needed acted upon as quickly as possible." Roles: monitoring analyst (v5 list), operations bridge/NOC operator, on-call engineer, event-rule owner. [ITIL 4 Foundation; M&EM PG via CDS]
- **M&EM processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Monitoring planning*: define the monitoring objective → define what should and can be monitored (feasibility) → define event types → define thresholds per event type → define a service health model (end-to-end correlations) → define correlation rules and rule sets → define monitoring action plans → define required tool capabilities. (b) *Event handling*: detect → log (timestamp, source, context) → filter noise and correlate → classify (informational, warning, exception) → select response → notify and execute response (exceptions typically trigger incident management). (c) *M&EM review*: review major events for lessons → tune filtering and correlation rules → update service health models after infrastructure changes → optimize response procedures and automation → assess tool capabilities and gaps → analyse statistics for trends and capacity planning. Metrics: satisfaction with the approach and adherence to it; % unmet or unrealistic monitoring recommendations; satisfaction with monitoring data and presentation; monitoring data quality; impact of event-management errors; event communication noise (irrelevant alerts); incidents or problems caused by poor event management. No named role is required (responsibilities spread across infrastructure, application and service teams). Recommendation: avoid monitoring events of unknown significance; collaborate after incidents to improve monitoring. AIOps uses named: anomaly detection, event correlation across layers, predictive alerts, auto-remediation, capacity prediction. Tool: "service health model" object (service → CIs → indicators → thresholds → health rules) and "monitoring action plan" per event type, versioned and reviewed. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)] [AI]
- **Monitoring planning and coverage model** — identify and prioritize services and components to monitor based on business objectives and dependencies; balance information need, granularity and frequency (too much data produces less information); policies for event types and responses. Tool: per-service coverage map (CIs, metrics, thresholds, owners), coverage gaps report, monitoring requirements in service design. [M&EM PG via CDS]
- **Event ingestion and normalization** — connectors for SNMP traps, syslog, webhooks/APIs (Prometheus/Alertmanager, Zabbix, Nagios, Datadog, Dynatrace, New Relic, Azure Monitor, CloudWatch, Google Cloud Monitoring, Grafana, Elastic, Splunk), email; common event schema (source, node, CI, metric, type, severity, message, time, additional info, message key). [industry]
- **Event processing rules** — filter, deduplicate (message key and counters), suppress (maintenance windows from the change schedule), enrich (CMDB: CI, service, owner, support group), classify informational/warning/exception, threshold and state rules, correlate (topology, time window, rule-based, ML), create alerts, trigger automated responses (runbooks), auto-create incidents associated with the CI and predefined technical data, auto-close alerts and incidents on clear events. [ITIL 4 Incident PG detection activity; industry]
- **Alert lifecycle** — Open → Acknowledged → (incident created / remediation running) → Resolved → Closed; flapping detection, reopen, severity escalation, maintenance flag. [industry]
- **AIOps** — dynamic baselines and anomaly detection, log-pattern mining, alert clustering and noise reduction, probable root cause (topology + recent changes), predictive alerts (v5: AIOps, anomaly detection, predictive maintenance in Operate; ITIL 4 PG: automation and machine learning for data analysis). [ITIL 4 PG; ITIL v5] [AI]
- **Service health dashboards** — service status derived from CI states and dependency rules, event console, heat maps, metric charts, status page publishing; making monitoring data available to stakeholders (service provider: performing as designed?; customer: getting what I paid for?; both: who is at fault?). [M&EM PG via CDS]
- **On-call management** — rotas and schedules, escalation policies, paging (SMS, voice, push, chat), acknowledgement tracking, overrides, handoffs, alert routing by service ownership. [industry]
- **Security and business events** — SIEM/SOC integration (security events → security incidents) and business-process monitoring (e.g., failed payment rates) as event sources, both explicitly in the ITIL purpose. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Digital-experience monitoring** — synthetic transactions and endpoint telemetry feeding experience metrics/XLAs. [industry; v5 experience]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — % incidents detected by monitoring (shared with incident PSF); MTTD = avg(detected_at − occurred_at); MTTA = avg(acknowledged_at − alert_created_at); event-to-alert compression = events/alerts; alert-to-incident ratio; false-positive rate = alerts closed "no action"/alerts; % critical CIs monitored = monitored critical CIs/critical CIs; % alerts auto-remediated; noise reduction % = 1 − alerts/raw events. [industry; PSF-derived]

### Implementation notes
- Partitioned append-only event table; dedupe key = hash(source, node, metric, type); alert table holds correlation group id; retention tiers.
- Correlation: sliding time windows + CMDB topology traversal (parent outage suppresses child alerts) + change-window suppression.
- Bi-directional alert ↔ incident sync (resolving one updates the other) with loop protection.

## Module: continuity

### Features
- **Practice card: Availability management** — Purpose: "to ensure that services deliver agreed levels of availability to meet the needs of customers and users." Availability: "the ability of an IT service or other configuration item to perform its agreed function when required." Activities: negotiate and agree achievable targets; design infrastructure and applications to deliver required availability; ensure services and components can collect availability data; monitor, analyse and report; plan improvements. Key measures: MTBF (how frequently a service fails) and MTRS (how quickly service is restored). Role: availability manager (v5 list). [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Practice card: Capacity and performance management** — Purpose: "to ensure that services achieve agreed and expected performance, satisfying current and future demand in a cost-effective way." Activities: performance and capacity monitoring, analysis and tuning, demand modelling (patterns of business activity), capacity planning, application sizing. Role: capacity manager. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Practice card: Service continuity management** — Purpose: "to ensure that the availability and performance of a service are maintained at sufficient levels in case of a disaster. The practice provides a framework for building organizational resilience with the capability of producing an effective response that safeguards the interests of key stakeholders and the organization's reputation, brand, and value-creating activities." Key terms: disaster (sudden unplanned event causing great damage or serious loss), recovery time objective (RTO: maximum acceptable time after a disruption before lack of business functionality severely impacts the organization), recovery point objective (RPO: point to which information must be restored to enable the activity to operate on resumption), business impact analysis (BIA: identifies vital business functions and dependencies), business continuity plans, IT continuity and disaster recovery plans; alignment with ISO 22301. The incident PG requires criteria separating major incidents from disasters. Roles: continuity manager, plan owners, recovery teams. [ITIL 4 Foundation; Incident PG]
- **Service continuity processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — PSFs (paraphrased): develop and manage continuity plans; mitigate continuity risks; ensure awareness and readiness. Processes: (a) *Business impact analysis*: identify vital business functions (VBF: a business capability required for the business to succeed) → analyse financial, operational and reputational impact of disruption → map dependencies between VBFs and supporting services → define RTO and RPO per VBF. (b) *Developing, exercising and maintaining continuity plans*: define scope → policy and governance → strategies (hot, warm, cold standby; reciprocal arrangements) → detailed plans → awareness and exercise programme → exercises (tabletop, simulation, full tests) → audit plan effectiveness and compliance. (c) *Response and recovery*: formal invocation when a disaster is declared → plan execution to restore services. Metrics: % products/services with documented continuity requirements and plans; plan-update timeliness; RTO achievement; RPO achievement; effectiveness of continuity measures; actual vs expected loss; training/awareness coverage; % plans tested. Recommendations: start with the most critical services; keep plans simple enough to follow under stress; automate response and recovery; include suppliers in the recovery chain. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Outage and availability tracking** — outage records (start/end, unplanned/planned/degraded, service and CIs, cause, linked incident/change), availability % per service and period with SLA-defined exclusions, MTBF/MTRS/MTBSI, composite availability via dependencies, availability SLA attainment. [ITIL; industry]
- **Availability and resilience analysis** — single-point-of-failure detection from CMDB (non-redundant dependencies of critical services), component failure impact analysis, fault-tree templates, availability plans with improvement actions. [ITIL heritage; industry]
- **Capacity monitoring and forecasting** — utilization trends per CI/service, thresholds, saturation-date forecasts, what-if modelling on business growth drivers, cloud right-sizing, performance baselines, capacity plan items and demand calendar. [ITIL 4; industry] [AI when ML forecasting]
- **Business impact analysis** — business functions/processes, impact over time (financial, operational, legal/regulatory, reputational), maximum tolerable period of disruption, RTO, RPO, minimum business continuity objective, dependencies (services, CIs, suppliers, people, sites, data), criticality tiers. [ITIL 4; ISO 22301]
- **Continuity and DR plans** — per service/site: recovery strategy (active-active, hot/warm/cold standby, cloud DR, manual workaround), recovery runbook steps, roles and call trees, contacts, invocation criteria, return-to-normal procedure; versioning, approval, offline export. [ITIL 4; industry]
- **Exercises and tests** — schedule tabletop, walkthrough, simulation and full failover tests; record achieved recovery time/point vs RTO/RPO; issues and actions to the CIR. [ITIL 4; ISO 22301]
- **Plan invocation (crisis mode)** — declare disaster against criteria, activate plan, instantiate task checklist with timers vs RTO, mass notification, status board, decision log, stand-down and review. [ITIL 4]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — availability % = (agreed service time − downtime)/agreed service time × 100; MTBF = available time/number of failures; MTRS = total downtime/number of failures; MTBSI = MTBF + MTRS; % services meeting availability targets; SPOFs on critical services; % CIs above utilization threshold; capacity-forecast accuracy; capacity-related incidents; performance SLA attainment; % critical services with a plan tested in the last 12 months; test success rate (RTO and RPO achieved); BIA coverage %; plan review currency. [ITIL; industry]

### Implementation notes
- Composite availability: serial dependencies A = ΠAᵢ; redundant (parallel) components A = 1 − Π(1 − Aᵢ); compute from the service model graph.
- Outages derived from major incidents and exception alerts, editable with audit; availability uses the agreed service-time calendar.
- Forecasting with seasonal time-series models per metric; saturation date = first forecast point crossing threshold (with confidence band).
- Continuity plans are structured documents plus task templates instantiated on invocation; store contact data so it can be exported for offline use.

## Module: security

### Features
- **Practice card: Information security management** — Purpose: "to protect the information needed by the organization to conduct its business. This includes understanding and managing risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information, as well as other aspects of information security such as authentication (ensuring someone is who they claim to be) and non-repudiation (ensuring that someone can't deny that they took an action)." Balance of prevention, detection and correction; policies, processes, behaviours, risk management, controls, audits, security incidents, vulnerability and identity/access management; ISO/IEC 27001/27002 alignment. Moved to product & service practices in v5. Roles: CISO, information security manager (v5 list), security analyst, data owner. [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5]
- **Practice card: Risk management** — Purpose: "to ensure that the organization understands and effectively handles risks. Managing risk is essential to ensuring the ongoing sustainability of an organization and creating value for its customers. Risk management is an integral part of all organizational activities and therefore central to the organization's SVS." Risk: "a possible event that could cause harm or loss, or make it more difficult to achieve objectives"; can also mean uncertainty of outcome (positive and negative). Activities: identify, assess (likelihood, impact), treat (avoid, reduce, transfer/share, accept), monitor, report; risk appetite and tolerance; ISO 31000 alignment. Roles: risk owner, risk manager, risk and compliance (GRC) manager (v5 Appendix C). [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5]
- **Information security processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Planning and implementation*: analyse strategy and context → define and agree policies → risk assessment → define and agree controls and plans → communicate policies and implement controls. (b) *Controls tests and exercises*: plan → prepare → perform → review. (c) *Security incident management*: detect and assign → triage and analyse → contain and recover. (d) *Assessment and review*: identify changes in business, technology or threat environment → identify missing controls → assess control effectiveness → assessment report. Terms: confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, non-repudiation, threat ("any potential event that could have a negative impact on an asset"), threat actor, vulnerability. Metrics: % products/services with documented security requirements; % with security plans; timeliness of plan updates; security risks analysed and evaluated; risks mitigated to acceptable levels; % security plans tested; improvement actions from plan tests; governing-body discussion of security; value streams with defined security steps; practices with security in process flows or roles. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Risk management processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Governance of risk management*: monitor the organization and environment for changes affecting risk → assess significance → document risk capacity and appetite thresholds → document risk policy and framework → management direction on priorities. (b) *Risk identification, analysis and treatment*: identify risks across the four dimensions → analyse and evaluate likelihood and impact against appetite → treat (avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept). (c) *Risk monitoring and review*: assess and test controls; formal risk audits (risk landscape, register accuracy, treatment effectiveness). Terms: risk appetite (amount and type of risk the organization is willing to pursue), risk capacity (maximum risk it can bear, e.g., regulatory limits), control; risks include opportunities. Metrics: time since last appetite/capacity review; % strategic risks fully documented; % employees who feel safe reporting risks (survey); risks reported by non-risk roles; % risks with likelihood, impact and owner; % risks with treatment plan and next action date; % risks reviewed in last 6 months; % controls reviewed/audited in last 6 months. Tool: risk "reported by" and "reporter role" fields to compute risk-culture metrics; appetite and capacity thresholds per risk category. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Security incident management** — restricted-visibility record type; classification (malware, phishing, unauthorized access, data breach, DoS, insider), severity, affected assets/data/people, containment → eradication → recovery → lessons learned steps, evidence and chain of custody, regulatory notification deadlines (e.g., GDPR 72 hours), playbooks, link to related IT incidents and problems. [ITIL 4 infosec; industry]
- **Vulnerability management** — scanner ingestion (CVE, CVSS, asset/CI), risk-based prioritization (asset criticality, exposure, exploitability), remediation tasks and changes, SLAs by severity, exceptions with approval and expiry; vendor vulnerability information is also a proactive-problem input. [ITIL 4 Problem PG; industry]
- **Policy and control library** — versioned policies with approval and staff attestation; control objectives and controls mapped to multiple frameworks (ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A, ISO/IEC 20000-1, NIST CSF, SOC 2, COBIT, ISO/IEC 42001 for AI); control owners, test procedures, evidence, results ("test once, comply many"). [ITIL 4 governance/DPI; v5 AI governance]
- **Risk register** — id, title, cause–event–consequence, category (strategic, operational, financial, compliance, security, supplier, project, AI), owner, inherent likelihood/impact, existing controls and effectiveness, residual likelihood/impact, rating on a heat map, appetite threshold, treatment decision and plan, actions, review date, status (identified → assessed → treatment planned → treating → monitoring → closed); links to services, CIs, suppliers, changes, problems (accepted risks), projects. [ITIL 4; ISO 31000]
- **Compliance and audit management** — regulatory/requirements register (GDPR, HIPAA, SOX, NIS2, DORA (EU financial), EU AI Act), audit plans and engagements, findings, corrective and preventive actions, continuous control monitoring through integrations. [ITIL 4 DPI GRC; industry]
- **Data protection** — data classification on records/fields (public, internal, confidential, restricted), PII masking, encryption of sensitive fields, retention and deletion schedules, data-subject requests. [ITIL 4 PESTLE legal; industry]
- **Security embedded in other practices** — access requests (SRM), security review of changes, security events (M&EM), secure configuration baselines (CMDB), supplier security assessments, security requirements in service design. [ITIL 4]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — security incidents by severity; MTTD/MTTR for security incidents; % critical vulnerabilities remediated within SLA; mean vulnerability age; % controls tested effective; open/overdue audit findings; % staff completing security training; risks above appetite; % risk actions completed on time; phishing-simulation click rate. [industry]
- **AI** — security-incident summarization, vulnerability prioritization, risk identification from free text, control-evidence review, policy question answering; AI systems themselves registered as risks/controls under ISO/IEC 42001. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Security incidents and HR-like sensitive cases live in a separate visibility domain: default deny, explicit membership, field-level encryption for notes, access logging of every read.
- Risk score = likelihood (1–5) × impact (1–5) with configurable matrices and appetite thresholds; residual derived from control effectiveness ratings.
- Requirement ↔ control ↔ evidence as many-to-many tables so one control satisfies multiple frameworks.

### Features

Key activities: creating a single point of visibility and control; maintaining a supplier strategy, policy and contract-management information; negotiating and agreeing contracts and arrangements; managing relationships and contracts with internal and external suppliers; managing supplier performance. Processes: (a) *Managing the common approach*: develop and agree the sourcing strategy → develop and agree supplier management procedures → communicate and embed → review and adjust. (b) *Managing supplier journeys*: identify available suppliers → engage suppliers and communicate demand → evaluate and select → contract → onboard and offboard → manage consumption and monitor performance → assess and review. RfX types: RFI (non-binding information), RFP (proposal against requirements), RFQ (prices for standard goods/services), RFB (competitive bids), RFD (demonstration/trial). Roles: supplier manager, supplier coordinator. Metrics: strategic goals failed due to third-party dependencies; alignment of sourcing with strategy; internal vs third-party cost comparison; satisfaction with third-party relationships and integration; regulatory breaches and impact; value realized from suppliers; losses or delays from poor third-party integration; incidents and SLA breaches involving third parties. Tool: supplier-journey stage on each supplier record, RfX objects with weighted scoring, and third-party attribution on incidents/breaches. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Supplier record** — name, category and segmentation (strategic, tactical, operational, commodity), criticality, contacts and escalation matrix, products/services supplied, contracts, supported CIs/assets, risk rating, financial health, certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2, ISO 14001), ESG/sustainability score, performance score, relationship owner, lifecycle status (prospective → due diligence → onboarding → active → under review → exit → inactive). [industry; v5 sustainability]
- **Contract record** — number, type (underpinning contract, MSA, SOW, license, subscription, lease, maintenance, cloud), parties, dates, renewal terms and notice period, value and payment schedule, SLAs/KPIs (UC targets), credits/penalties, obligations, termination and exit clauses, documents, approval workflow, renewal and notice alerts. [ITIL; industry]
- **Performance management** — scorecards combining UC timer attainment from incidents/requests/changes assigned to the supplier, quality, delivery, responsiveness, innovation and satisfaction; periodic supplier reviews; improvement plans; disputes. [ITIL 4]
- **Supplier participation in workflows** — supplier groups as assignees, supplier portal with restricted access to assigned records, e-bonding (bi-directional ticket exchange) using "defined standard interfaces" with data-exchange rules, vendor ticket ids, supplier-pending SLA pause. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §6; SIAM]
- **Risk, due diligence and exit** — onboarding questionnaires, security and privacy assessments, concentration risk, exit plans; cloud-service acquisition lifecycle (ITIL 4 Acquiring & Managing Cloud Services extension). [ITIL 4 extension]
- **Sourcing events** — RFI/RFP/RFQ, weighted evaluation, award, contract creation. [industry]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — UC attainment = supplier targets met/measured; supplier-caused incidents per period; % suppliers with current contract and SLA; renewals handled before notice date %; savings achieved; supplier performance score trend; % critical suppliers with risk assessment and exit plan. [industry]
- **AI** — contract summarization and clause/obligation extraction, renewal-risk alerts, supplier performance insights, invoice anomaly detection. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Supplier users authenticate into a restricted "external" persona: record-level rules grant access only to records assigned to their supplier group; no access to CMDB beyond referenced CIs.
- UC timers are additional SLA instances on the same record with conditions "assigned to supplier X".

## Module: financial

### Features
- **Practice card: Service financial management** — Purpose: "to support the organization's strategies and plans for service management by ensuring that the organization's financial resources and investments are being used effectively." Activities: budgeting and costing, accounting, charging (chargeback/showback), financial planning and forecasting, investment appraisal (ROI, NPV, payback), cost models (direct/indirect, fixed/variable, CapEx/OpEx), cost per service/unit and pricing. Moved to product & service practices in v5. Roles: IT financial manager, budget holder, sponsor (authorizes budget), service owner (cost accountability). [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5]
- **Cost model and allocation** — cost pools (labour, hardware, software, cloud, facilities, external services), allocation drivers (usage, users, tickets, CIs, storage), allocation to services, products, consumers; optional TBM taxonomy. [industry]
- **Budgets and forecasts** — per cost center, service and project; actuals imported from ERP; variance analysis; rolling forecasts; approval workflow. [industry]
- **Chargeback/showback** — rate cards per offering/unit, billing runs, statements, disputes; the incident PG lists "resolution costs calculation and reporting" and "resolution price calculation and invoicing" as closure procedures. [ITIL 4 Incident PG]
- **Effort and time tracking** — time entries on incidents, requests, changes, problems and project tasks → labour cost per record, per service, per customer. [industry]
- **Cloud financial management** — cost ingestion per account/tag, tagging compliance, anomaly alerts, rightsizing and commitment recommendations. [industry]
- **Business cases and benefits** — investment appraisal for demands and projects, benefits register and realization tracking (shared with project/portfolio). [ITIL 4]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — cost per service, per user, per ticket = allocated cost/units; budget variance % = (actual − budget)/budget; % costs allocated to services; forecast accuracy = 1 − |forecast − actual|/actual; chargeback recovery % = recovered/allocated; cloud waste % = idle or unattached spend/total cloud spend; unit-cost trend. [industry]
- **Practice processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Managing the approach*: analyse stakeholder requirements → define and agree the approach → implement → review and adjust. (b) *Financial planning (budgeting)*: prepare the budgeting process → collect and analyse data → compile budgets → communicate and agree → monitor costs and income → review and report. (c) *Management accounting*: identify and capture costs → select and follow a cost-allocation model → manage exceptions → standard and tailored reports. (d) *Economic modelling*: identify resources and their relations → identify costs and cost drivers → develop and test a resource-based costing model → automate and communicate it → pricing recommendations. Four activity areas: budgeting, accounting, charging (chargeback/showback), financial reporting. Terms: cost type, CapEx, OpEx, TCO, ROI, VOI (value on investment, including non-financial benefits). Roles: service financial manager, service financial authority, financial analyst. Example metrics: stakeholder satisfaction with financial management and with financial information; strategic decisions blocked by ineffective financial management; audit findings; incorrect or missing financial information; overhead costs as % of total costs. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Unit-cost and pricing for catalogue offerings** — price per offering/request item and per unit of consumption (seat, VM-hour, GB-month, ticket) derived from the economic model; prices shown in the portal at order time; price-list versioning and effective dates. [ITIL 4 economic modelling; industry]
- **Financial data quality** — exceptions queue for unallocated costs, missing cost centre on assets/CIs, untagged cloud spend; financial audit trail. [ITIL 4 practice metrics]

### Implementation notes
- Data model: `cost_type` → `cost_pool` (labour, hardware, software, cloud, facilities, external services; CapEx/OpEx flag) → `allocation_rule` (driver: users, devices, tickets, CI count, storage, CPU-hours, % split) → `allocation_run` (period, version, status draft/approved/locked) → `allocation_result` (service/offering, consumer org, amount, currency). Optional TBM-style hierarchy (cost pools → IT towers → solutions/services) stored as configurable taxonomy, not hard-coded.
- Labour cost = Σ(time entries × role or person rate); time entries carry a CapEx/OpEx flag (capitalizable project work vs run work).
- Actuals imported from ERP/GL with a mapping table (GL account → cost type/pool); periods are locked after close; corrections go into the next open period with audit.
- Budgets as versions (original, revised, forecast) per cost centre/service/project and period; variance = actual − budget per cell.
- Investment appraisal helpers: ROI = (benefits − costs)/costs; NPV = Σ CFₜ/(1 + r)ᵗ − I₀; payback period = first period where cumulative cash flow ≥ 0; store assumptions with the business case for later benefits-realization comparison.
- Multi-currency: amounts stored in transaction currency plus base currency at the period's rate; rates table versioned.
- Chargeback statements are immutable once issued; disputes create adjustment lines in a later run rather than editing history.

Waterfall, Agile and hybrid approaches (complements: PRINCE2, PMBOK, Scrum). Roles: project manager, sponsor, project board. [ITIL 4 Foundation]

Activities: demand intake, evaluation and prioritization of investments, approval and funding, portfolio monitoring and rebalancing, benefits realization. Roles: portfolio manager, investment board. [ITIL 4 Foundation]

Activities: sponsorship, stakeholder management, communication, resistance management, training, reinforcement; v5 Transformation toolbox (secondary) names ADKAR, Kotter's 8 steps, Lean change management, OKRs, retrospectives, stakeholder mapping, impact mapping, force-field analysis and maturity assessment. Roles: OCM lead, change sponsor, change agents/champions. [ITIL 4 Foundation; v5 Transformation]
- **Practice card: Business analysis** — Purpose: "to analyse a business or some element of a business, define its associated needs, and recommend solutions to address these needs and/or solve a business problem, which must facilitate value creation for stakeholders. Business analysis enables an organization to communicate its needs in a meaningful way and express the rationale for change. It enables the organization to design and describe solutions that enable value creation in line with the organization's objectives." Techniques: elicitation, process modelling, gap analysis, user stories, use cases, MoSCoW, data analysis (BABOK complement). Role: business analyst. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Demand and idea intake** — ideas and demands from portal, CIR, problems, customer feedback and strategy; triage; business case; scoring (value, effort, risk, strategic alignment; WSJF or RICE); stage gates to project, epic or enhancement (v5 Discover stage). [ITIL 4; ITIL v5]
- **Project records** — charter, objectives, scope, sponsor, manager, status (Proposed → Approved → Planning → Executing → Closing → Closed; On hold; Cancelled), phases and stage gates, WBS tasks with dependencies (Gantt), agile artefacts (epics, stories, sprints, boards), resource plan and capacity, budget vs actuals, RAID log (risks, assumptions, issues, decisions/dependencies), milestones, deliverables, RAG health, benefits. [industry]
- **Portfolio views** — ranked investment backlog, roadmap, demand vs capacity, scenario planning, financial roll-ups, alignment heat map against strategic objectives/OKRs. [ITIL 4]
- **Requirements management** — functional and non-functional requirements with traceability to designs, tests (SVT) and releases. [ITIL 4 business analysis]
- **OCM planning** — stakeholder map (influence/interest), change impact assessment by group, communication plan and calendar, training plan, readiness and adoption surveys, adoption metrics (usage, proficiency), resistance log, champion network. [ITIL 4 OCM]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — on-time delivery %; on-budget %; schedule variance SV = EV − PV; cost variance CV = EV − AC; SPI = EV/PV; CPI = EV/AC; benefits realized/planned; % portfolio spend aligned to strategic objectives; resource utilization; adoption rate after change; readiness score; requirements volatility; % requirements traced to passing tests. [industry]
- **Practice processes (practice guides, via secondary)** — *Project management* (PRINCE2-shaped): managing the organization's programme/project approach (develop and agree → communicate and embed → review and adjust); *directing projects* (authorize initiation → authorize the project → authorize a stage or exception plan → ad hoc direction → authorize closure); *managing projects* (start up → initiate → control a stage → manage stage boundaries → close); *managing product delivery* (accept, execute, deliver work packages). Roles: programme manager, project executive, project manager, project support, team manager, senior user. Example metrics: satisfaction with the approach; satisfaction with project/programme benefits; deviation count and impact; failures caused by an ineffective approach; % projects on time and on budget; % benefits realized; satisfaction with management and progress. *Portfolio management*: managing the organization's approach to portfolios (analyse strategy and resources → agree approach → set guidelines for portfolio groups and categories → communicate → review); managing portfolio lifecycles (plan → monitor → review). Roles: portfolio manager, portfolio owner, portfolio coordinator. Metrics: planned vs actual ROI; stakeholder satisfaction; strategic decisions supported by portfolios; adherence to the review schedule; portfolio-effectiveness trend; number of incorrect portfolio decisions. *Organizational change management* PSFs (paraphrased): create a change-enabling culture; maintain a holistic OCM approach; realize changes effectively; metrics: awareness of change principles, attitudes to change, resistance levels, satisfaction with procedures and communication. *Business analysis* PSFs (paraphrased): an organization-wide business-analysis approach; timely and effective solution proposals; metrics: satisfaction with needs understanding and proposed solutions, deviations from strategy, value realized, analysis timeliness; v5 terms utility, warranty, experience. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Benefits register** — benefit (measurable improvement perceived as an advantage by a stakeholder), owner, baseline, target, measurement method, realization date, actual value; linked to business case, portfolio item and the KPIs that evidence it; reviewed after closure. [ITIL 4 portfolio/project; industry]
- **Stage gates as approvals** — gate templates (idea → business case → initiate → stage n → close) with required artefacts and approvers (project executive/board, portfolio owner, architecture board, security, finance). [ITIL 4; PRINCE2 complement]

### Implementation notes
- Hierarchy: portfolio → programme → project → stage/phase → work package → task; demands/ideas convert into portfolio items keeping lineage; each level rolls up cost, effort, RAG status and risk.
- Scheduling: tasks with dependencies (FS/SS/FF/SF and lag), critical-path computation, baselines as immutable snapshots; agile items (epic/story/sprint) can live alongside WBS tasks and roll up through story points or % complete.
- Earned value: BAC from baseline; PV from baseline schedule; EV = % complete × BAC (or completed points/total points × BAC); AC from time entries and costs; SPI/CPI and EAC = BAC/CPI recomputed nightly.
- Prioritization formulas offered as scoring models: WSJF = cost of delay (user/business value + time criticality + risk reduction/opportunity enablement)/job size; RICE = reach × impact × confidence/effort; weighted scoring against strategic objectives.
- Resource management: allocations (person or role × period × hours) compared with capacity from the workforce calendar; over-allocation alerts; demand vs capacity heat map.
- Link project tasks to changes, releases and requirements so delivery work flows into change enablement and release management (ITIL 4 separates project delivery from change authorization).

## Module: workforce

### Features
- **Practice card: Workforce and talent management** — Purpose: "to ensure that the organization has the right people with the appropriate skills and knowledge and in the correct roles to support its business objectives." Activities: workforce planning, recruitment, onboarding, learning and development, performance measurement, succession planning, competency management, employee engagement and satisfaction, knowledge transfer. Competency profiles from the practice guides: L (leader: decision-making, delegating, motivating, evaluating outcomes), A (administrator: assigning and prioritizing tasks, record-keeping, reporting, basic improvements), C (coordinator/communicator), M (methods and techniques expert), T (technical expert). v5 adds "Organizations, people and AI" (AI as a collaborative partner taking repetitive, data-intensive work) and competency categories (technical, problem-solving, communication, collaboration, leadership, adaptability, emotional intelligence — secondary). [ITIL 4 Foundation; PGs; v5]
- **Skills and competency matrix** — skills catalogue, proficiency levels, certifications (ITIL, vendor), role competency profiles (L/A/C/M/T codes per role), gap analysis, learning plans; feeds skills-based routing and swarm invitations. [ITIL 4 PGs; industry]
- **Service desk workforce management** — contact-volume forecasting by channel and interval (Erlang C for voice/chat), shift planning and rosters, breaks, schedule adherence, real-time occupancy, on-call schedules. [industry; ITIL 4 service desk]
- **Team structures** — groups by technical domain, product/service, territory or consumer type (incident PG); tiered vs flat/swarming teams; product- and service-aligned teams sharing one lifecycle (v5); Conway's law awareness (v5 secondary). [ITIL 4 Incident PG; v5]
- **Culture features** — the incident PG warns against rewarding lone "heroes" and promotes collective responsibility, no-blame and continual learning: recognize team outcomes and knowledge contributions, blameless review templates, kudos. [ITIL 4 Incident PG §4.2.2]
- **Employee experience and satisfaction** — pulse surveys, eNPS, workload and after-hours paging indicators; v5: "employee experience predicts customer experience" (secondary). [ITIL 4 CDS; v5 Experience]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — skills coverage = critical services with ≥ N qualified people/critical services; training hours per FTE; certification rate; eNPS = %promoters − %detractors; attrition rate; schedule adherence = time in scheduled state/scheduled time; occupancy = handling time/logged-in time; time to competency for new hires. [industry]
- **Practice processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *Organizational planning*: strategic analysis → service value stream analysis → organizational design → initiating and monitoring organizational change → organization monitoring and review. (b) *Employee journey management*: analyse workforce needs and identify a journey model → verify and adjust → follow the model → manage exceptions → review journey models (employee journey = end-to-end experience across touchpoints, relationships and interactions; employee persona). (c) *Talent management*: define a competency vision → assess competencies → plan development and optimization → steer the development programme → manage exceptions → review. PSFs (paraphrased): keep workforce and talent management aligned with strategy; keep people motivated and competent; make administrative processes support strategy. Metrics: strategic alignment; stakeholder satisfaction; plans affected by workforce or skill gaps; attrition and exit reasons; employee satisfaction; time to fill roles; skill-development progress; staffing relative to service demand; coverage of critical positions; diversity. Recommendation: share workforce management between HR and line managers. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Employee-journey templates** — hire/onboard → develop → move/promote → offboard journeys with tasks across HR, IT, facilities and security (accounts, equipment, access, training, badge), feeding ESM (see esm). [ITIL 4 workforce PG concepts; industry]
- **Critical-role coverage** — per service and role: named primary/backup people, single-person dependencies, succession candidates; alerts when coverage drops below the minimum (also a continuity input). [ITIL 4 workforce metrics]

### Implementation notes
- Skills model: `skill` (taxonomy, category), `proficiency_scale` (e.g., 1–5 with behavioural anchors), `person_skill` (level, evidence, verified_by, verified_at, expiry), `role_profile` (required skills and levels plus L/A/C/M/T competency codes from the practice guides), `certification` (issuer, id, expiry). Gap = required level − actual level, aggregated per team/service.
- Skills-based routing/expert finder: score = Σ wᵢ·min(levelᵢ/requiredᵢ, 1) × availability factor (shift, on-call, current load/WIP) — used for assignment suggestions and swarm invitations; inferred skills (from resolved work) kept separate from verified skills.
- Workforce management for contact centres: forecast contacts per interval (seasonal time series), staff requirement via Erlang C for voice/chat (inputs: arrival rate, average handle time, target service level and answer time), shrinkage factor; rosters generated against requirements; adherence computed from presence/state events.
- Privacy: performance reviews, HR notes and engagement survey responses are restricted (manager/HR only; survey results aggregated with a minimum group size to preserve anonymity).

## Module: portal

### Features
- **Practice card: Service desk** — Purpose: "to capture demand for incident resolution and service requests. It should also be the entry point and single point of contact for the service provider with all of its users." PSFs (verbatim): "Enabling and continually improving effective, efficient, and convenient communications between the service provider and its users"; "Enabling the effective integration of user communications into value streams." Service empathy: "the ability to recognize, understand, predict, and project the interests, needs, intentions, and experiences of another party in order to establish, maintain, and improve the service relationship." Omnichannel communication: "unified communications across multiple channels based on sharing information across the channels and providing a seamless communication experience." Channel design depends on the service relationship model and type, user profile, provider profile and PESTLE factors. Channels: human (voice, live chat, email, walk-in) and technology (web portals, interactive voice menus, mobile apps, chatbots). Supports incident, service request, problem, configuration and relationship management; owns the channel for planned-change notifications (content from change/release). Roles: service desk manager, service desk agent (v5 list), supervisor, super users. [ITIL 4 Foundation; Service Desk PG via CDS]
- **Service desk processes (practice guide, via secondary)** — (a) *User query handling*: acknowledge and record → validate (identity, entitlement, context) → triage and route to incident management, service request management or another practice/value stream. (b) *Communicating to users*: identify and confirm the target audience → identify and confirm channels → package the information → send → collect and process receipt confirmations and feedback. (c) *Service desk optimization*: review performance, user satisfaction and channel effectiveness → identify and prioritize improvements → communicate changes. Concept "moment of truth": any interaction in which users form an impression of service quality. Channel guidance: phone for complex/urgent, portal for routine requests, chat for quick questions, email for non-urgent, walk-up for hands-on help, social media for feedback. Practice metrics: quality (accuracy, completeness) of logged information; channel convenience; satisfaction with information and channels; whether captured data meets value-stream needs; incorrect triage rate. AI uses named: virtual agents, intelligent routing, sentiment analysis that raises priority on frustration/urgency, real-time knowledge suggestions, predictive staffing. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)] [AI]
- **Identity and entitlement validation at intake** — caller verification (SSO identity, security questions or one-time code for phone/walk-up), entitlement check against the service offering/contract before work starts, VIP/high-risk flags; failed validation is logged. [Service desk PG "validate" step; information security]
- **Self-service portal** — branded, multilingual, responsive and accessible; universal search across knowledge, catalogue, community and own records; catalogue browsing and ordering; smart "report an issue" form with deflection suggestions; my requests/incidents/approvals; service status and outage banners; announcements; surveys and feedback; personalization by role, location and entitlement. [ITIL 4 shift-left; industry]
- **Virtual agent** — intent detection (incident, request, question, status), knowledge answers with citations, catalogue ordering with slot filling, incident creation with classification, status lookups, password reset/unlock automation, live-agent hand-off with full context (v5 Communication capability; v5 Support stage: chatbots, intelligent routing, auto-resolution). [ITIL 4 channels; ITIL v5] [AI]
- **Omnichannel intake** — email-to-record (parsing, threading, attachments), phone/CTI (screen pop, call recording links), live chat, messaging apps (Teams, Slack, WhatsApp), walk-up/tech bar with queue and appointments, social; one contact history per user across channels so users never repeat information (the CDS omnichannel example). [ITIL 4 Service Desk PG via CDS]
- **Triage and routing into value streams** — the service desk triages user queries and forwards them to the right value stream (incident handling, request fulfilment, access, problem, change, feedback); skills- and load-based routing; triage console. [Service Desk PG via CDS]
- **Agent workspace** — unified queue, record with user context (assets, open records, recent changes, outages, VIP), knowledge suggestions, response templates and macros, collaboration with resolver groups, presence, multi-record tabs. [industry]
- **Shift-left** — move knowledge closer to users (FAQs, self-service, better-trained front line, swarming); shift-left analytics (records resolvable at self-service or first line) and automation backlog. [ITIL 4 CDS]
- **Communications** — planned-change and outage notifications, broadcast messages, status page, subscriptions per service. [Service Desk PG via CDS]
- **Surveys** — post-resolution CSAT, periodic satisfaction, NPS/CES, touchpoint surveys for experience metrics. [ITIL 4; DSV]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — first contact resolution = resolved during first contact/contacts; average speed of answer; abandonment rate = abandoned/offered; average handle time; self-service share = self-service contacts/all contacts; deflection rate; channel mix; first-time-right routing = records not reassigned after triage/records; time to triage; CSAT; contacts per user per month; cost per contact. [industry]

### Implementation notes
- Interaction (contact) record separate from work records: one interaction can create or update several records; preserves channel metadata and transcripts.
- Email threading via record number in subject plus Message-ID/In-Reply-To headers; loop and auto-reply detection.
- Virtual agent = retrieval over user-permitted knowledge + tool calls for catalogue/incident actions executed with the user's permissions; hand-off payload includes transcript, detected intent, collected fields.

## Module: esm

### Features
- **ITIL basis for enterprise service management** — ITIL 4 deliberately generalized from "IT service management" to "service management": the SVS, service value chain, four dimensions and guiding principles apply to any service provider (Wikipedia notes the ITIL 4 aim "to reduce the emphasis on IT Service Management in favor of general Service Management"). Key definitions are domain-neutral (service, product, service offering, service relationship, customer/user/sponsor), so HR, facilities, legal, finance, procurement and security teams are service providers with their own offerings, catalogues, agreements and value streams. ITIL v5 widens the frame to "digital product and service management" and its digital spectrum explicitly includes purely analogue and digitally supported services (e.g., facilities work), so non-IT services sit inside the same lifecycle model. [ITIL 4 Foundation; Wikipedia; ITIL v5]
- **Department-as-service-provider model** — each business function (HR, facilities, finance, legal, procurement, security, marketing, IT) is configured as a provider unit with: its own service catalogue and request models, assignment groups, knowledge base, SLAs/OLAs, business calendar, approval policies, intake channels, surveys and reports; all units share one people/org model, one portal and one record engine. [ITIL 4 service relationship model; industry ESM pattern]
- **Unified employee portal** — one entry point for all providers ("single point of contact" generalized beyond IT): universal search across every department's knowledge and catalogue, a "my items" view across departments, department-specific branding/sections, life-event navigation ("I'm moving office", "I'm going on parental leave"), and smart routing so the employee does not need to know which department owns the request. [ITIL 4 service desk purpose; DSV omnichannel]
- **Employee journeys (hire-to-retire)** — journey templates spanning departments: pre-boarding → onboarding (contract, payroll, equipment, accounts, access, badge, desk, training, buddy) → internal moves/role changes (access re-evaluation, equipment swap) → leave and return → offboarding (access revocation, asset return, final pay, knowledge handover, exit survey). Each step is a task or request in the owning department's queue; the journey shows progress and SLA to the employee and manager. ITIL's workforce & talent practice describes "employee journey management" with journey models, exceptions and reviews. [ITIL 4 workforce & talent PG via secondary; DSV onboarding/offboarding]
- **HR case management** — case types (policy inquiry, payroll issue, benefits, leave, employee relations, grievance, investigation, document request), confidentiality levels (standard, restricted, highly confidential — e.g., employee-relations investigations visible only to named HR specialists), HR knowledge with audience by country/employee type, HR SLAs, sensitive-data masking, case-to-task delegation to payroll/benefit providers. [industry; ITIL 4 information security and privacy principles]
- **Facilities / workplace services** — request models for moves, maintenance, cleaning, room setup, access cards, parking; planned/preventive maintenance schedules for building assets (assets and CIs extended to non-IT classes: HVAC, lifts, rooms, floors); space and desk booking; visitor management; work orders with on-site technicians (see field_service); supplier (contractor) dispatch with UC timers. [ITIL 4 service request + asset concepts applied to non-IT; industry]
- **Finance, procurement and legal desks** — finance (AP/AR inquiries, expense issues, vendor setup), procurement (purchase requests with budget approvals → PO → receipt), legal (contract review requests, NDAs, matter intake with conflict check and privilege flag); each with catalogues, approval policies and confidentiality settings. [industry; ITIL 4 SRM and supplier management]
- **Cross-department value streams** — one request can spawn tasks in several departments (e.g., new hire: HR, IT, facilities, security, finance) orchestrated by a flow; status rolls up into a single requester-visible record; OLAs between departments track internal hand-offs. [ITIL 4 value streams; OLA concept]
- **Department-specific agreements and reviews** — SLAs per department offering, internal OLAs, satisfaction surveys per department, departmental service reviews and improvement registers (CIR entries tagged by department). [ITIL 4 SLM, CI]
- **Employee experience measures** — per-journey and per-touchpoint experience surveys, time-lost indicators, journey cycle time, first-day readiness (onboarding: % new hires with equipment and access on day 1). ITIL v5 Experience treats employee experience as a predictor of customer experience (secondary). [ITIL v5 Experience; industry]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — first-day readiness = new hires with all day-1 items delivered/new hires; journey cycle time = journey_completed − journey_started; cross-department hand-off delay = Σ wait time between department tasks; self-service share per department; CSAT per department; case reopen rate; confidential-case access violations (target 0). [industry]
- **AI** — cross-department intent routing ("which department owns this?"), policy question answering over department knowledge with audience filtering (country, employee type), HR case summarization with sensitive-data redaction, document generation (employment letters), sentiment detection for employee-relations cases (with human review). [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Model "provider unit" (department) as a first-class scope: catalogue items, groups, knowledge bases, SLAs, forms and reports carry `provider_unit_id`; admins can be delegated per unit (see admin/access).
- Sensitive case types (HR employee relations, legal matters, security investigations) use a deny-by-default ACL with explicit membership, field-level encryption of notes, read-access logging and exclusion from global search and AI retrieval unless the viewer is entitled.
- Journey engine = parent "journey" record + flow that instantiates child tasks/requests per department with dependencies and due dates relative to an anchor date (start date, last day); changes to the anchor date re-plan open tasks.
- Extend CMDB/asset classes for facilities assets and locations (site → building → floor → room/desk) so the same incident/request/maintenance engine works for buildings.

## Module: csm

### Features
- **Practice card: Relationship management** — Purpose: "to establish and nurture the links between the organization and its stakeholders at strategic and tactical levels. It includes the identification, analysis, monitoring, and continual improvement of relationships with and between stakeholders." PSFs (paraphrased): an effective, continually improved organization-wide approach to relationships; healthy relationships within the organization; healthy relationships with external stakeholders. Processes (practice guide, via secondary): (a) *Managing a common approach to relationships*: analyse organizational culture, strategy and stakeholders → develop and agree key relationship principles → develop relationship models for key stakeholder groups → embed behaviour patterns in daily interactions → review and adjust. (b) *Managing relationship journeys*: identify stakeholders and the relevant relationship model → verify and adjust the model to the situation → follow the model → manage exceptions → review the relationship. Terms: cooperation (working with others toward one's own goals), collaboration (working toward shared goals), relationship model ("a repeatable approach for managing relationships with a particular type of stakeholder"). Roles: relationship manager, relationship agent (v5); business relationship manager (BRM, ITIL 4 extension module, now absorbed into v5). Metrics: adoption of the relationship approach; satisfaction with relationship management; broken relationships due to non-adoption; employee satisfaction with workplace relationships; attrition due to toxic relationships; satisfaction with external relationships; external image; broken external relationships. [ITIL 4 Foundation; ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Service consumer model** — organizations in a service relationship act as provider and/or consumer; consumer roles: customer (defines requirements, responsible for outcomes), user (uses the service), sponsor (authorizes budget). Tool: account (consumer organization) with contacts tagged by role(s); one person can be customer, user and sponsor; contracts and SLAs reference the customer; budget approvals route to the sponsor; usage, incidents and surveys come from users. [ITIL 4 Foundation]
- **Service relationship types** — basic (standard products/services, relationship managed at the level of service delivery; e.g., "out-of-the-box" offerings), cooperative (some tailoring, joint planning of service levels and delivery), partnership (provider and consumer may "act as one organization", aligning on strategic goals and priorities). Tool: relationship type per account and per supplier, driving default engagement models (review cadence, named contacts, QBRs, joint planning). [ITIL 4 DSV via CDS study guide]
- **Customer journey (DSV, seven steps) as an account lifecycle** — *Explore* (understanding service consumers, providers and targeting markets), *Engage* (aspects of service value, relationship types, building relationships and trust, analysing customer needs, managing suppliers and partners), *Offer* (managing demand and opportunities, specifying and managing customer requirements, designing service offerings and UX, selling and obtaining offerings), *Agree* (agreeing and planning value co-creation, negotiating and agreeing utility, warranty and service experience), *Onboard* (planning onboarding, fostering relationships with users, user engagement and delivery channels, enabling users, elevating mutual capabilities, offboarding), *Co-create* (service mindset, ongoing service interactions, nurturing user communities), *Realize* (measuring service value, tracking, assessing and reporting value realization, evaluating realization and improving journeys, realizing value for the provider). DSV also covers touchpoints and service interactions, journey mapping, design, measurement and improvement. Tool: journey stage per account/opportunity/offering subscription, with stage-specific checklists, artefacts and metrics. [ITIL 4 DSV course syllabus]
- **Account and contact management** — account hierarchy (parent/subsidiary, business units), sites, contacts with consumer roles, account team (relationship manager, service delivery manager, technical account manager), entitlements (offerings subscribed, quantities, support hours, channels), contracts and SLAs, assets/CIs installed at the account (install base), service health per account, open cases and escalations, account plan and objectives. [industry; ITIL 4 relationship + SLM]
- **External case management** — customer-raised cases (incidents, questions, requests, complaints) via customer portal, email, chat, phone and API; entitlement check at intake (is this customer entitled to this support level?); case → internal incident/problem/change escalation with customer-safe communications; case states (new, in progress, awaiting customer, awaiting internal/vendor, resolved, closed) and customer acceptance of resolution. [industry; ITIL 4 service desk + incident]
- **Customer portal and communities** — branded per customer or product; knowledge (public and entitled), case submission and tracking, service status for subscribed services, downloads, community forums (DSV "nurturing user communities"), ideas portal. [DSV; industry]
- **Demand and opportunity management** — demand records from customers (new needs, upsell, change of scope), pipeline of offers, proposals with offering configuration and pricing, conversion to agreements; DSV "shaping demand and service offerings". [DSV]
- **Agreements and value-focused SLAs** — negotiation workflow (draft → negotiation → agreed → active → under review → renewed/terminated), utility + warranty + experience targets, outcome-based metrics, exceptions and service credits; SLAs must relate to catalogue services and to defined outcomes (watermelon-effect warning). [ITIL 4 Foundation; DSV]
- **Onboarding and offboarding of customers and users** — onboarding plans (accounts, access, training, channels, data migration, welcome communications), user enablement content, readiness checks; offboarding plans (data return/deletion, access revocation, asset recovery, final billing, exit survey, lessons learned). [DSV]
- **Service reviews and QBRs** — scheduled reviews per relationship type (monthly for partnerships, quarterly for cooperative), auto-assembled packs (SLA attainment, experience/CSAT/NPS, incidents/problems/changes affecting the customer, value realized, roadmap, risks), action log to CIR. [ITIL 4 SLM/DSV]
- **Value realization tracking** — expected outcomes per agreement, value indicators (business metrics provided by the customer, usage, adoption, cost avoided), realization reports, journey improvement actions. [DSV "Realize"]
- **Feedback, complaints and compliments** — capture channels, complaint handling with severity, root cause and response SLA, compliments routed to teams, trend analysis; linked to relationship health. [ITIL 4 SRM request types; DSV feedback management]
- **Relationship health score** — composite of CSAT/NPS/CES, SLA attainment, escalations, complaint count, sponsor engagement, value-realization status, renewal risk; thresholds trigger relationship-manager actions. [industry; RM metrics]
- **Stakeholder maps** — stakeholders per account with influence/interest, attitude, relationship owner and engagement plan. [ITIL 4 RM; OCM]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — CSAT = satisfied responses/responses; NPS = %promoters − %detractors; CES (customer effort score) average; account SLA attainment; escalation rate = escalated cases/cases; complaint rate per 1,000 users; churn/non-renewal rate; time to onboard = onboarding_completed − agreement_signed; value-realization attainment = realized benefits/expected benefits; review cadence adherence. [industry; DSV]
- **AI** — account health prediction and churn/renewal risk, sentiment analysis of customer communications, case summarization for account managers, QBR narrative drafting, next-best-action for relationship managers, entitlement-aware answer generation in the customer portal. [AI]

### Implementation notes
- External users are a separate persona with company-scoped visibility: they see only their account's cases, assets and agreements (and entitled knowledge); contact roles (customer/user/sponsor/admin) control approval rights and what they can see across their organization.
- Entitlement engine: entitlement (account × offering × channel × support hours × quantity/expiry) evaluated at intake; no entitlement → route to sales/relationship manager rather than rejecting silently.
- Case ↔ internal record links propagate status but filter fields: only customer-visible comments and states flow out; internal work notes never leave.
- Journey stage stored as data on account/subscription with timestamps per stage to compute stage durations and drop-offs.

## Module: analytics

### Features
- **Practice card: Measurement and reporting** — Purpose: "to support good decision-making and continual improvement by decreasing the levels of uncertainty." Measurement = a means of decreasing uncertainty based on observations expressed in quantifiable units; metric = a measurement or calculation monitored or reported for management and improvement; KPI = an important metric used to evaluate success in meeting an objective. Requirements (paraphrased PSFs): measurements driven by objectives; data quality and availability; reporting that supports decision-making. Processes (practice guide, via secondary): (a) *Designing the measurement and reporting system*: develop metrics and measurement methods → form KPI scorecards → design report templates and a reporting policy; (b) *Reporting and evaluating*: gather and process data → analyse and produce reports → evaluate reports and support decisions. Metrics: data-to-errors ratio; % reports without errors; consumer satisfaction with data quality; % automatically generated reports; report timeliness; stakeholder satisfaction with reports. Recommendations: link metrics to actions; consider behavioural effects of metrics; keep data consistent through proper tools. No dedicated role (practice owners and service managers share it). [ITIL 4 Foundation; M&R PG via ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Practice card: Continual improvement** — Purpose: "to align the organization's practices and services with changing business needs through the ongoing improvement of products, services, and practices, or any element involved in the management of products and services." Improvement = "a deliberately introduced change that results in increased value for one or more stakeholders" (v5 study site wording). Requirements (paraphrased PSFs): an effective organization-wide approach to continual improvement; effective and efficient improvement across the organization. Processes (via secondary): (a) *Managing a common approach to continual improvement*: analyse requirements, culture, strategy and stakeholders → identify models and methods (Lean, Six Sigma, Agile, PDCA) → define approach, governance and decision-making → communicate and integrate → review the approach. (b) *Managing continual improvement initiatives*: identify and log opportunities from any source → assess, prioritize and approve → plan scope, resources, timeline and expected outcomes → facilitate implementation (remove blockers, manage dependencies) → measure results against expected outcomes and record lessons learned. Role: continual improvement manager (coordinates the approach, maintains the CIR, facilitates prioritization, reports outcomes). Metrics: stakeholder satisfaction with improvement value; adoption of an improvement culture; economic value (ROI, NPV); % successful initiatives; % failed or counter-productive improvements; continual improvement productivity index. Common mistake: a CIR that is created but not kept up to date — it should be updated from service reviews, incident reviews and problem investigations. [ITIL 4 Foundation; CI PG via ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **Continual improvement model (ITIL 4)** — guided wizard per improvement initiative: (1) What is the vision? (link to strategy/OKR) → (2) Where are we now? (baseline metrics, assessment) → (3) Where do we want to be? (targets, CSFs/KPIs) → (4) How do we get there? (plan, options, experiments) → (5) Take action (tasks, changes, projects; iterative) → (6) Did we get there? (measure vs target) → (7) How do we keep the momentum going? (embed, communicate, next iteration). v5 keeps a seven-step model with refined wording (one secondary site renders steps 6–7 differently — unverified). [ITIL 4 Foundation; ITIL v5]
- **Continual Improvement Register (CIR)** — "a database or structured document used to record and manage improvement initiatives throughout their lifecycles"; one or several registers (organization, practice, team, department); fields: id, title, description, source (incident review, PIR, problem, service review, audit finding, survey, employee idea, maturity assessment, risk, customer feedback), affected value stream/service/practice/product, four-dimensions impact, guiding-principle tags, expected benefit (value, cost, risk, experience, sustainability), baseline and target KPIs, effort/cost estimate, priority (value vs effort or WSJF), owner, sponsor, status (idea → submitted → assessed → approved → planned → in progress → implemented → benefits measured → closed; rejected/deferred/archived), target date, linked changes/projects/tasks, actual results, lessons learned. [ITIL 4 Foundation; CI PG via secondary; industry]
- **Improvement methods toolkit** — PDCA, Lean (waste identification, value-stream mapping), Six Sigma DMAIC, Kaizen events, Toyota Kata (HVIT), A3 problem solving, retrospectives, SWOT, balanced scorecard, gap analysis, benchmarking, maturity assessment; each as a template object attached to a CIR item. [ITIL 4 DPI/HVIT; industry]
- **Goal cascade and scorecards** — objectives → critical success factors → KPIs → metrics → measurements, cascaded from organization to value stream, service, practice and team (DPI); balanced scorecard perspectives (financial, customer, internal process, learning & growth); OKRs (v5 transformation toolbox, secondary); every KPI has owner, formula, data source, frequency, target, thresholds (RAG), baseline and history. [ITIL 4 DPI; ITIL v5]
- **Dashboards and reports per practice** — out-of-the-box dashboards for each practice using the practice-guide key metrics (incident, problem, change, request, service desk, knowledge, SLM, M&EM, configuration, asset, release, deployment, catalogue, continuity, availability, capacity, security, supplier, financial, project, portfolio, workforce, relationship, CI); role-based home pages (agent, team lead, practice owner, service owner, executive); drill-down from KPI to records. [ITIL 4 PGs]
- **Value-stream analytics** — per value stream: lead time, cycle time per step, wait time, flow efficiency = value-adding time/lead time, % complete & accurate per hand-off, throughput, WIP, rework loops; v5 activity classification (value-adding, supporting, coordinating, non-value-adding); bottleneck detection; lifecycle-stage tagging in ITIL 4 (plan, improve, engage, design & transition, obtain/build, deliver & support) and v5 (discover, design, acquire, build, transition, operate, deliver, support) vocabularies. v5 Appendix B maps each practice to each lifecycle activity as *enabling* (directly participates) or *supporting* (provides information/methods); store that mapping as configurable data for practice heat-maps. [ITIL 4 CDS/DPI; ITIL v5 (secondary)]
- **Experience analytics** — XLA attainment, sentiment trends, journey analytics (drop-offs per touchpoint), digital-experience scores per device/app/location, "watermelon gap" between SLA attainment and experience. [ITIL v5 Experience; DSV]
- **Maturity and capability assessments** — ITIL Maturity Model: practice capability levels 1–5 assessed with capability criteria (each criterion mapped to a PSF, one of the four dimensions and a level; a level is achieved when its criteria and all lower-level criteria are met) and organizational/value-system maturity levels 1–5 over guiding principles, governance, value chain, practices and continual improvement; v5 study site names value-system levels Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimizing (secondary; ITIL 4 level names unverified). Assessment types: comprehensive (practices + value system), high-level (value system only), selected practices. Tool: assessment campaigns, questionnaires and evidence (documents, interviews, performance data), scoring, gap = target − current per practice weighted by business importance, radar charts, and automatic CIR items for gaps; periodic re-assessment trend. [ITIL Maturity Model; ITIL v5 (secondary)]
- **Benchmarking** — compare KPIs across teams, departments, regions or peer organizations (anonymized), with normalization (per user, per service, per 1,000 CIs). [ITIL 4 DPI]
- **Self-service reporting** — report builder (list, pivot, trend, bar/line, heat map, scorecard), scheduled distribution, subscriptions, exports, embedded charts in records and portals, report-level and row-level security, data dictionary describing every metric. [industry; M&R PG "report templates and policy"]
- **Data quality monitoring** — completeness/validity checks on key fields (CI on incidents, resolution codes, categories), data-to-errors ratio, correction workflows; analytics flags metrics computed on low-quality data. [M&R PG metrics]
- **KPIs for the improvement system itself** — % CIR items with measured benefits; average time from idea to implementation; % improvements delivering expected value; number of active improvement owners (culture adoption); % KPIs with owners and targets; report automation rate; report timeliness. [CI and M&R PG metrics]
- **AI** — natural-language questions over metrics ("why did MTTR rise last month?") answered with permission-filtered data and cited queries, anomaly detection on KPI series, automated narrative for service reviews, improvement-opportunity mining (clustering incidents, rework loops, long waits in value streams), forecasting of KPIs, maturity-gap recommendations. [ITIL v5 cognition/clarification; AI]

### Implementation notes
- Metric definitions as versioned metadata: id, name, formula (expression over facts), grain, filters, unit, direction (higher/lower is better), owner, target/threshold sets per scope, data source, refresh schedule; dashboards reference metric ids so formulas change in one place.
- Fact tables derived from the record event log (state transitions, assignments, SLA events) — e.g., `fact_state_interval(record_id, state, start, end, business_seconds)` — so lead/cycle/wait time and flow efficiency come from history, not mutable fields.
- Value-stream steps map to (record type, state set) pairs; a record's path through steps yields per-step durations; flow efficiency = Σ value-adding step time/(completed_at − created_at).
- Row-level security is enforced in the query layer for every report, dashboard, export and AI analytics answer (same ACL engine as records); aggregated reports over restricted data use minimum-group-size rules.
- CIR items are ordinary work records (with a dedicated state machine) so they reuse assignment, SLA, approvals and notifications; benefits measurement creates a scheduled check that compares the KPI after implementation with the baseline.

## Module: ai

### Features
- **ITIL position on AI (ITIL 4 → v5)** — ITIL 4 treats AI/ML, RPA and CI/CD as automation technologies inside the "information and technology" dimension and practice automation tables (e.g., ML-based incident classification "very high" impact at high volumes; ML for monitoring data analysis). ITIL (Version 5) is described by PeopleCert as "AI-native": AI appears in Foundation (generative AI, agentic AI, AI maturity, AI governance, the AI Capability Model), in every lifecycle activity, in the "Organizations, people and AI" dimension (human–AI collaboration), in the Experience book (experience management when AI is involved) and in the separate ITIL AI Governance extension (training from Nov 2026). A March 2026 PeopleCert webinar stressed that v5 "does not promote AI for AI's sake" (Global Knowledge, French). Agentic AI = systems that "operate with a degree of autonomy", acting toward objectives rather than fixed instructions (Dion Training summary). [ITIL 4 PGs; ITIL v5 via secondary sources]
- **ITIL AI Capability Model (6C)** — sits in the information & technology dimension; used to inventory which AI capabilities are in use and to define risks, controls and countermeasures per capability; capabilities are interconnected, not mutually exclusive: *Creation* (generate content, code, configurations, workflow designs — incident summaries, postmortems, articles, release notes); *Curation* (organize, filter, classify and improve data quality — alert correlation and noise reduction, flagging obsolete articles, duplicate CMDB records, intelligent routing); *Clarification* (explain, simplify or translate — technical detail into stakeholder updates, insight extraction from complex data); *Cognition* (decide, predict, detect patterns — severity and root-cause prediction, anomaly detection, workload forecasting, change-risk scoring); *Communication* (interact with people in natural language — virtual agents, chat and status-page updates); *Coordination* (orchestrate workflows and act — assign responders, escalate, trigger runbooks/remediation, route work). Tool: every AI feature and agent is registered with its 6C capability set, which drives its default governance template. [PeopleCert community (12 May 2026); incident.io (1 Feb 2026); ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)] [AI]
- **AI governance model (v5)** — v5 names four reasons traditional IT governance is insufficient for AI: autonomy in decision-making (choices made "without human approval at each step"), lack of transparency ("it's often unclear why an AI made a particular decision"), evolving ethical dilemmas, rapidly evolving regulation; and proposes four governance perspectives: (1) decision authority and risk, (2) ethical principles, (3) data governance and performance, (4) regulatory compliance. AI Governance module outcomes add decision-authority frameworks, fairness criteria, transparency/explainability/accountability mechanisms, AI-readiness assessment and continuous governance. Cited figures (ITIL AI Governance white paper via secondary): ~90% of organizations use AI but ~18% have a fully implemented AI governance framework; ~82% use AI agents but ~44% have policies to secure them. [incident.io; PeopleCert community; Purple Griffon; Global Knowledge]
- **AI system inventory (including shadow AI)** — register of every AI system, model, agent, prompt template and third-party AI service: owner, purpose, 6C capabilities, practices/value streams where used, data sources and data classification, model/provider and version, autonomy level, risk tier, applicable regulations, human-oversight design, evaluation results, status (proposed → assessed → approved → pilot → production → retired); discovery of unauthorized AI use ("shadow AI": tools used "without formal authorization or oversight") via SaaS/expense/network signals. Registered AI systems are CIs/assets so change, incident, problem and supplier practices apply to them. [PeopleCert community roadmap step 1; ITIL v5]
- **Decision-authority matrix for AI actions** — per AI feature/agent and action type: autonomous (act and log), act-then-notify, propose-and-approve (human in the loop), advise-only, or prohibited; conditions by risk (service criticality, change risk, customer-facing, financial/security impact, confidence); approvers by role (incident manager, change authority, service owner); escalation paths; who may override; rollback procedure required for autonomous actions. Questions to answer per v5-aligned guidance: which actions are autonomous vs approved, how escalation boundaries are enforced, what the rollback is if automation makes things worse, how decisions are logged for review. [incident.io (ITIL 5 interpretation); Dion Training]
- **Confidence thresholds and uncertainty display** — every AI suggestion carries a confidence score; per feature thresholds for "show", "pre-fill" and "auto-apply"; UI distinguishes uncertain from confident output; low-confidence → human. [incident.io; ITIL 4 incident PG part-automatic classification] [AI]
- **Human review of generated content** — AI-drafted customer/stakeholder updates, knowledge articles, PIRs and SLA narratives require review before publication (configurable per audience); AI-generated content is labelled as such; insufficient information → AI declines rather than invents (hallucination control). [incident.io; ITIL v5 Compass AI strategy page] [AI]
- **Explainability and audit trail** — for each AI output/action: inputs and sources used (citations to records/articles), model and version, prompt/template version, confidence, rationale/top features, human decision (accepted/edited/rejected), downstream actions; viewable inside the record timeline and exportable for post-incident reviews and audits. [ITIL v5 transparency perspective; ISO/IEC 42001] [AI]
- **AI change control** — model updates (training data, configuration logic, prompts, decision thresholds, provider/model version) go through change enablement with documented approval, risk assessment and tracking; staged rollout and rollback of model versions; AI feature flags and kill switches per tenant/department/feature. [Dion Training (ITIL 5 AI governance); ITIL 4 change enablement]
- **AI performance monitoring (observability)** — monitoring & event management applied to AI: accuracy/acceptance rate of suggestions, override rate, drift in input data and outcomes, latency, cost, hallucination/complaint reports, fairness/bias metrics across user groups (e.g., routing bias), safety incidents; thresholds create events and incidents; periodic review cycles and validation controls. Risks to monitor (v5-aligned summary): operational risk, compliance gaps, unclear accountability for automated decisions, unintended service disruption, model drift, data quality issues. [Dion Training; ITIL v5 Compass] [AI]
- **AI documentation in knowledge management** — per AI system: data sources, assumptions, limitations, intended use, known failure modes, evaluation results and user guidance ("model cards") stored as controlled knowledge. [Dion Training (KM documents data sources, assumptions, limitations)]
- **AI incidents and problems** — record types/categories for AI failures (harmful output, wrong action, data leakage, bias complaint), with severity, containment (disable feature), notification duties (EU AI Act serious-incident reporting for high-risk systems), root-cause analysis and corrective actions. [ITIL 4 incident/problem applied to AI; EU AI Act]
- **AI impact and risk assessment** — questionnaire per AI use case: impact on individuals and the organization, reversibility, transparency, data sensitivity, regulatory class (EU AI Act prohibited/high/limited/minimal risk), ethical considerations, required controls; ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system alignment (AI policy, roles, AI impact assessment, AI system lifecycle, data for AI systems, information for interested parties, use of AI systems, third-party relationships) and ISO/IEC 20000-1 SMS integration; statement of applicability. [PeopleCert community; ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (own knowledge)]
- **AI use cases by v5 lifecycle activity (secondary site)** — Discover: market analysis, demand forecasting, sentiment analysis; Design: AI-assisted design generation, accessibility testing; Acquire: vendor evaluation, contract analysis, procurement optimization; Build: code generation, automated testing, defect prediction; Transition: deployment-risk assessment, automated rollback; Operate: predictive monitoring, anomaly detection, auto-remediation; Deliver: personalized service, chatbot-assisted support; Support: ticket routing, knowledge suggestions, auto-resolution. LLM uses by practice: incident thread summaries, problem/PIR report drafts, change-description analysis and impact prediction, KM process documentation drafts, SLA report narratives, role-specific training content. [ITIL v5 Compass AI strategy page (secondary)] [AI]
- **AIOps capability layers and maturity (secondary site, not ITIL text)** — layers: data ingestion (metrics, logs, traces) → noise reduction (dedupe, correlation, suppression) → anomaly detection → root-cause analysis across the service graph → predictive alerting → auto-remediation via predefined runbooks; maturity: reactive → automated (threshold) alerting → intelligent alerting (ML anomaly detection and correlation) → predictive (failure/capacity forecasting with remediation recommendations) → autonomous (self-healing for known scenarios). Suggested adoption order: alert-noise reduction, virtual agent for password resets/FAQs, intelligent routing, knowledge auto-suggestion, anomaly detection on critical services, predictive capacity, auto-remediation. [ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)] [AI]
- **AI and experience management** — the v5 Experience book covers "experience management in the AI context" (e.g., measuring user experience of AI-mediated interactions: containment with satisfaction, effort, hand-off quality, trust) and AI governance. [ITIL (Version 5) Experience course outline]
- **Human–AI workforce design** — "Organizations, people and AI" dimension: define which work AI takes (repetitive, data-intensive), how humans supervise it, new roles (AI product owner, AI governance lead/committee — names unverified), competence development (AI literacy), and accountability that stays with service owners and IT leadership; AI is decision support, not a replacement for accountable decisions. [ITIL v5 via Dion Training; ITIL v5 Compass]
- **AI metrics** — suggestion acceptance rate = accepted/shown; auto-resolution rate; deflection with satisfaction (self-service sessions resolved and rated ≥ 4/5); override rate; mean confidence vs observed accuracy (calibration error); AI-attributed incidents; % AI systems inventoried with owner and risk tier; % AI features with completed impact assessment; time to disable (kill-switch drill); cost per AI-assisted resolution. [industry; derived from v5 governance]

### Implementation notes
- AI gateway service: all model calls go through one service that enforces tenant/feature enablement, data-classification rules (which fields may leave the platform; PII redaction), per-feature model routing, prompt-template versioning, rate/cost budgets, logging (inputs/outputs hashed or stored per retention policy) and kill switches.
- Retrieval (RAG) uses the same ACL engine as search: candidate documents are filtered by the requesting user's permissions before ranking; the answer cites only retrieved items; restricted domains (HR ER cases, security incidents) are excluded unless the user is entitled.
- Agents act with the invoking user's (or a scoped service identity's) permissions — never a super-user — and every tool call is checked against the decision-authority matrix (autonomous/approve/advise), with approvals created as normal approval records.
- Store each AI interaction as an `ai_event` (feature, 6C tags, model, version, prompt id, inputs refs, output, confidence, citations, user decision, actions taken) linked to the business record; analytics on acceptance/override/drift come from these events.
- Evaluation harness: golden datasets per feature (e.g., labelled historical incidents for classification), offline evaluation before a model change is approved, online A/B or shadow mode before auto-apply is enabled.

## Module: access

### Features
- **ITIL basis for access control** — ITIL 4 has no separate "access management" practice (it was an ITIL v3 Service Operation process); identity and access are covered by *information security management* (confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication — "ensuring someone is who they claim to be" — and non-repudiation — "ensuring that someone can't deny that they took an action") and *service request management* (requests for access to a resource or service are a standard request type, with approvals that may be required by information security). "Access to resources" is also one of the three components of a service offering (goods, access to resources, service actions), so entitlements are part of the service model. ITIL v3 heritage access process, still a useful template: request access → verify (identity and entitlement) → provide rights → monitor identity status (joiner/mover/leaver) → log and track access → remove or restrict rights. [ITIL 4 Foundation; ITIL v3 Service Operation (heritage)]
- **Organization model from the "organizations and people" dimension** — legal entities/companies, business units, departments, cost centres, locations (region → site → building → floor), teams/assignment groups (ITIL groupings: by technical domain, product/service, territory or consumer type), service-provider units (IT, HR, facilities…), external organizations (customers, suppliers, partners); people with manager hierarchy, roles and group memberships; the service relationship roles customer, user and sponsor on consumer organizations. [ITIL 4 Foundation; Incident PG §4.2]
- **Role catalogue seeded from ITIL roles** — practice roles (incident manager, major incident manager, problem manager/coordinator, change manager/coordinator, change authority/CAB member, release manager, deployment manager, configuration manager/librarian, IT asset manager/custodian, licence manager, service level manager, service owner, service catalogue manager, service desk manager/agent, knowledge manager, continuity manager, availability manager, capacity manager, CISO/information security manager, risk manager/owner, supplier manager/coordinator, service financial manager, portfolio manager/owner, project manager, relationship manager/agent, continual improvement manager, business analyst, architect, test manager) plus v5 Appendix C generic roles (practice owner, practice manager, product owner, service owner, head of product and service management, risk and compliance manager, sustainability officer). Each role bundles permissions and is mapped to a competency profile (L/A/C/M/T codes). [ITIL v5 practice cards and Appendix C via secondary]
- **RACI per practice activity** — responsibility matrix templates per process activity (e.g., incident registration: service desk agent R; classification: agent R, incident manager A; closure: per incident model — product owner, service owner, incident manager or agent), used to derive default permissions and approvals. [ITIL 4 PG role tables]
- **Role- and attribute-based permissions** — RBAC for features/actions (create, read, update, approve, assign, close, delete, administer) per record type; ABAC conditions for row-level scope (my records, my group's queue, my company, my provider unit, services I own, CIs I manage, records I'm on the watch list for, customer-visible only); field-level rules (internal work notes vs public comments, cost/price fields, sensitive HR/security fields, security-classified CI attributes). [industry; ITIL 4 infosec]
- **Segregation of duties (SoD)** — configurable SoD rules: change requester ≠ approver for normal/high-risk changes; implementer ≠ sole approver; access-request requester ≠ approver; asset disposal approver ≠ executor; admins cannot approve their own role grants; SoD violations blocked or flagged with justification; reports for auditors. [ISO/IEC 27001 control on segregation of duties; ITIL 4 change authority]
- **Delegation** — time-bound approval delegation (out of office), delegated administration per provider unit/company (local admins manage their catalogue, groups, SLAs within global guardrails), "request on behalf of" (manager/assistant), team-lead visibility of members' items, temporary elevation ("break glass") with mandatory reason, expiry and post-review. [industry; ITIL 4 governance (delegated authority)]
- **Governance patterns → administration models** — v5 describes governance patterns (directive, guided, federated, autonomous; secondary source). Tool: choose centralized (global admins only), guided (templates + approvals for local changes), federated (local admins within guardrails) or autonomous (team-owned configuration with audit) per domain. [ITIL v5 (secondary)]
- **Multi-company, domain separation and multi-provider (SIAM)** — each record carries company/domain; MSP mode separates customer data, processes and configuration per domain with inheritance from a global domain; supplier users see only records assigned to their supplier group and the CIs referenced; customers see only their organization's records; shared services visible across domains by explicit publication. [ITIL 4 supplier management/SIAM; v5 MSP pattern (secondary)]
- **Identity lifecycle (joiner–mover–leaver)** — people records fed from HR (authoritative source) and the identity provider; movers trigger access re-evaluation; leavers trigger revocation tasks, asset recovery and account disabling with SLA (same-day target); orphan account detection. [ITIL v3 access management heritage; industry]
- **Access requests, entitlements and recertification** — entitlement catalogue (roles, groups, applications, data sets, privileged roles) with owners; request → owner approval → provisioning (automated via IdP/IGA or manual task) → time-bound expiry; periodic recertification campaigns where owners/managers confirm or revoke access; SoD checks at request time. [ITIL 4 SRM + information security]
- **Authentication** — SSO (SAML/OIDC), MFA, step-up authentication for sensitive actions (approvals of high-risk changes, viewing restricted cases), session policies, external-user authentication (customer/supplier portals), service accounts and API tokens with scopes and expiry. [ITIL 4 infosec "authentication"; industry]
- **Non-repudiation and access logging** — immutable audit of every write, approval (with authenticated identity and, optionally, e-signature) and every read of sensitive records; tamper-evident log storage; exports for investigations. [ITIL 4 infosec "non-repudiation"]
- **Data classification and privacy controls** — classification on records/fields drives masking, encryption, export restrictions, retention and AI eligibility; consent and data-subject requests for personal data. [ITIL 4 PESTLE legal; ISO/IEC 27001/27701]
- **AI and permissions** — AI features never widen access: retrieval and analytics are filtered by the requesting user's ACL; agents act with the user's identity or a narrowly scoped service identity; AI actions follow the decision-authority matrix (autonomous/approve/advise) and are logged as the acting identity plus the AI feature; restricted domains are excluded from AI context unless entitled. [ITIL v5 AI governance perspectives "decision authority and risk", "data governance"]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — access fulfilment within SLA = access requests fulfilled ≤ target/fulfilled; leaver revocation time = avg(access_revoked_at − termination_effective_at); recertification completion = items reviewed/items in campaign; revocation rate in recertification; orphan accounts count; SoD violations detected/blocked; privileged accounts with expiry %; sensitive-record access anomalies. [industry]

### Implementation notes
- Policy engine evaluates (subject attributes, action, resource attributes, context) → allow/deny with reasons; policies stored as data, versioned, testable ("what can user X see on record Y?" simulator), cached per session with invalidation on group/role change.
- Enforce row-level security in the database (e.g., Postgres RLS keyed on company/domain/provider unit) in addition to the application layer, so APIs, reports, exports and AI retrieval share one enforcement point.
- Sensitive domains use deny-by-default with explicit membership lists; their records are excluded from global indexes or indexed with ACL tokens filtered before ranking.
- Delegations, elevations and impersonation are first-class records with start/end, reason and approver; every action taken under them records both the real and the effective identity.

## Module: integration

### Features
- **ITIL basis** — the "information and technology" and "partners and suppliers" dimensions require information to flow across practices, tools and organizations. ITIL 4 CDS covers integration and data sharing (sections on integration topologies and integration approaches), integrated service management toolsets and toolset expectations, information models, reporting and advanced analytics, and collaboration and workflow; the incident PG requires "standard interfaces" with suppliers in multi-vendor environments (data-exchange rules, tools, processes); SCM notes configuration data may be held in several CMDBs and federated; v5 stresses ecosystems and supplier integration (catalogue PG metric "integration health": missing, ineffective or manual integrations). [ITIL 4 CDS (table of contents); Incident PG §6; SCM; ITIL v5]
- **Integration topologies supported** — point-to-point connectors for simple cases, hub-and-spoke via an integration hub/broker, and event bus/streaming for high-volume and multi-consumer flows (generic topologies; the exact CDS terminology was not verified). [ITIL 4 CDS (unverified wording); industry]
- **Open APIs for every record type** — REST (and optionally GraphQL) CRUD, query, attachments, bulk import/export, schema/metadata endpoints, the same ACL as the UI, pagination, filtering, rate limits, API versioning, OAuth 2.0 client credentials and user-delegated tokens. [industry]

[industry; ITIL 4 M&EM]
- **Integration hub / low-code connectors** — reusable actions for identity (AD/Entra ID, Okta, LDAP), HR (Workday, SAP SuccessFactors), ERP/finance (SAP, Oracle, Dynamics), procurement, endpoint/MDM (Intune, Jamf), discovery and cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP), monitoring/observability (Prometheus, Datadog, Dynatrace, Zabbix, Splunk, Elastic), SIEM/SOAR, CI/CD and VCS (GitHub, GitLab, Azure DevOps, Jenkins), chat (Teams, Slack), telephony/CTI, email, survey tools, LMS, EA tools; credential vault; connection health monitoring. The practice guides' tool categories (orchestration and integration platforms; remote administration and deployment; job scheduling; SIEM; procurement; contract management; CRM; LMS; talent/HR; financial management systems; EA management; discovery and inventory; barcode/QR; geolocation) indicate which systems each practice expects to exchange data with. [ITIL practice guides tool categories via ITIL v5 Compass; industry]
- **E-bonding / service integration (SIAM)** — bi-directional exchange of incidents, requests, problems and changes with suppliers and customers: field and state mapping, correlation ids (local and remote numbers), acknowledgement and retry, conflict rules (who owns which fields), attachment sync, SLA/UC pause and resume on hand-off, supplier ticket reference displayed on the record; standard interface definitions per supplier (data-exchange rules). [ITIL 4 Incident PG §6; SIAM]
- **CMDB federation and reconciliation** — federated sources (discovery, cloud inventories, ITAM, monitoring, EA tool) with identification and reconciliation rules (see cmdb) and per-attribute source precedence. [ITIL 4 SCM]
- **Master data ownership** — authoritative sources declared per object (people/org → HR/IdP; locations → facilities; CIs → discovery; services → service catalogue; suppliers/contracts → procurement/ERP; costs → finance); the tool flags edits that conflict with the authoritative source. [ITIL 4 information model concept; industry]
- **RPA and orchestration** — RPA bots for systems without APIs (fulfilment steps), orchestration flows calling external systems with error handling and compensation; all automated actions logged on records. [ITIL 4 CDS automation; SRM]
- **Collaboration-tool integration** — create/update records from chat, major-incident channels created automatically, approvals in chat, ChatOps commands, notifications to channels, transcripts captured back to the record. [ITIL 4 CDS collaboration and workflow; HVIT ChatOps]
- **Data exchange for analytics** — replication to a data warehouse/lake (CDC streams or scheduled extracts) with metric definitions, so enterprise BI can use ITSM data under the same row-level policies. [ITIL 4 CDS reporting and advanced analytics]
- **IT4IT and standard data models** — optional mapping of records to The Open Group IT4IT value streams (strategy-to-portfolio, requirement-to-deploy, request-to-fulfil, detect-to-correct) and data objects for interoperability. [IT4IT (complementary standard)]
- **AI/agent integration** — tools exposed to AI agents (search records, create incident, order catalogue item, run runbook) with per-tool scopes and the decision-authority matrix; MCP-style tool servers for external assistants (industry trend; not ITIL text). [ITIL v5 coordination capability; industry]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — integration error rate = failed messages/messages; mean sync latency; % e-bonded records in sync (state/field parity); % manual hand-offs replaced by integrations; time to onboard a new supplier interface; API availability. [industry; catalogue PG "integration health"]

### Implementation notes
- Transactional outbox for outbound events (write event in the same DB transaction as the record change, deliver asynchronously) to avoid lost or phantom events; idempotency keys on inbound calls.
- Mapping tables per integration (local field ↔ remote field, value maps for states/priorities/categories) versioned like configuration; loop prevention by tagging changes with their source integration.
- Each integration has its own service identity with least-privilege scopes, rate limits and audit; secrets in a vault, rotated.
- Integration monitor: per connection health, throughput, error queue with replay, alerting into the event module.

## Module: devops

### Features
- **Practice card: Software development and management** — Purpose (ITIL 4): "to ensure that applications meet internal and external stakeholder needs, in terms of functionality, reliability, maintainability, compliance, and auditability" (v5 summaries add operability). In ITIL 4 a technical management practice; in v5 a product & service practice. Processes (practice guide, via secondary): (a) *Managing the organization's approach*: analyse strategy, resources and portfolios → agree the development approach and product-type guidelines → communicate → review and adjust; (b) *Software development*: prioritize tasks → design → develop units → test units → integrate units → test the full system; (c) *Maintenance and improvement*: review the software system → plan improvements → initiate and control them. Terms: software quality (how well a product meets stated and implied needs under specified conditions), technical debt, Definition of Done (agreed functional and non-functional completion criteria). Approaches: Agile (Scrum, Kanban), DevOps (with CI/CD), Waterfall, SRE. Roles: product owner/manager, Scrum master/agile coach, delivery manager, solution architect, software developer, UX/UI designer, QA engineer. Metrics: satisfaction with the development approach and its rate of change; process adherence; improvement throughput and velocity; policy compliance; satisfaction with products and quality; release cadence and reliability; development cost and technical debt; resource utilization; software availability; security breaches and audit costs. [ITIL 4 Foundation; ITIL v5 Compass (secondary)]
- **High-velocity IT (ITIL 4 HVIT) as requirements** — five desired outcomes with techniques (per the HVIT companion book and course outlines): *valuable investments* (prioritization techniques, minimum viable products and services, product/service ownership, A/B testing); *fast development* (infrastructure as code, loosely coupled architecture, reviews, continual business analysis, CI/CD, continuous testing, Kanban); *resilient operations* (technical debt management, chaos engineering, definition of done, version control, AIOps, ChatOps, site reliability engineering); *co-created value* (service experience); *assured conformance* (DevOps Audit Defense Toolkit, DevSecOps, peer review). Five key behaviour patterns: accept ambiguity and uncertainty; trust and be trusted; continually raise the bar; help get customers' jobs done; commit to continual learning (confirmed against the HVIT reference guide description). [ITIL 4 HVIT]
- **Product backlog and work items** — epics, features, stories, bugs, technical-debt items, spikes; backlog ranking with WSJF; sprints/iterations and Kanban boards with WIP limits; acceptance criteria and Definition of Done checklists that include operability items (monitoring configured, runbook and knowledge article written, alerts routed, SLOs defined, support handover). [ITIL 4 SDM/HVIT]
- **Source-control and pipeline traceability** — link commits, branches, pull requests (with peer-review evidence), builds, test results, security scan results (SAST/DAST/SCA), SBOM, artifacts and deployments to stories, changes, releases, incidents and problems; "suspect commits" on incidents from recent deployments to affected CIs. [HVIT assured conformance; ITIL 4 change/release]
- **Automated change enablement for pipelines** — pipeline-created change records; pipeline-deployed changes classified as standard (pre-authorized) changes when policy gates pass (tests, security scan, peer review, deployment verification, post-deployment monitoring); automated risk scoring for normal changes with low-risk auto-approval; emergency fixes with automated rollback and mandatory post-deployment review; change freeze/error-budget gates. [ITIL 4 change enablement; ITIL v5 study site DevOps guidance (secondary)]
- **Policy as code** — machine-readable governance rules enforced in pipelines and provisioning (e.g., OPA, Sentinel, Kyverno, cloud policy services): change risk rules, secret scanning, encryption, network segmentation, data residency, drift detection against baselines; policy results stored as change/release evidence. [HVIT; ITIL v5 study site (secondary)]
- **SRE reliability management** — SLIs per service, SLOs stricter than SLAs, error budget = 100% − SLO, burn-rate alerts, error-budget policy (e.g., pause feature deployments when exhausted), toil tracking and reduction targets, production-readiness reviews. SLOs quantify Operate/Deliver quality; SLA remains the customer commitment (see slm). [HVIT resilient operations; ITIL v5 study site (secondary)]
- **Blameless post-incident reviews** — structured reviews after P1/P2 incidents covering multiple contributing factors (not a single root cause), searchable repository, remediation items prioritized by SLO impact and tracked in the backlog/problem record. [ITIL 4 incident/problem culture; HVIT; SRE]
- **Progressive delivery and feature management** — feature flags, canary, blue-green, ring deployments, automated rollback; release ≠ deployment (ITIL 4). [ITIL 4 release/deployment; HVIT]
- **Platform engineering / internal developer platform** — self-service provisioning via the request catalogue (golden paths), pipeline templates as pre-approved standard changes, environment management (infrastructure & platform practice), secrets/certificate automation (information security), cost visibility (service financial), compliance scanning (SVT/infosec). [ITIL v5 study site (secondary)]
- **Observability** — metrics, logs and traces (OpenTelemetry) feeding SLIs, incident diagnosis (traces), problem analysis (log correlation) and AIOps; v5 attaches the term "observability" to monitoring & event and infrastructure & platform management. [ITIL v5 (secondary)]
- **Chaos engineering and resilience tests** — experiment records (hypothesis, blast radius, abort conditions, results, follow-ups), linked to services and continuity tests. [HVIT]
- **Technical debt register** — debt items with cause (workaround chosen instead of a system solution — the ITIL incident PG definition), interest (incidents, toil hours, delay), principal (effort to fix), owner, priority; workarounds/known errors roll up into debt. [ITIL 4 Incident PG; SDM]
- **Value-stream (idea-to-production) analytics** — flow from demand → backlog → build → deploy → operate with flow metrics (flow time, flow efficiency, WIP, throughput) and DORA metrics. [ITIL 4 CDS; HVIT]
- **KPIs (formulas)** — DORA: deployment frequency (deployments to production per day/week), lead time for changes = avg(deployed_at − first_commit_at), change failure rate = deployments causing incident/rollback/hotfix ÷ deployments, time to restore service = avg(restored_at − impact_start); SLO attainment; error-budget remaining = 1 − (observed errors ÷ allowed errors); toil % of engineering time; escaped defects per release; PR review time; technical-debt ratio = debt effort ÷ total development effort; % changes auto-approved via policy. Mapping (secondary site): deployment frequency ↔ change enablement and deployment management; lead time ↔ Build and Transition; change failure rate ↔ change enablement and SVT; time to restore ↔ incident management and service continuity. [DORA; ITIL v5 study site (secondary)]
- **AI** — code generation and review assistance, test generation and test-failure triage, defect prediction, PR/change summarization, deployment-risk scoring, automated rollback decisions, suspect-commit identification for incidents, postmortem drafting (v5 Build/Transition AI examples). [ITIL v5] [AI]

### Implementation notes
- Ingest VCS/CI/CD events by webhook into `dev_event` (type, repo, ref, commit, pipeline run, environment, status, timestamps, actor) and link to work items by keys in commit messages/branch names; deployments create or update deployment records and close the loop on change records.
- DORA computation: deployments from deployment records; failures = deployments linked to incidents (caused-by), rollbacks or hotfix deployments within N hours; restore time from incident impact start/end; lead time from earliest linked commit.
- Error budget per SLO window: allowed_bad = (1 − SLO) × total_events; remaining = allowed_bad − bad_events; burn rate = (bad/total) ÷ (1 − SLO); multi-window burn alerts create events in the event module.
- Change policy evaluation is deterministic and logged (inputs, rule versions, outcome), so auditors can replay why a deployment was auto-approved (assured conformance).

### Implementation notes
- Work order is its own record type linked to the parent work item so multiple visits, crews and parts lines can exist per incident/request/change; parent SLA and on-site SLA tracked separately.

- Mobile offline sync with per-field conflict rules (server wins for assignment/schedule; device wins for captured work data), queued attachments, and audit of offline edits with device timestamps.
- Location data is personal data: collect only during working status, retain briefly, restrict visibility to dispatch roles.
